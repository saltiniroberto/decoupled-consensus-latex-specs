\title{Height Filtering and Healing for a Simplex-Style Finality Variant}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We compose a Goldfish-based available chain with a self-contained,
Simplex-inspired finality gadget at fixed validator weight, and we prove
that the composition heals after a period of asynchrony.  Finality
safety is accountable and uses no synchrony assumption.  For healing,
the protocol periodically disables justification at one height.  After
synchrony returns, the proof tracks the first honest official
confirmation above that height --- a veto-free available-confirmed
block --- shows that a bounded number of honest-proposer opportunities
forces it, that every honest fork choice then remains in its subtree,
and that finality restarts at a separately assumed aligned opportunity.
The healing argument assumes no common view of the stabilization
gadget: four per-round deadline views and a graded veto rule replace
that assumption.
\end{abstract}

\section{Protocol overview}\label{sec:overview}

This section names the three protocol parts and states how they interact.
Later sections define their state and prove the claims used by the recovery
theorem.

\begin{definition}[Protocol components]\label{def:protocol-components}
The \emph{available chain} is the Goldfish-based block-production and
fork-choice protocol.  The \emph{stabilization gadget}, abbreviated SG,
selects the root from which that fork choice walks and publishes a head for
the next round.  The \emph{finality gadget}, abbreviated FG, is the
self-contained height, justification, progress, and finalization state
machine defined in this paper.  It is a Simplex-style variant, not the
literal Simplex state machine.  A
 \emph{recovery round}, defined in Definition~\ref{def:recovery-timing}, has
 one stable-root-setting first-slot proposal and one common SG/FG signing
 action two network-delivery bounds into its second slot.  The second slot's
 ordinary proposal and raw vote occur before that action.  The remainder of
 that slot and any later slots continue ordinary Goldfish.
The \emph{round proposer} is the proposer of the stable-root-setting first
slot; proposer selection is receiver-local, and an \emph{honest round
proposer} names the event that one honest proposal wins that selection
at every honest validator, as Definition~\ref{def:recovery-timing}
states.  It is distinct from an ordinary \emph{slot proposer} in any
later Goldfish slot.  The global stabilization time, written \(\GST\), is the point after
which the network delay is at most \(\Delta\).

In plain words, the available chain chooses a live head, SG constrains that
choice, and FG gives accountable finality.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Relation to Simplex]\label{def:simplex-relation}
The comparison with Chan and Pass's Simplex protocol~\cite{simplex} is:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item an exact vote for this paper's current-height target plays the role of
 a Simplex vote to notarize one non-dummy block;
 \item this paper's height-progress tally plays the role of notarizing the
 Simplex dummy block and moving to the next iteration;
 \item this paper's finality pair plays the role of a Simplex
 \(\mathsf{finalize}(h)\) message.
\end{enumerate}
The protocol deltas are substantive: the target is the first block on the
chain at a height rather than a predetermined leader proposal; progress may
be proved by an empty target or another already-on-chain target; finality may
be piggybacked from a later confirmed descendant; weights replace process
counts; and periodic nonjustifiable heights disable one justification branch.
No theorem from Simplex is imported.  Accountable safety and
recovery for this variant are proved in this paper.

In plain words, Simplex supplies the notarize/dummy/finalize pattern, while
the state machine and proofs here stand on their own.
\end{definition}

This separation follows the accountability-gadget
model~\cite{accountability}.

During recovery, the round proposal contains a candidate block, a stable
root, ordinary validity and ancestry evidence, and Goldfish's ordinary
proposal view.  A graded root carries its grade-2 witness.  When no grade-2
root exists, the proposer carries evidence for its own Simplex root, and
a receiver accepts it only when it equals the receiver's own selection;
a receiver whose own newer finalized selection already lies ahead on the
proposal path starts farther forward on that same path.  Each receiver checks the stable root against its own grades
and independently applies normal Goldfish view merge.  The proposal
never carries or merges the SG head set.  Four
later-defined SG grades use one strict-majority threshold.  G3 supplies each
receiver's lower bound, G2 witnesses the proposer's candidate, and G1 is the
receiver's upper acceptance check for a candidate strictly beyond its
own selection.  G0 then prevents
a new SG head or current-height pair when strict-majority evidence may exist
on a conflicting branch; it does not suppress latest-justification finality.

The proof reads forward, with one flagged exception: the recovery half
opens with a six-concept interface, Definition~\ref{def:root-interface},
whose defining rules follow in later sections.
Sections~\ref{sec:model}--\ref{sec:safety} define the
finality state and prove accountable safety,
Theorem~\ref{thm:accountable-safety}.  Section~\ref{sec:store} defines
the evidence store and raw-evidence merge.  Section~\ref{sec:fork-choice-state}
defines the finality-derived fork-choice root and nonjustifiable heights.
Section~\ref{sec:grades} defines the four SG grades and the round's
deadline views.  Section~\ref{sec:available-confirmation} defines the
time-shifted-quorum available confirmation and proves that two available
confirmations cannot conflict and that a confirmed block captures every later
honest vote-time walk.  Section~\ref{sec:recovery-proposals} defines the
stable root and the recovery proposal rule.
Sections~\ref{sec:finality-votes}--\ref{sec:rounds} define validator voting
and the round schedule.
Section~\ref{sec:first-confirmation} proves that the standard
every-\(\rho\)-round event's honest proposers force the first
official confirmation at height \(H+1\):
Lemma~\ref{lem:honest-proposer-coverage} proves that honest-proposer
rounds are actual recovery opportunities, up to three bounded
obstruction classes --- one store-maximum rise, one redirection by the
unique old justification, and finitely many rounds at whose cutoffs a
finalized height is first processed.
Section~\ref{sec:healing} proves that the confirmation restores
stabilization, together with catch-up and the two height advances.
Finality restart then uses a separate clean target opportunity; the proof
explicitly requires the signing states and including branches to have
processed the restart justification as their latest unfinalized one.
Sections~\ref{sec:incentives} and~\ref{sec:refinement} are closing commentary:
they record the incentive requirement and the implementation requirements a
deployment must meet, and no result depends on either.
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} summarizes the construction.

\section{Model and quorum weights}\label{sec:model}

This section fixes the validator weights, block tree, height counter, messages,
and slashing rules.  The state machine and every later threshold argument use
these definitions.

\begin{definition}[Fixed validator weights]\label{def:validator-weights}
Let \(V\) be a fixed validator set and let \(w(i)\) be validator~\(i\)'s
fixed positive integer weight.  Write
\[
  W=\sum_{i\in V}w(i),
  \qquad
  q=\left\lceil\frac{2W}{3}\right\rceil,
  \qquad
  m=\left\lfloor\frac W2\right\rfloor+1.
\]
For \(S\subseteq V\), write \(w(S)=\sum_{i\in S}w(i)\).  A quorum is a set
\(Q\subseteq V\) with \(w(Q)\geq q\).
A strict majority is a set \(M\subseteq V\) with \(w(M)\geq m\), equivalently
\(2w(M)>W\).
A quorum check against~\(q\) may equivalently use
\(3w(Q)\geq2W\).  Every other threshold check uses its stated exact integer
inequality.

In plain words, finality uses \(q\), while every SG grade uses \(m\).
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[Fixed electorate and fault bound]\label{ass:fixed-electorate}
Every chain and round uses the fixed identities and weights~\(V,w\).
Finality checks use \(q\), and SG grades use \(m\).  The Byzantine weight \(b\)
satisfies the exact below-one-third bound
\[
  3b<W.
\]
Honest validators follow the durable signing rule defined below; Byzantine
validators may send arbitrary validly signed messages.

In plain words, accountable finality and recovery assume less than one third
faulty weight.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[SG fault bound]\label{ass:sg-fault-bound}
The four-grade and G0 lemmas that cite this assumption require only
\[
  2b<W.
\]
They do not require a finality quorum or full honest participation.  The full
healing theorem also uses Assumption~\ref{ass:fixed-electorate}, whose
below-one-third bound implies this one.

In plain words, the SG safety mechanism itself tolerates faulty weight below
one half; finality recovery needs the stronger finality bound.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Goldfish committees]\label{ass:goldfish-committees}
Each Goldfish slot has a committee, a known subset of \(V\), whose members
cast that slot's raw Goldfish votes, counted one unit per member.  How
committees are drawn is outside this paper's scope.  In every slot, honest
committee members strictly outnumber faulty ones, and every honest member
is awake and casts its vote at the slot's vote time.  Write \(\eta\) and
\(f'\) for the honest and faulty member counts of a slot's committee, so
\(\eta>f'\) in every slot.  The weighted
thresholds \(q\) and \(m\) apply to SG and FG messages over the fixed
electorate \(V\) and never count committee units; committee counts never
enter an SG or FG threshold.

In plain words, each slot's available-chain voters are given from outside
with an honest majority, while the finality thresholds keep their own
weighted electorate.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Cryptographic model]\label{ass:crypto}
Executions are parameterized by a security parameter \(\lambda\).
The hash function is collision resistant and the signature scheme is
existentially unforgeable against an adversary that runs in time
polynomial in \(\lambda\), controls the Byzantine validators and the
message schedule subject to the stated network assumptions, and acts
over an execution horizon polynomial in \(\lambda\) --- the finite
security horizon of the healing analysis.  A function is
\emph{negligible}, written \(\operatorname{negl}(\lambda)\), when
it is eventually smaller than every inverse polynomial.  Every
deterministic statement of this paper is conditioned on the complement
of two negligible-probability events: a collision or forgery within
the horizon, and --- for the healing analysis only --- the failure of
the proposer-recurrence event of
Assumption~\ref{ass:frontier-opportunities}.  The healing theorem's
\(1-\operatorname{negl}(\lambda)\) probability is taken over
exactly these events; no other statement in this paper is
probabilistic.

In plain words, hashes and signatures work and the assumed proposer
events occur, except with negligible probability over the polynomial
horizon.
\end{assumption}

\paragraph{Standing assumptions.}
The paper's hypothesis set is the cryptographic model just stated and
these seven protocol assumptions, and no others.  Three protocol
assumptions are global and stated above, alongside the cryptographic
model:
Assumption~\ref{ass:fixed-electorate} (fixed electorate and the
below-one-third finality fault bound),
Assumption~\ref{ass:sg-fault-bound} (the weaker SG fault bound
\(2b<W\)), and Assumption~\ref{ass:goldfish-committees} (per-slot
committees with honest majority).  Four are scoped to the analyzed
recovery window and stated with the recovery machinery:
Assumption~\ref{ass:recovery-network} (post-\(\GST\) delivery
strictly within one bound, with relay on receipt),
Assumption~\ref{ass:recovery-goldfish} (the retained ordinary-Goldfish
interface and the imported view-merge fact),
Assumption~\ref{ass:frontier-opportunities} (the relay point,
normalized frontier, proposer recurrence, and inclusion), and
Assumption~\ref{ass:clean-target-opportunity} (the finality restart's
clean target).  Each result names the assumptions it uses, directly or through the
named assumptions of the results it cites; the recovery-window
assumptions enter only the recovery development.

\begin{lemma}[The honest weight reaches every protocol threshold]
\label{lem:integer-thresholds}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:fixed-electorate},
\[
  W-b\geq q\geq m>b,\qquad q>b,\qquad 2q-W>b.
\]

In plain words, all honest validators can form either threshold, while the
Byzantine validators alone can form neither.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The integer inequality \(3b<W\) gives
\[
 b\leq\left\lfloor\frac{W-1}{3}\right\rfloor,
\]
so \(W-b\geq\lceil2W/3\rceil=q\).  The value \(m\) is the least integer
whose doubled value exceeds \(W\).  Since \(2q\geq4W/3>W\), we have
\(q\geq m\).  The inequality \(2b<W\) gives \(b<m\), and hence also
\(q>b\).  Finally,
\[
 2q-W\geq W/3>b.
\]
\end{proof}

The paper uses fixed weights.  Changing validator sets or balances requires
a separate weighted-overlap theorem; assigning a different electorate to
each height would not prove that overlap.

\begin{lemma}[Two quorums intersect in weight at least \(2q-W\)]\label{lem:quorum-intersection}
Any two quorums \(Q,Q'\subseteq V\) satisfy
\[
  w(Q\cap Q')\geq 2q-W.
\]
Consequently their intersection contains non-Byzantine weight under
Assumption~\ref{ass:fixed-electorate}.

In plain words, two finality quorums share enough weight to expose a fault if
they certify incompatible claims.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since \(w(Q\cup Q')\leq W\),
\[
 w(Q\cap Q')=w(Q)+w(Q')-w(Q\cup Q')
 \geq 2q-W.
\]
The adversary controls strictly less weight than this lower bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Height]\label{def:height}
Height is the finality-gadget counter and is separate from slot.  Genesis is
treated as justified and finalized at height \(0\), and the genesis state is
at current height \(1\).  Fix protocol constants \(K\geq2\) and
\(D_{\rm debt}\geq1\).
A justification certificate or progress certificate advances height by
exactly one.

In plain words, slots measure time while height counts finality-gadget
progress.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Blocks and chains]\label{def:block-chain}
Genesis has no parent.  Every other block has one parent, a slot
greater than its parent's slot, a signature by the proposer assigned to that
slot, a finite canonically ordered set of signed attestations, and a claimed
post-state root.
No field may name the block's own root.  For rooting, the canonical
encoding of a chain state omits, from its
latest block \(L\), both the claimed post-state-root field and the
proposer signature: the signature signs over the claimed root, so
keeping it would reintroduce the root through the signing domain.
Neither the root a block claims nor the signature that binds it
therefore enters the state that the claim names.  A proposer constructs
a block in three deterministic steps: assemble the unsigned,
root-free block, compute the post-state and its root from the
signature-free and root-free encoding, then fill the root field and
sign.  The signature authenticates the complete block on the wire; the
state root authenticates content; the two never nest.  A block root is known after its
post-state has been computed.  From then on we identify the block with its
root.  This identification, and every deterministic hash tie-break in this
paper, relies on the collision resistance of
Assumption~\ref{ass:crypto}: two distinct canonically encoded objects
have distinct roots, except with negligible probability.  Every
statement below is conditioned as that assumption states.
Blocks form a tree rooted at genesis.

Write \(B\preceq C\) when \(B=C\) or \(B\) is an ancestor of \(C\), and write
\(B\prec C\) when \(B\preceq C\) and \(B\neq C\).  Blocks are
\emph{compatible}, written \(B\sim C\), when \(B\preceq C\) or
\(C\preceq B\).  Otherwise they conflict.
Throughout this paper, ``descendant'' is reflexive and means
\(\preceq\); a proper or strict descendant is written with \(\prec\).

In plain words, ancestry is reflexive, strict ancestry excludes equality, and
two blocks conflict exactly when they lie on different branches.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[State-height]\label{def:state-height}
The state-height of a block \(B\) is the value of \(h\) in \(B\)'s post-state.

In plain words, state-height says which finality height the block's chain has
reached.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Current-height target]\label{def:current-height-target}
At height \(h\), \(s_h\) is the start slot chosen by the transition that
entered \(h\), and \(T_h\) is the current-height target.  The cases are:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item At genesis, \((s_1,T_1)=(0,\genesis)\).
 \item If a block \(B\) carries the transition into \(h>1\), then
 \(s_h=B.\slot\), \(T_h\) initially is empty, and the following slot
 processing records \(B\) as \(T_h\).
\end{enumerate}
These two cases are exhaustive: an empty slot never carries a height
transition, by Lemma~\ref{lem:empty-slot-noop} below.
Once nonempty, \(T_h\) remains fixed until the next height transition.  The
one-slot delay in case 2 is necessary because \(B\)'s root is known only
after \(B\)'s post-state has been computed.

A checkpoint-sync state in the middle of a height authenticates
\((h,s_h,T_h)\).  A checkpoint exactly at the transition block may carry
\(T_h=\bot\), but it authenticates that block so the same-slot rule in
Definition~\ref{def:ordinary-current-target} derives the same target until
the following slot records it.

In plain words, the target is the unique first block of the height on that
chain, recorded as soon as its root can be named.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Attestation]\label{def:fg-message}
An attestation is a signed object \(v\) with fields
\[
 (v.\validator,\ v.\mathsf{round},\ v.\headf,\ v.\height,\ v.\target,\
  v.\finalizeHeight,\ v.\finalizeTarget).
\]
The stabilization head is a block root or \(\bot\).
The height pair is a target vote
\((h,T)\) with \(T\neq\bot\), a timeout vote \((h,\bot)\), or the empty pair
\((\bot,\bot)\).  The finality pair is a pair \((h_f,T_f)\) with
\(T_f\neq\bot\), or the empty pair.

Signed messages have one canonical encoding and unforgeable identity,
except with probability negligible in \(\lambda\).
Two distinct canonical messages signed by one validator for one globally
unique round are round-message equivocation.  For SG support, the validator
has equivocated when two such messages have different head fields and at
least one is nonempty.  Two messages with the same head supply one SG head,
even if another field differs.  A nonempty stabilization head can support a
later grade.  The two finality-gadget pairs affect a chain only when the
signed attestation is included in a valid block on that chain.

In plain words, one signed attestation carries the SG head, the current-height
choice, and an independent finality commitment.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Valid attestation inclusion]
\label{def:valid-attestation-inclusion}
An attestation included in block \(B\) must be validly signed.  Every
attestation must be well formed under the canonical encoding, name a validator
in \(V\), and verify under that validator's key.  A root in an attestation is
not an additional block-validity condition.  The state transition reads only
the two finality-gadget pairs.  Its height pair changes state only when
Definition~\ref{def:vote-contribution} accepts it against \(B\)'s prestate and
parent chain; its finality pair changes state only when it names that
prestate's latest unfinalized justification.  The stabilization head is raw
SG evidence.  If its block or ancestry is unknown, the store keeps it pending
for later uncompleted SG actions.

In plain words, an unknown SG head cannot invalidate a block or change its
finality state, and each finality pair is classified against the chain that
actually includes it.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Target, progress, and finality contributions]
\label{def:vote-contribution}
Fix a block \(B\) and its prestate at current height \(h\) and target \(T_h\).
An attestation contributes to the target set exactly when its height pair is
\((h,T_h)\) with \(T_h\neq\bot\).  It contributes to the progress set exactly
when its height pair is \((h,\bot)\), or is \((h,T)\) with
\(T\preceq B.\parent\), the ancestry being evaluated against the
including block's parent chain.  It contributes to the finality set for \((h_f,T_f)\)
exactly when its finality pair equals \((h_f,T_f)\).
An exact target vote therefore contributes to both target and progress.
A different-height pair and every pair naming a block not on that parent chain
contribute to neither height set.  The parent bound prevents a message from
using the including block itself as progress evidence.

In plain words, exact equality justifies, while any signed current-height
position already on the including chain proves progress.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Slashing rules]\label{def:slashing}
Two signed attestations by one validator are slashable when either condition
holds:
\begin{description}
  \item[E1:] one finality pair is \((h,T)\) with \(T\neq\bot\), while the
  other height pair is \((h,T')\) with \(T'\neq T\), including
  \(T'=\bot\);
  \item[E2:] both height pairs have height \(h\), both targets are nonempty,
  and the targets differ.
\end{description}
The two conflicting fields may occur in the same signed attestation.
An empty height pair has height \(\bot\), so it conflicts with neither pair.

In plain words, a validator cannot finalize one target and later help a
different height outcome, or sign two targets at one height.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Durable signing history]\label{def:signing-history}
Validator \(i\) durably stores, for each height \(h\), its first nonempty
target \(\mathcal H_i.T(h)\), whether it signed a timeout
\(\mathcal H_i.\tau(h)\), and the target in its first finality pair
\(\mathcal H_i.\mathrm{lock}(h)\).  It writes each value before releasing the
signature and restores the values after restart.

In plain words, a validator remembers every choice needed to avoid E1 and E2.
\end{definition}

E2 forbids two target choices at one height.  E1 makes a timeout conflict
with an earlier finality pair for another target.

\section{Deterministic finality state machine}\label{sec:state-machine}

This section defines the chain state and its direct per-block and empty-slot
transition.  The accountable-safety proof uses only this transition, while
the voting and healing sections later supply its signed inputs.

\begin{definition}[Chain state]\label{def:chain-state}
A chain state is
\[
  \sigma=(L,s,h,s_h,T_h,\nj,\targetParticipation,\progressBit,
          J,h_j,F,h_F,P).
\]
Here \(L\) is the latest block and \(s\) is the slot cursor.  A block
post-state leaves \(s=L.\slot\).  Each \textsc{process\_slot} call closes the
slot at the cursor and then increments \(s\), so a slot-advanced local state
may have \(s>L.\slot\).  The value \(h\) is the current height,
\((s_h,T_h)\) the current height's start slot and target, and
\(\nj\in\{\mathsf{false},\mathsf{true}\}\) says whether that height is
nonjustifiable.  The flag is set when the chain enters~\(h\) and does not
change until the next height transition.  The pair \((F,h_F)\) is the
chain's greatest finalized root and height.

Initially,
\[
 h=1,\qquad
 (J,h_j)=(F,h_F)=(\genesis,0),\qquad
 (s_1,T_1)=(0,\genesis).
\]
The remaining initial value is \(\nj=\mathsf{false}\), and, for every
validator \(i\),
\[
 \targetParticipation[i]=\progressBit[i]=\mathsf{false},\qquad
 P=\varnothing.
\]
Write \(\sigma_{\rm gen}\) for this initial state.  A height advance is
consumed in an occupied slot containing block~\(B\) and sets
\(s_{h+1}=B.\slot\), with \(T_{h+1}=\bot\) initially; an empty slot
never consumes one, by Lemma~\ref{lem:empty-slot-noop} below.  The
\textsc{process\_slot} call that closes the occupied
slot records the first block at or after~\(s_{h+1}\) once its root is
available, so the transition block becomes the new target when its slot
is closed.

In plain words, the state records one deterministic height flag and the two
quorum tallies used to leave that height.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Nonjustifiable height]\label{def:nonjustifiable}
When a chain enters height \(h\), it sets
\[
 \nj \;=\; (K\mid h)\ \wedge\ (h-h_F>D_{\rm debt})
\]
from that transition's post-finality state.  The value remains fixed until
the next height transition.  The justification branch is disabled when
\(\nj=\mathsf{true}\), while the progress branch remains enabled.  Thus a
periodic height is nonjustifiable only during sustained finality debt;
healthy operation does not sacrifice every \(K\)-th height.

Every successor of a nonjustifiable height is an ordinary height: both exact
target and progress quorums are valid there, and target keeps priority when
both thresholds are present.

In plain words, a periodic height disables justification only after finality
has fallen sufficiently far behind; recovery installs no special height mode.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Participation state]\label{def:participation-state}
The bit \(\targetParticipation[i]\) records whether validator~\(i\) made a
current-height vote for the exact stored target~\(T_h\).  The bit
\(\progressBit[i]\) records whether it made a current-height message whose target
is empty or is an ancestor of the including block's parent --- the
parent-chain bound of Definition~\ref{def:vote-contribution}, which
prevents a block from proving its own progress.  Write
\[
  Q_{\rm target}(\sigma)=\{i:\targetParticipation[i]=\mathsf{true}\},
  \qquad
  Q_{\rm prog}(\sigma)=\{i:\progressBit[i]=\mathsf{true}\}.
\]
The pair \((J,h_j)\) is the chain's latest justified block and height.
The set \(P\) contains validators whose finalize pairs were accepted while
that pair was the latest unfinalized justification.  A newer justification
resets \(P\); the state keeps no finality tally for an older justification.

The state does not store a target root per validator.  Signed attestations
remain available as E1 and E2 evidence.  Participation state belongs to one
block state and is never combined across branches.

In plain words, two bits per validator determine height progress, while one
signer set records commitments to the latest justification.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Direct attestation processing]
\label{def:block-attestation-processing}
Let \(B\) be a block whose prestate has current height \(h\), target \(T_h\),
and latest justified pair \((J,h_j)\).  Each valid attestation \(a\) in \(B\)
is processed independently:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item if \(a\)'s height pair is the exact nonempty pair \((h,T_h)\), set
   \(\targetParticipation[a.\validator]\) to true;
 \item if \(a\)'s height pair is \((h,\bot)\), or is \((h,T)\) with
   \(T\preceq B.\parent\), set \(\progressBit[a.\validator]\) to true; and
 \item if \(h_j>h_F\), \(F\preceq J\), and \(a\)'s finality pair is
   \((h_j,J)\), add \(a.\validator\) to \(P\).
\end{enumerate}
The two height tests are independent, so an exact target vote sets both bits.
Every set is cumulative across blocks until its height transition or newer
justification resets it.  A finality pair for a justification created by
\(B\)'s direct height check is not counted in \(B\), because every
attestation is classified against the prestate.

In plain words, signed attestations update the three tallies directly, and
the including chain decides whether a nonempty height target proves progress.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Direct height-event position]
\label{def:direct-height-position}
The consensus state has no finality-round cursor, scheduled outcome, or
pending-outcome flag.  After a valid block's attestations have been folded
into its parent state, the block transition immediately checks the height
events once.  For a slot with no block on the chain,
\textsc{process\_slot} checks the same events once before advancing the slot
cursor.  When the cursor still names an occupied slot, its block post-state
already contains that check, so \textsc{process\_slot} does not repeat it.

A public round or recovery schedule may determine when validators sign and
when an action takes a local view.  That schedule is outside consensus state
and cannot delay, reorder, or add a height-event invocation.  Relative to the
ordinary direct-attestation machine, the changed consensus surface is
exactly two disclosed items: the progress contribution in
Definition~\ref{def:vote-contribution}, which accepts an empty target or a
nonempty target ancestral to the including parent, and the periodic
nonjustifiable-height guard of Definition~\ref{def:nonjustifiable}, which
sets the flag \(\nj\) in the chain state and disables the
justification branch at flagged heights.

In plain words, every included attestation reaches the next direct check,
with no scheduled consensus outcome between inclusion and processing.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Height-event processing]\label{def:height-outcome}
Every direct height check is processed in this order:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item if \(h_j>h_F\), \(F\preceq J\), and \(w(P)\geq q\), set
   \((F,h_F)\gets(J,h_j)\);
 \item if \(\nj=\mathsf{false}\), \(T_h\neq\bot\), and
   \(w(Q_{\rm target})\geq q\), set
   \((J,h_j)\gets(T_h,h)\), reset \(P\), and advance to \(h+1\);
 \item otherwise, if \(w(Q_{\rm prog})\geq q\), advance to \(h+1\)
   without changing the latest justification.
\end{enumerate}
An advance resets both participation arrays, starts with an empty target, and
sets the new height's nonjustifiable flag by
Definition~\ref{def:nonjustifiable}.  Its start slot is the advancing block's
inclusion slot; an empty slot never advances
(Lemma~\ref{lem:empty-slot-noop}).
Justification therefore wins whenever both height thresholds are present.
An empty height pair sets neither bit.

In plain words, each direct check first finalizes the old latest
justification, then prefers the exact current target, and otherwise advances
by progress.
\end{definition}

Figures~\ref{alg:state-replay} and~\ref{alg:attestation-processing} give the
complete deterministic transition.  A block is evaluated from its parent's
authenticated post-state.  Slot replay first advances past the occupied
parent slot without repeating its height check and then processes every
genuine intervening empty slot exactly once.  It stops at the new block's
slot, whose attestations and height events are processed once.

\begin{definition}[Valid block]\label{def:valid-block}
A non-genesis block is valid exactly when its parent is valid and known, its
slot and proposer signature satisfy Definition~\ref{def:block-chain}, every
included attestation satisfies
Definition~\ref{def:valid-attestation-inclusion}, and replaying
Figures~\ref{alg:state-replay} and~\ref{alg:attestation-processing} from the
parent's post-state returns a state, rather than \(\mathsf{invalid}\), whose
root is its claimed post-state root.  Two different valid blocks signed by
the assigned proposer for one slot are proposer equivocation; structural
validity does not choose between them.

In plain words, validity checks the proposer, parent, individual signed
attestations, and the deterministic state transition.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Finality action state]\label{def:finality-action-state}
Fix a scheduled signing action \(a\) and let \(\slot(a)\) be its slot.
For \emph{any} accepted block \(X\) with \(X.\slot\leq\slot(a)\),
define the closed state
\[
  \sigma_a[X]
  =
  \textsc{process\_slots}\bigl(\sigma[X],\slot(a)\bigr),
\]
where emptiness is resolved on \(X\)'s own chain: the chain through
\(X\) has no block after \(X\), so every slot in
\((X.\slot,\slot(a))\) is empty on it and the closure is total.  A slot
\(s<\slot(a)\) is \emph{resolved empty} on a chain when that chain
contains no block at slot \(s\); resolution is a function of the chain
and the action slot alone, so two validators fixing the same chain and
slot resolve identically, while a conflicting chain may occupy the slot,
with different derived states, as it must.

The action's own signing state is \(\sigma_a[B]\), where \(B\)
is the latest block of the action's selected chain processed before the
action.  No separate runnability guard is needed: the chain is fixed by
its tip, \(B\) is that tip, and every slot strictly between
\(B.\slot\) and \(\slot(a)\) is resolved empty on it by construction,
so the closure is total here exactly as for any other block.  The
closure \(\sigma_a[X]\) of any
other accepted block --- the recovery sections apply it to their
context blocks --- likewise closes \(X\)'s own chain.
Thus the first call to \textsc{process\_slot}, when one is needed, closes the
occupied cursor position \(B.\slot\): it may materialize the current target,
but it does not repeat the height-event check already stored in \(B\)'s
post-state.  Later calls process each genuine intervening empty slot once.
The loop stops with cursor \(\slot(a)\): it neither reruns
\textsc{process\_block} for \(B\) nor processes the current action slot.  If
\(B.\slot=\slot(a)\), the loop makes no call.

For a recovery action, \(\slot(a)\) is the round's second slot.  If a block in
that slot has already been processed, then \(B.\slot=\slot(a)\) and the loop
makes no call.  Otherwise the loop closes the latest occupied slot and every
resolved empty slot before the second slot, but it does not declare the second
slot empty.

In plain words, a signer derives its height and finality fields from one
authenticated chain after closing exactly the slots before its action:
the signing rules of the recovery sections read their height, cursor, and
target fields from this closed state, and the safety lemmas quantify over
it.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[An empty slot never changes height, finality, or participation]
\label{lem:empty-slot-noop}
For every block \(X\) and action slot, \(\sigma_a[X]\) differs from
\(\sigma[X]\) at most in the slot cursor, advanced to the action slot,
and in a materialized target name; every height, nonjustifiable-flag,
start-slot, justification, finality, and participation field is
unchanged --- in particular
\(\sigma_a[X].h=\sigma[X].h\) --- and every height transition is consumed
at a block.

In plain words, closing empty slots can name a pending target but can
never advance the chain.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The participation tallies are written only by
\textsc{process\_attestation}, which is called only by
\textsc{process\_block}, and \textsc{state\_transition} runs
\textsc{process\_height\_events} directly after each block with exactly
those tallies.  A later empty slot adds no attestation:
\textsc{process\_slot} materializes at most the pending target name and
sets no participation bit --- an attestation contributes a target bit
only when its pair names the nonempty \(T_h\) current at its inclusion,
so a target materialized after every inclusion has tally zero.  The
height-event check's target and progress branches are deterministic
functions of the tallies and the disabled-branch flags, so the empty
slot's check reproduces the outcome
of the preceding block's check, which did not fire; and directly after an
advance both arrays are reset, so the following empty slots have empty
tallies.  The finality branch reads \(P\), \((J,h_j)\), and
\((F,h_F)\): if it fired at the preceding block, \(h_F=h_j\) afterwards
and its guard \(h_j>h_F\) fails in every later empty slot; if it did
not fire, a justification advance resets \(P\), while a progress
advance and a no-advance both leave \(P\), \(J\), \(h_j\), \(F\), and
\(h_F\) unchanged, so the empty slot evaluates the same guard on the
same inputs and does not fire either.  Hence no empty-slot check ever fires, every height transition
is consumed at a block, and the closure of
Definition~\ref{def:finality-action-state} changes neither the height
nor the finality fields.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The finalized block precedes the justified and latest blocks]
\label{lem:finalized-before-justified}
Every reachable block post-state and finality action state satisfies
\[
  F\preceq J\preceq L
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  h_F\leq h_j<h.
\]

In plain words, finality never moves off the current chain or ahead of its
latest justification.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The claim holds at genesis.  Direct attestation processing changes only
participation fields.  A valid block replaces \(L\) by its child.
The block's immediate height-event check, and the corresponding check in
each empty slot, applies the following cases in the same order.
The finality branch copies the previous \((J,h_j)\) into \((F,h_F)\).
The justification branch sets \(J=T_h\), which lies on the current chain and
already contains the previous \(J\), and then increments \(h\).  The progress
branch increments only \(h\).  These cases preserve both ancestry and height
inequalities.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Derived certificates]\label{def:certificates}
A \emph{justification certificate} \(\mathsf{JC}(h,T)\) consists of distinct
signed exact-target attestations of weight at least \(q\), their valid
inclusions on one chain, and the direct height-event invocation that set
\((J,h_j)=(T,h)\).  A target quorum at a nonjustifiable height is not a JC.
A \emph{progress certificate} \(\mathsf{PC}(h)\) consists of distinct signed
height-\(h\) attestations of weight at least \(q\), their valid inclusions
setting the progress bits on one chain, and the direct height-event invocation
that advanced height without a new justification.  A \emph{finality certificate}
\(\mathsf{FC}(h,T)\) consists of \(\mathsf{JC}(h,T)\), distinct finality
commitments to \((h,T)\) of weight at least \(q\) accepted while it was the
latest unfinalized justification, and the direct invocation that finalized it.

A transport object may bundle any such attestations and proofs.  Decoding the
bundle yields the individual attestations; its label creates no state and
does not replace their ordinary inclusion checks.  Receiving a certificate
does not change another branch.

In plain words, a certificate proves what cumulative included attestations
already made the state machine do.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[One height cannot justify two targets]\label{lem:target-uniqueness}
Two conflicting targets cannot both have justification certificates at one
height unless validators of total weight at least~\(2q-W\) violate E2.

In plain words, one finality height cannot justify two forks without exposing
one quorum intersection.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The signer sets of the two certificates intersect in weight at least
\(2q-W\) by Lemma~\ref{lem:quorum-intersection}.  Every signer in the
intersection signed both distinct targets at height \(h\), which is E2.
\end{proof}

\section{Accountable safety}\label{sec:safety}

This section proves that finalized blocks form one chain without assuming
synchrony or any property of SG or the available chain.  The fork-choice and
healing sections later use this result to discard finalized-conflicting data.

\begin{lemma}[A certificate advances the height by one]\label{lem:height-progression}
Every height transition increments the height by exactly one and requires a
justification certificate or progress certificate counted using the fixed
validator weights.

In plain words, a chain cannot skip a finality height or advance it without a
quorum.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The justification and progress rules are the only rules that advance height.  Both
increment it by one, and both use the weights required by
Assumption~\ref{ass:fixed-electorate}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A target bit names the current-height target]\label{lem:height-target-freshness}
If a target \(T\) is counted in \(Q_{\rm target}\) at height \(h\) on a chain
ending at \(B\), then \(T=T_h\prec B\), and the chain's post-state at \(T\)
has current height \(h\).

In plain words, a target bit always refers to the first block of the current
height and is processed only by a later block.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The target-participation predicate requires \(T\) to equal the stored
current-height target.  At height \(1\), the target is initialized directly
to genesis, whose post-state has current height \(1\); every non-genesis
including block is its strict descendant.  At each later height, slot processing records the
first block at or after the height start slot and replaces it only at the next
height transition.  Strictness is structural rather than a separate check:
while the field is still empty no bit can be set; the field is filled only by
the \textsc{process\_slot} call that advances past the target block's slot, so every block whose
prestate has \(T_h\neq\bot\) is already a strict descendant of~\(T_h\); and a
block cannot include a vote naming itself by
Definition~\ref{def:block-chain}.  The preceding height's direct transition was consumed at a block
(Lemma~\ref{lem:empty-slot-noop}); if that block is \(T\) itself, its
post-state is already
at height~\(h\) and its slot is the new start slot.  No height transition
occurs between that post-state and installing~\(T\) as~\(T_h\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A chain has one current-height target]\label{lem:chain-target-uniqueness}
Each reached height has at most one current-height target on a given chain;
once nonempty, it is the chain's unique first block at that height.  If a
target vote for \(T\) sets the target bit on one height-\(h\) branch, then
every height-\(h\) branch that has processed beyond \(T\) and contains \(T\)
has the same current-height target and gives the vote both bits.  Distinct
current-height targets at one height lie on conflicting branches; targets
of different heights on one chain are compatible and are not constrained
by this claim.

In plain words, exact target votes transfer to every extension that contains
their target; the broader progress rule does not change the stored target.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Height never decreases and each transition increments it by one.  A chain
therefore has at most one first block at or after the start of height~\(h\), which
Definition~\ref{def:current-height-target} stores after its root becomes available and until
the next transition.  The authenticated post-state of the transition block
fixes the same start slot on every extension containing it.  If another
height-\(h\) branch has processed beyond target block~\(T\), it inherits that
  state and has already recorded~\(T\).  The target and progress predicates in
  Figure~\ref{alg:attestation-processing} then both count the vote.  Two distinct first
blocks at one height cannot be comparable, so distinct current-height targets
conflict.
\end{proof}

For justification, exact equality prevents a target from one branch from
justifying another.  For progress, ancestry is deliberately broader: a vote
for any target already on the including chain proves that its signer reached
the height and cannot redirect the chain to that target.

\begin{lemma}[Two bits per validator preserve both tallies]\label{lem:target-bit-compression}
For a given chain state, all information needed for the justification and
progress rules is contained in the two Boolean arrays
\[
  \targetParticipation
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \progressBit.
\]
Retaining the signed messages is sufficient to prove E1 and E2 violations.

In plain words, the chain state needs two bits per validator even though a
progress contribution may name any earlier block on the chain.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By the target-participation guard in
Figure~\ref{alg:attestation-processing} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:chain-target-uniqueness}, every vote counted toward
justification names~\(T_h\).  Its signer set is therefore
\(\{i:\targetParticipation[i]=\mathsf{true}\}\).
The progress guard writes only a Boolean after checking that the vote has the
current height and has either an empty target or a target on the including
block's parent chain.  The height transition later reads only
\(\{i:\progressBit[i]=\mathsf{true}\}\), with each validator counted once.  These
are the only two signer sets used by
\(\mathsf{process\_height\_events}\).
E1 and E2 are checked
from pairs of retained signed messages, so they do not require target roots
in the participation state.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A chain past a finalized height contains its finalized block]\label{lem:past-finalized}
Let \(C\) be finalized at height \(h\), and let \(\sigma\) be either a block
post-state on a chain ending at \(B\) or a finality action state derived from
that chain.  If \(\sigma.h>h\), then \(C\preceq B\).  For \(h\geq1\), the
only exception requires E1-slashable validators of total weight at least
\(2q-W\).

In plain words, advancing past a finalized height must either include its
finalized block or expose the same quorum-intersection fault.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(h=0\), then \(C\) is genesis and the result is immediate.  Assume
\(h\geq1\).
Follow the deterministic state derivation along the chain ending at \(B\).
For a finality action state, Definition~\ref{def:finality-action-state}
additionally advances past the latest occupied cursor position and processes
every genuine intervening empty slot, without processing the action slot.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:height-progression}, this derivation has a first call to
\textsc{advance\_height} that moves from \(h\) to \(h+1\).  That call uses
either a target quorum \(Q_J\) or a progress quorum \(Q_P\).  Every
participation bit in the selected quorum was set by an attestation included in
some block \(D_i\preceq B\); an empty-slot transition includes no new
attestation.

Let \(Q_F\) be the finality quorum for \(C\), and let \(Q\) be the selected
height-advance quorum.  Lemma~\ref{lem:quorum-intersection} gives
\(w(Q_F\cap Q)\geq2q-W\).  Fix a signer \(i\) in this intersection that is
not E1-slashable.  Its height-\(h\) message cannot have an empty target or a
target different from \(C\), because either choice conflicts by E1 with its
finality commitment to \((h,C)\).  Its message therefore targets \(C\).

There are two cases.  If the target branch advanced the height, the exact
target guard makes \(C\) the current-height target on the including chain, so
Definition~\ref{def:current-height-target} gives \(C\preceq D_i\).  If the
progress branch advanced the height, direct attestation processing counts a
nonempty target only when
\(C\preceq D_i\).  In both cases \(C\preceq D_i\preceq B\).
Consequently, if \(C\npreceq B\), every signer in \(Q_F\cap Q\) is
E1-slashable, giving the stated weight.

This use of E1 includes the explicit-timeout case \(a.\target=\bot\).
E2 alone would not cover a finality commitment to \(C\) followed by one
height message for another target, because the commitment need not itself
contain a second height target.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finalized blocks form a chain]\label{lem:finalized-chain}
For any finalized pairs \((C,h)\) and \((C',h')\), if \(h\leq h'\) then
\(C\preceq C'\), unless the fault bound in
Assumption~\ref{ass:fixed-electorate} is violated.

In plain words, an honest observer can order every finalized block by
ancestry.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(h=0\), then \(C\) is genesis and the result is immediate.  Assume
\(h\geq1\).
If \(h=h'\), intersect a finality certificate for \(C\) with the justification
certificate for \(C'\).  E1 forces every non-slashable intersection signer to
have \(C=C'\).

If \(h<h'\), let \(\sigma'\) be the block post-state or finality action state
in which \(C'\) finalized, and let \(B'\) be the latest block of that chain.
The state has already advanced beyond \(h'\), and hence beyond \(h\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:past-finalized} gives \(C\preceq B'\).  The certificate
validation for \(C'\) gives \(C'\preceq B'\), so both blocks lie on this
chain.
Lemma~\ref{lem:height-target-freshness} gives state-height \(h\) at \(C\)
and state-height \(h'\) at \(C'\).  Because state-height is nondecreasing along a
chain and \(h<h'\), we have \(C\prec C'\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conflicting finalization exposes slashable weight]\label{thm:accountable-safety}
Conflicting blocks cannot both finalize unless, for some height \(h\), at least
\(2q-W\) weight is provably E1- or E2-slashable.

In plain words, conflicting finalization always produces enough signed
evidence to cross the accountable fault threshold.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If the two conflicting finalizations have the same height, intersect either
finality quorum with the other target's justification quorum.
Lemma~\ref{lem:quorum-intersection} gives intersection weight at least
\(2q-W\), and Definition~\ref{def:slashing} makes every intersection signer
E1-slashable unless the targets are equal.  Equal targets cannot conflict.

If the finalizations have different heights, let \((C,h)\) be the earlier
one.  For \(h=0\) the earlier pair is \((\genesis,0)\), finalized by
stipulation and an ancestor of every valid chain, so no finalized pair
conflicts with it and this branch arises only for \(h\geq1\), where
\(C\)'s finality quorum exists.  The later finalization's chain advanced beyond \(h\).  The proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:past-finalized} intersects \(C\)'s finality quorum with the
quorum that first advanced that chain past \(h\), again obtaining weight at
least \(2q-W\).  Every intersection signer is E1-slashable unless the
advancing chain contains \(C\).  If it contains \(C\), the later finalized
target is compatible with \(C\), contrary to the theorem premise.  Thus
conflicting finalizations expose the stated slashable weight in both cases.
\end{proof}

The proof does not require synchrony.  It is independent of the SG grades,
the available chain, graded agreement, confirmation, and nonjustifiable
heights.

\section{Fork-choice and evidence store}\label{sec:store}

This section defines an append-only logical raw-evidence store for one actor.
Consensus block states and fork-choice values are deterministic derived data,
never fields merged between sibling branches.

\begin{definition}[Store]\label{def:stored-data}
A logical store replica held by validator \(u\) is
\[
 S=(\mathsf{blocks},\mathsf{attestations},\mathsf{objects},
    \mathsf{receipts},\mathsf{completed\_actions},\mathsf{time}).
\]
Every replica of \(S\) runs inside \(u\)'s own node and records \(u\)'s local
receipts and completed actions.
The block and attestation components retain signed blocks and individual
signed attestations, including unknown-parent or unknown-reference objects
and finalized-conflicting evidence.  The \(\mathsf{objects}\)
component retains every other individually signed protocol object the
protocol consumes --- raw Goldfish votes, signed round proposals, SG
heads, and relayed certificates --- under the same receipt discipline.  A step-independent fact
\(\mathsf{received}(x,t)\) records that raw object \(x\) was received at
local time \(t\).  Define its first receipt time by
\[
 r_S(x)=\min\{t:\mathsf{received}(x,t)\in S.\mathsf{receipts}\},
\]
with \(r_S(x)=\infty\) if the set is empty.  A completed-action record
\((u,I,M,\Pi,y)\) contains the actor, scheduled action, exact deadline input,
predecessor records \(\Pi\), and fixed result \(y\).

The blocks, attestations, objects, receipt facts, and completed-action
records are sets.  Every completed-action record in \(S\) has first component \(u\), and
every receipt records a delivery to \(u\).  A completed action is never
recomputed when later evidence arrives.
Per-block post-states, pending-reference indices, fork-choice state, grade
views, and pending action results are derived and may be cached, but are
not independent merge inputs.

Every scheduled action \(I\) has a public finite predecessor-action list and
a deadline \(d_I\).  It is pending before \(d_I\).  At \(d_I\), the actor
first records every delivery at that time and then creates one immutable
action record.  Its input is
\[
 M_I(S)=
 \{x:r_S(x)\leq d_I\ \text{and}\ r_S(z)\leq d_I
       \text{ for every dependency }z\text{ needed to interpret }x\}.
\]
The dependency condition includes the full known block ancestry.  The
\(S\) in this formula is the exact store held at the deadline, and
the record stores \(M_I(S)\) explicitly; a later merge
does not reinterpret that past view.  A required predecessor that is absent
or non-unique makes the deterministic result \(\mathsf{abstain}\).  After the
deadline, a missing record means that actor missed the action; it is not
synthesized later.  No ``open action'' event and no fact for a future action
is needed.  At every finite time, receipt facts exist only for objects
actually received and action records only for deadlines already reached.
An honest actor runs one signing process: for each scheduled action,
exactly one replica --- the actor's active one --- computes the record
and releases any signature in its result, while a passive replica stores
and merges data but never completes a signing action.  Divergent
completed histories for one action therefore arise only from recovery of
the actor's own storage, never from two live signers, and one honest
identity never releases two signed results for one action.  The merge
tolerance below is a data-availability property, not concurrent signing.

The logical store retains every raw object.  If a protocol rule gives an
object \(x\) expiry \(d(x)\), that expiry applies only to rules that consume
live evidence; evidence that remains usable for accountability or another
future operation has not expired for that use.  Its live status in \(S\) is
the derived predicate
\[
  \mathsf{live}_S(x)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d(x)>S.\mathsf{time}.
\]
A receipt remains evidence of an earlier delivery after \(x\) is no longer
live.  An object still needed by a pending action is not eligible for physical
deletion.

In plain words, each object gets one ordinary receipt history, and each action
freezes one deadline view exactly once.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Store merge]\label{def:store-join}
Stores \(S_1,S_2\) are compatible only when they use the same genesis and
protocol rules.  For compatible stores, \(S_1\sqcup S_2\) takes the union of
blocks, attestations, other signed objects, receipt facts, and
completed-action records --- each of the five set components of
Definition~\ref{def:stored-data} --- and takes
the greater clock value.  Merging serves lock-free parallel processing of
different store operations within one node; the same operation also merges
replicas of one validator's store.  For an action not yet completed,
the deadline input is derived from the merged receipts by \(M_I(S)\).
Thus a dependency received before \(I\) was scheduled, or before the object
that refers to it, is admitted whenever both receipt times are at most
\(d_I\) --- Corollary~\ref{cor:merged-action-inputs} below makes these
pending-action inputs independent of merge order.

A merge never rewrites a completed action.  If the copies contain different
completed histories for one action of \(u\), both remain.  A later operation that
requires one predecessor result uses it only when it has a unique
history-compatible predecessor; otherwise that operation records
\(\mathsf{abstain}\) at its deadline.  A history-compatible predecessor tuple
contains exactly one required record for each predecessor action and follows
the exact predecessor names recursively recorded in those records.  Merge
does not manufacture a common past action,
combine local facts from different actors, or guarantee a continuation from
two divergent completed executions.  Signed equivocations remain evidence.

The merged clock is \(t=\max(t_1,t_2)\).  An object with expiry \(d\) is live
after the merge exactly when \(d>t\), equivalently when it is unexpired at
both input clocks.  The logical union retains expired raw evidence but never
makes it live again.  No signed object is ever physically deleted;
Remark~\ref{rem:no-deletion} below records why.
An implementation may
physically delete an expired \emph{unsigned} object once no pending
action can use it; completed records already fix
the results of past steps.  A later physical merge need not fetch that object
from an older replica, because the maximum clock still classifies it as
expired.  This physical garbage collection does not change the logical store
operations or any future input.

In plain words, replicas of one actor can join their evidence, while that
actor's past actions remain the actions that actually occurred.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Why signed objects are never deleted]
\label{rem:no-deletion}
A signed finality-gadget attestation's height and finality fields remain
potential E1 and E2 evidence for every future conflicting message, and
any other signed object --- a raw Goldfish vote, an SG head, a round
proposal --- is potential equivocation evidence against a copy its
signer may reveal later, so deleting the first copy would destroy a
provable fault.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Store merge is associative, commutative, and idempotent]
\label{thm:store-merge}
On compatible replicas of one actor, \(\sqcup\) is associative, commutative,
and idempotent, and both the merged logical evidence and the receipt cutoff
\(M_I(S)\) of every still-pending action are therefore independent of merge
order.  The operation does not require the store
from which its inputs were copied, and it does not identify distinct
completed histories.

In plain words, merge order among one actor's replicas cannot change evidence
available to an action before its deadline.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Compatibility fixes the same genesis and protocol rules in every
input.  The five evidence and history components --- blocks,
attestations, objects, receipts, and completed-action records --- use
set union, and the clock uses maximum.  Both operations are associative, commutative, and idempotent.
The first-receipt value \(r_S(x)\) is the minimum timestamp in the resulting
union, so \(M_I(S)\) is also independent of merge order.  The live predicate
is a deterministic filter at the maximum clock.  A pending action with unique
compatible predecessors is a deterministic function of that cutoff and
those predecessors.  Completed records are retained rather than recomputed.
The theorem makes no non-abstention claim for a successor of divergent
completed histories or any claim for a merge between different actors.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Pending actions have merge-order-independent inputs]
\label{cor:merged-action-inputs}
If compatible replicas belong to actor \(u\), no input store has completed
\(u\)'s action \(I\), every input clock is at most \(d_I\), and the merged
store has one history-compatible result for every predecessor of \(I\), every
finite merge order gives \(I\) the same deadline input \(M_I\) and maximum
clock.

In plain words, all by-deadline objects and already-known dependencies can be
combined before an action without making it depend on merge order.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is the set-union and maximum part of
Theorem~\ref{thm:store-merge}.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Total raw-block replay]\label{def:total-raw-replay}
For a stored signed object whose parent has an accepted state, the
\emph{structural precheck} first verifies canonical decoding, the proposer
signature and slot assignment, a slot strictly greater than the parent's,
the canonical finite attestation list, and every stateless attestation check
in Definition~\ref{def:valid-attestation-inclusion}.  A failed precheck
classifies that object as invalid for state derivation without removing it
from the raw store.

In addition, a block is \emph{slot-eligible} in a view exactly when its
slot's scheduled start is at or before that view's time --- the store
clock \(S.\mathsf{time}\) for the main store, the cutoff for a
deadline sub-view.  A block that is not slot-eligible is not given to
the transition and remains queued raw, exactly as an unknown-parent
object does; it becomes eligible when the clock reaches its slot.  This
is the in-limbo rule of the Goldfish source, which keeps future-slot
messages outside the protocol view until their slot begins.  Eligibility
is monotone: a merge takes the maximum of the two clocks, so a block
eligible in a view is eligible in every later view.  Every derived
state, including the fork-choice state of
Section~\ref{sec:fork-choice-state}, therefore ranges only over
accepted blocks whose slots have begun by the deriving view's time.

Only an object that passes this precheck is given to
\textsc{state\_transition}.  The transition is evaluated as a total
operation returning either a state or \(\mathsf{invalid}\); a failed
precondition or transition check returns \(\mathsf{invalid}\) to the caller.
It never aborts the derivation loop.  An object with an unknown parent is not
evaluated and remains queued until that parent is accepted, and an
attestation or SG head with unknown referenced blocks likewise remains raw
evidence for later actions.

In plain words, malformed objects are skipped, while incomplete objects wait
for their dependencies and cannot stop replay of valid siblings.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Stored evidence grows and derived block states do not change]
\label{lem:store-monotonicity}
If a logical store \(S\) is extended to \(S'\) by receipt or merge
operations, every logical raw object and completed record in \(S\) remains in
\(S'\).  Every block state derived in both stores is identical, and the
greatest accepted state-height cannot decrease.

In plain words, later evidence can connect more blocks but cannot rewrite an
accepted block's state.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Logical raw components grow by insertion or set union.  Optional physical
deletion under Definition~\ref{def:store-join} changes only the representation
of evidence that no future operation can use.  A block state is the
deterministic replay of its parent state and immutable individual
attestations.  Each derivation pass either accepts such a block or skips its
candidate and continues; no failure exits the loop.  At a finite store the
fixed point is the least parent-closed set of slot-eligible blocks that
pass the precheck, transition, and state-root check, independent of
candidate order.  Slot eligibility is monotone in the store clock
(Definition~\ref{def:total-raw-replay}), and a merge takes the maximum
of the two clocks, so extension by receipt or merge only enlarges the
candidate set.  The
accepted block set can therefore only gain connected valid blocks, so its
maximum state-height cannot decrease.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A local finalized state never moves backward or sideways]
\label{lem:local-finality-irreversible}
Along one valid branch, \(h_F\) never decreases and every later finalized
block descends from the earlier one.  Within the fault bound, store
recomputation never selects a finalized-conflicting value.

In plain words, local finality remains irreversible even if the live
available-chain tip reorganizes.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The only assignment to \((F,h_F)\) copies the current later
\((J,h_j)\), which descends from the old \(F\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:finalized-before-justified}.  Store evidence grows by
Lemma~\ref{lem:store-monotonicity}, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:finalized-chain} makes all accepted finalized pairs compatible
under the fault bound.
\end{proof}

Every block is replayed from its own parent state and its own individual
attestations.  Sibling tallies are never combined.  Finalized-conflicting
blocks remain evidence but supply no live fork-choice, height, or finality
support.

Pruning below a finalized root retains the complete authenticated consensus
state, fixed weights, current height, start slot and target, nonjustifiable
flag and debt inputs, both participation arrays, latest justification and its
evidence, receipt records needed by pending actions, and every local durable
signing lock that can still affect an honest action.
Lemma~\ref{lem:target-bit-compression} makes both participation arrays a
complete record of the retained height tallies.

derives fork choice from this raw store.

\section{Fork-choice state and nonjustifiable heights}\label{sec:fork-choice-state}

This section defines the finality-derived root and the periodic height at which
justification is disabled.  The grade construction uses that root as
its floor, and the recovery proof uses the disabled height to exclude an old
justification at the fresh recovery height.

\begin{definition}[Derived fork-choice state]\label{def:fork-state}
Fork choice is computed from the store of Section~\ref{sec:store} as follows.
Let \((\Sigma.F,\Sigma.h_F)\) be the finalized pair recorded in
accepted block states with the greatest height, breaking ties by target
hash --- the same deterministic argmax rule the justification selection
below uses, so the choice is total in every finite store; under the
accountable-safety bound the tie case is empty.  Let \(\Sigma.\T\) be
the accepted block tree restricted to descendants of~\(\Sigma.F\).  Accepted blocks that conflict
with~\(\Sigma.F\) may remain in the store as evidence, but their heads supply
no branch support to fork choice.  Signer-level slashing and round-equivocation
evidence remains valid independently of the referenced branch.  Among latest
justifications in accepted block states whose
blocks descend from~\(\Sigma.F\) and whose justified target itself
descends from~\(\Sigma.F\) --- the operative filter, matching the
pseudocode's \(F\preceq\sigma[B].J\) --- let \((\Sigma.J,\Sigma.h_j)\) have the greatest
height, breaking ties by target hash.  Let
\(\Sigma.\hmax\) be the greatest state-height of any block in this restricted
tree.  Blocks outside the subtree of \(\Sigma.F\) do not supply branch,
height, or finality support.

In plain words, the derived state restricts attention to the finalized
subtree and reads its heights, latest justification, and maximum from
accepted block states.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Viable subtree and Simplex root]\label{def:finality-root}
A block is \emph{viable} in~\(\Sigma\) when it has an accepted leaf descendant
whose state-height is at least \(\Sigma.\hmax-1\).  Write
\(\viableTree(\Sigma)\) for the viable subtree.  The \emph{Simplex root}
is the derived value
\[
  \simplexRoot(\Sigma)=
  \begin{cases}
    \Sigma.J,&\Sigma.\hmax=\Sigma.h_j+1,\\
    \Sigma.F,&\text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
\]
Thus the latest justification roots the walk only while it is exactly one
height behind \(\Sigma.\hmax\).  Once a timeout moves a chain one height
farther, the walk starts from the finalized root instead.
Every SG stable root used by an available-chain walk must lie in
\(\viableTree(\Sigma)\) and descend from \(\simplexRoot(\Sigma)\).  The walk
then stays inside that viable subtree.

In plain words, fork choice keeps only the two highest reachable heights and
starts from the latest justification only while it is one height behind.
\end{definition}

with hash tiebreaks, so they are total functions of every finite valid
store; no fault-bound premise is needed for the derived state to exist.
The bound governs what the selections return, not whether they return:
under the accountable-safety bound, Lemmas~\ref{lem:finalized-chain} and
\ref{lem:local-finality-irreversible} make the greatest finalized pair
unique and monotone as evidence is added, so the finalized tiebreak never
chooses between conflicting pairs, and the justification tiebreak breaks
only same-height ties inside the accepted finalized subtree.  Outside the
bound the derivation remains a total function --- accountable safety
requires the machine to keep operating and producing evidence after a
violation, not to halt on an undefined maximum.  The \(F\)-filter on
\(\mathcal J\) makes finalized-conflicting justifications inert.

\begin{lemma}[The finalized block and Simplex root are viable]
\label{lem:finalized-root-viable}
The viable subtree contains \(F\), and the Simplex root is a well-defined
member of that subtree, including at genesis.

In plain words, pruning and the root rule always leave a valid place from
which fork choice can start.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The tree \(\T\) contains only descendants of \(F\) and has a leaf whose
state-height is \(h_{\max}\), so \(F\) is viable.  Suppose the root rule
selects \(J\).  If \(J=\genesis\), then
Lemma~\ref{lem:finalized-before-justified} gives \(F=J\).  Otherwise, some
accepted block state records \(J\) as a height-\(h_j\) justification.  That
block has state-height \(h_j+1=h_{\max}\), and an accepted leaf descending
from it witnesses that \(J\) is viable.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The fork-choice maximum height never decreases]
\label{lem:filtered-hmax-monotone}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:fixed-electorate}, as an honest store gains valid
evidence, the value \(h_{\max}\) derived after restricting the block tree to
the greatest finalized root never decreases.

In plain words, learning a later finalized root cannot discard every branch
that reached the old maximum height.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(x\) be the old value and let \(F'\) be the greatest finalized root after
the store grows.  If \(F'\) is unchanged, the accepted tree only gains blocks,
so the claim follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:store-monotonicity}.  Suppose instead
that \(F'\) is newly selected at finalized height \(h_F'\), and let \(L\) be
an old accepted block of state-height \(x\).

There are two cases.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item If \(x>h_F'\), Lemma~\ref{lem:past-finalized}, applied to the chain
   ending at \(L\), gives \(F'\preceq L\).  Thus \(L\) remains in the
   finalized-root subtree and still witnesses height \(x\).
 \item If \(x\leq h_F'\), the valid branch that finalized \(F'\) had already
   processed its height-\(h_F'\) justification.  By
   Lemma~\ref{lem:height-progression}, that branch contains an accepted block
   of state-height at least \(h_F'+1>x\), and this block descends from \(F'\)
   by Lemma~\ref{lem:past-finalized}.
\end{enumerate}
In both cases the new finalized-root subtree has maximum state-height at least
\(x\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Store fork choice is total]\label{lem:store-fork-choice-total}
For every finite valid store, walking from the Simplex root through viable
children with any fixed deterministic child rule terminates at a viable
leaf.  Hence the store fork-choice output always exists and descends from
\(F\).

In plain words, the filtered tree is nonempty and a deterministic walk cannot
get stuck.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The finalized and justification selections are deterministic argmax rules
with hash tiebreaks, so \(F\), \(J\), and hence the Simplex root exist
in every finite valid store without any fault-bound premise.
Lemma~\ref{lem:finalized-root-viable} supplies the starting vertex.  A viable
nonleaf has at least one viable child on a witnessing path to a viable leaf.
The accepted store is finite, so repeated strict child steps terminate.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Store fork choice never leaves a finalized block]\label{lem:finalized-lockin}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:fixed-electorate}, once an honest store has processed
the finalization of block~\(F\), every later block in the live fork-choice tree
and every later store fork-choice output descends from~\(F\).

In plain words, finalized-conflicting blocks may remain as evidence but never
again affect fork choice.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The accepted tree is restricted to descendants of the store's greatest
finalized block.  Lemma~\ref{lem:finalized-chain} makes every later finalized
block descend from~\(F\), so this restriction never moves behind or across
\(F\).  Lemma~\ref{lem:store-fork-choice-total} puts every Simplex root and
store fork-choice output in that accepted tree.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A nonjustifiable height cannot become a justification root]\label{lem:nonjustifiable-fg}
If every relevant valid branch enters \(H\) with
\(K\mid H\) and \(H-h_F>D_{\rm debt}\), no justification at~\(H\) can form
on any such branch.
Throughout the
period in which \(\Sigma.h_{\max}=H+1\), every honest
Simplex root is its own \(\Sigma.F\); in particular, finality cannot reintroduce a
height-\(H\) justification as the walk root.  If the store already contains,
or later imports, a branch at height~\(H+2\), the only newly eligible
justification root is at height~\(H+1\), not height~\(H\).

In plain words, after the chain leaves \(H\), an old height-\(H\)
justification can never return as a Simplex root.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Definition~\ref{def:nonjustifiable} sets \(\nj=\mathsf{true}\) at \(H\) on
every relevant branch.  The state transition never selects the justification branch at~\(H\), even if
compatible locked target repeats reach quorum, so no valid height-\(H\)
justification exists.
Every chain with \(\Sigma.h_{\max}=H+1\) therefore has greatest justified height
at most \(H-1\).  While \(\Sigma.h_{\max}=H+1\), we have
\(\Sigma.h_{\max}\geq\Sigma.h_j+2\), so the condition for selecting~\(J\) is
not met and Definition~\ref{def:finality-root} selects~\(\Sigma.F\).  The same
reasoning applies after importing any branch that leaves
\(\Sigma.h_{\max}=H+1\), because a height-\(H\) justification remains
impossible.  When \(\Sigma.h_{\max}=H+2\), the Simplex-root rule can instead
select only a height-\(H+1\) justification.  The recovery proof later shows
that every such justification formed during recovery is on the healed chain.
\end{proof}

One height every \(K\) heights is eligible to become nonjustifiable while
finality debt persists.  The bound \(K\geq2\) ensures that its successor is
not periodic and is therefore justifiable.  Every height retains the ordinary
progress branch, including that successor.
