\title{Fresh Simplex: Graded Healing and Finality Liveness\\
\large Repaired working draft}
\author{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We give a repaired abstract version of Fresh Simplex and write out its healing
and finality-liveness arguments in full.  The finality state machine retains
fixed-target justification, marker-based timeout advancement, and the E1/E2
accountable-safety rules under one immutable integer-weight quorum base per
height.  An empty vote carries a head and finality piggyback but sets no height
marker; both a target vote and an explicit timeout do set one.

The stabilization gadget uses latest signed head messages with the
\(2/3\)--\(1/3\) graded-agreement thresholds.  A round proposer points only to
a root.  Validators locally credit previous-round votes for that root and give
the proposer the benefit of the doubt for equivocators; no vote aggregate or
``record'' certificate is carried.  The abstract round deliberately orders an
available vote and delayed Goldfish confirmation before the SG head pre-vote,
then waits one delivery bound before the FG checkpoint action.  Thus an honest
opening proposer aligns the chain, numeric height, and interval-first target
before every honest validator makes its FG decision.

At a commonly nonjustifiable height~\(H\), the advance through
\(H+1\) removes FG interference because no \(J(H)\) can exist.  A
confirmation-gated marker quorum advances to~\(H+2\); graded concentration,
fresh-root intersection, and Goldfish confirmation safety then make the
certified available prefix permanent.  Above the prior honest signing/reached
frontier, old justifications can no longer redirect the walk.  The first fresh
ordinary height justifies its common opener, and the following round's
finality piggybacks finalize it under finality-first settlement.  No separate
pending-finality guard is used.

Goldfish cross-view confirmation safety and persistence, data availability,
honest-proposer fairness, and canonical inclusion are explicit module
assumptions.  The paper's staged FG timing is intentionally stronger than the
current spread-duty executable profile; the refinement obligation is stated
separately rather than hidden in the proof.
\end{abstract}

\section{Claim boundary and design summary}\label{sec:claim-boundary}

This is a repaired working copy.  The source paper
\texttt{height\_filter\_healing.tex} is intentionally unchanged.

The result has four deliberately separate layers.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Sections~\ref{sec:model}--\ref{sec:safety} define the finality state
    machine and prove accountable safety under one immutable quorum base per
    height.
  \item Sections~\ref{sec:store}--\ref{sec:forkchoice} define a confluent raw
    store, latest-message grades, root-only fresh-quorum syncing, and the
    Goldfish-anchored walk.
  \item Section~\ref{sec:rounds} stages Goldfish confirmation before the SG and
    FG actions.  Section~\ref{sec:signer} defines the confirmation-gated signer.
  \item Section~\ref{sec:healing} separates available-chain healing from the
    later clean-justification and finality-liveness handoff.
\end{enumerate}

The user-facing Goldfish confirmation rule remains floorless and is not gated
by FG state.  The \(G_0\) veto is used only to define the internal
\emph{safe-confirmed} prefix that opens FG target/timeout decisions; it does
not change what users regard as available-confirmed.  General cross-view
Goldfish safety is a module property, not an inference that honest frozen vote
sets or their relative denominators are equal.

\section{Model and immutable quorum instances}\label{sec:model}

\begin{definition}[Quorum base]\label{def:quorum-base}
A \emph{quorum base} is
\[
  {\cal B}=(\mathsf{id},V,w,W,q),
  \qquad
  W=\sum_{i\in V}w_i,
  \qquad
  q=\left\lceil\frac{2W}{3}\right\rceil ,
\]
where every weight \(w_i\) is a positive integer.  For \(S\subseteq V\), write
\(w(S)=\sum_{i\in S}w_i\).  A quorum is a set \(Q\subseteq V\) with
\(w(Q)\geq q\).  Its guaranteed intersection weight is
\[
  \iota({\cal B}) \coloneqq 2q-W.
\]
All threshold checks use integer weights or the equivalent cross-multiplication
\(3w(Q)\geq 2W\); no rounded \emph{adjusted denominator} is part of the
protocol.
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[Base commonality]\label{ass:base-commonality}
For every numeric finality height \(h\), the protocol specifies exactly one
base, denoted \({\cal B}_h\).  Every target, timeout, and finality field at
height~\(h\) names that base identifier, and a state counts the field only
under \({\cal B}_h\).  SG head messages instead name their round and are
evaluated under the recovery-window base of
Assumption~\ref{ass:recovery-electorate}.  A pointer carries only a block root;
its receiver evaluates previous-round SG support under that same round base,
independently of the receiver's current numeric finality height.  The Byzantine
weight \(b_h\) satisfies
\[
  b_h < \iota({\cal B}_h).
\]
The mapping \(h\mapsto{\cal B}_h\) is immutable once height \(h\) is reachable
and is common to all valid chains in the weak-subjectivity domain.
\end{assumption}

When the base is \({\cal B}_h\), write \(w_h\), \(W_h\), \(q_h\), and
\(\iota_h\) for its weight function and three scalar fields.  During the
fixed recovery window, \(w\), \(W\), \(q\), and \(\iota\) without subscripts
refer to \({\cal B}^{\star}\).

The assumption can be instantiated by a fixed validator set, or by a
reconfiguration protocol that activates a new base only from previously
finalized state.  It cannot be instantiated by independently reading balances
from competing proposal post-states.  This paper proves no intersection claim
between different bases.

\begin{lemma}[Weighted quorum intersection]\label{lem:quorum-intersection}
For one base \({\cal B}\), any two quorums \(Q,Q'\) satisfy
\[
  w(Q\cap Q')\geq 2q-W=\iota({\cal B}).
\]
Consequently their intersection contains non-Byzantine weight under
Assumption~\ref{ass:base-commonality}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since \(w(Q\cup Q')\leq W\),
\[
 w(Q\cap Q')=w(Q)+w(Q')-w(Q\cup Q')\geq 2q-W.
\]
The adversary controls strictly less weight than this lower bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Height and interval]\label{def:height}
Height is the finality-gadget counter, separate from slot.  Genesis is treated
as justified and finalized at height \(0\), and the genesis state is at current
height \(1\).  A justification certificate or marker timeout certificate
advances height by exactly one.

The block whose processing enters height \(h>1\) is the height's
\emph{interval opener} \(O_h\), and its slot is the entry slot \(s_h\).  For
height \(1\), the first non-genesis block is \(O_1\).  The opener is fixed for
the lifetime of the height and remains authenticated in consensus state.  The
block that becomes an opener cannot contain a target decision naming itself,
because its root is not available to that decision's signer; descendants can
name it.  A checkpoint-sync state therefore carries the exact \(O_h\) even
when its trusted block is in the middle of the interval.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Blocks and chains]\label{def:block-chain}
A block has a parent, a strictly increasing slot, a deterministic finite set of
signed messages, and a post-state.  Its identity is available to the abstract
transition when that post-state is recorded.  Blocks form a tree rooted at
genesis.
Write \(B\preceq C\) when \(B\) is an ancestor of \(C\), and \(B\sim C\) when
they are compatible.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[FG message]\label{def:fg-message}
An FG message by validator \(i\) has a round number, a head root, and two
independent fields:
\[
  m=(i,r,\mathsf{head},d,\Phi).
\]
The \emph{height decision} \(d\) is one of
\[
  \mathsf{empty},\qquad
  (\mathsf{timeout},{\cal B}_h,h),\qquad
  (\mathsf{target},{\cal B}_h,h,T).
\]
The finality field \(\Phi\) is a finite set of commitments
\(( {\cal B}_{h_f},h_f,T_f )\), with at most one target per height.  An empty
decision has no height and sets no marker.  The head field and \(\Phi\) are
still populated and may respectively support the next round's pointer and
older pending finality.
\end{definition}

A target decision is valid on a chain at height \(h\) only when its base is
\({\cal B}_h\), its target is the stored interval opener \(T=O_h\), and
\(T\) is a strict ancestor of the including block.  At a nonjustifiable height
the state machine disables the justification branch, but an exact compatible
target repeat remains a valid \emph{marker}; this is necessary for a validator
already locked at that height to keep contributing to progress without
violating E1.  A timeout decision is valid only at the chain's current height
and base.  A finality commitment is counted only when it names a retained
justification certificate in the chain state and uses that height's base.

\begin{definition}[Slashing rules]\label{def:slashing}
Two signed messages by the same validator are slashable when either condition
below holds; the two conflicting projections may also occur in one malformed
envelope.
\begin{description}
  \item[E1:] one carries \(( {\cal B}_h,h,T )\in\Phi\), while the other's
    height decision at \(h\) is a timeout or targets \(T'\neq T\); or
  \item[E2:] they contain target decisions at the same height and base for
    distinct targets.
\end{description}
Empty decisions do not conflict with a finality commitment because they make
no height decision.
\end{definition}

E2 makes target equivocation directly accountable.  E1 is the load-bearing
link between finality and every certificate that can carry a conflicting chain
past the finalized height.

\section{Deterministic finality state machine}\label{sec:state-machine}

The post-state of a block is
\[
  \sigma=(L,s,h,s_h,O,\nu_h,{\cal E},{\cal J},J,h_j,F,h_F,{\cal P}).
\]
Here \(L\) is the current block, \(s\) its slot, \(h\) the current height,
\((s_h,O)\) the current height's entry slot and opener, and
\(\nu_h\in\{\mathsf{ordinary},\mathsf{nonjustifiable}\}\) its authenticated
entry-time class.  The class is latched when the chain enters~\(h\) and never
changes while that chain remains at~\(h\), including when finality is processed
in the same closure.  The pair
\((h_F,F)\) is the greatest finalized height and root.  Initially \(h=1\),
\((h_j,J)=(h_F,F)=(0,\mathsf{genesis})\), and \(O=\bot\); processing
the first non-genesis block sets its root as \(O_1\), and
\(\nu_1=\mathsf{ordinary}\).  Whenever a block
\(B\) advances height, its post-state sets
\(s_{h+1}=B.\mathsf{slot}\) and \(O_{h+1}=B\).

Here \({\cal E}[i]\) is the \emph{set} of all valid current-height target and
timeout decisions observed from \(i\), rather than a first- or last-seen
scalar.  The \emph{marker set} is
\[
  Q_{\rm mark}(\sigma)=
  \{i:{\cal E}[i]\text{ contains a valid target or timeout decision at }h\}.
\]
For a block \(T\), its target support is
\[
  Q_T(\sigma)=
  \{i:(\mathsf{target},{\cal B}_h,h,T)\in{\cal E}[i]\}.
\]
The set \({\cal J}\) retains every observed triple
\((h,T,\mathsf{JC}(h,T))\); below, \((h,T)\in{\cal J}\) abbreviates existence
of that retained certificate.  The distinguished pair \((h_j,J)\) is the
greatest retained pair by height and then root hash and is used by ordinary
fork choice; it does not erase older pending pairs.  Finally,
\({\cal P}[h,T]\) is the set of validators whose observed messages carry a
valid commitment to a retained \((h,T)\in{\cal J}\).

Before applying a block's message batch, the transition imports every valid
historical \(\mathsf{JC}(h_f,T_f)\) carried by the block with \(h_f<h\), provided
its base, target state, ancestry, and authenticated ordinary-height
installation witness verify and \(T_f\) lies on the including chain.  A bare
target quorum from a state whose latched class was nonjustifiable is marker
evidence only and is never importable as a justification.  Valid import adds
the triple to \({\cal J}\) and updates the distinguished pair, but does not
rewind or advance current height.  An embedded
\(\mathsf{FC}(h_f,T_f)\) is processed in this order: import its historical
\(\mathsf{JC}\), then count its commitments.  A certificate for the current
height instead contributes its target decisions to \({\cal E}\) and follows
the ordinary height-advance closure.  A same-batch commitment that depends on
the newly created pair does not mutate that block's ancestor-derived poststate:
it remains only raw network or mempool evidence and must be re-included in a
descendant whose prestate retains the pair, or carried in an \(\mathsf{FC}\)
that imports the historical \(\mathsf{JC}\) before counting commitments.

A block batch for block \(B\) is normalized as a mathematical set of signatures
before it is applied.  If the pre-state opener is empty, processing \(B\) first
sets \(O=B\); no message in \(B\) can name \(B\) itself.  Applying the batch
then unions every valid decision into \({\cal E}\) and every valid finality
commitment whose referenced pair is retained by the prestate (or by a prior
historical-certificate import) into its \({\cal P}[h,T]\).  Thus equivocation
is retained and batch iteration order cannot overwrite evidence.

Whenever height closure is invoked, the following ordered closure is evaluated
exactly once.  The compact safety abstraction may invoke it after a block
batch; the liveness refinement invokes it only at round-close settlement after
the timely electorate has been accumulated:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item let
    \({\cal F}=\{(h_f,T_f)\in{\cal J}:h_f\geq h_F\ \wedge\
    F\preceq T_f\ \wedge\
    w_{h_f}({\cal P}[h_f,T_f])\geq q_{h_f}\}\); if nonempty, set \(F\) to
    the target of the lexicographically greatest \((h_f,\mathsf{hash}(T_f))\)
    candidate and set \(h_F\) to its height;
  \item let \({\cal C}=\{T:w_h(Q_T)\geq q_h\}\).  If
    \(\nu_h=\mathsf{ordinary}\) and
    \({\cal C}\neq\varnothing\), choose the least hash in \({\cal C}\), add
    \((h,T,\mathsf{JC}(h,T))\) to \({\cal J}\), update \((h_j,J)\) to the
    greatest retained pair.  Then advance \(h\), set
    \((s_h,O)\) to the current block's slot and root, latch the new height's
    class by Definition~\ref{def:nonjustifiable}, and reset \({\cal E}\);
  \item otherwise, if \(w_h(Q_{\rm mark})\geq q_h\), advance \(h\) and
    reset \({\cal E}\), again setting \((s_h,O)\) to the current block's slot
    and root and latching the new height's class.  This is the timeout branch
    even when some members of the marker quorum are compatible target votes.
\end{enumerate}
Finality is therefore processed before a justification or timeout enabled by
the same block.  The hash choice makes the transition total even outside the
fault bound; Lemma~\ref{lem:target-uniqueness} shows that it is never exercised
between two valid quorum targets under the stated bound.

A target decision therefore has two independent effects: it contributes to its
exact-target justification tally and sets that validator's timeout marker.  A
validator is counted once in \(Q_{\rm mark}\), even if equivocation leaves
several decisions in \({\cal E}[i]\).  Justification has priority when an exact
target quorum and a marker quorum are simultaneously present; otherwise the
marker quorum advances by timeout.  This is why a validator already locked on
the unique target at~\(h\) need not vote timeout: its existing or repeated
target already counts toward the timeout branch.

Finality tallies are not reset by a later justification.  More generally, the
round-close refinement used by the liveness theorem evaluates finality before
justification and timeout after cumulatively processing the round's timely
votes.  Consequently a full piggyback quorum for a clean old justification is
consumed before a simultaneous new justification is installed.  No
pending-finality fork-choice guard is needed.  The abstract machine does not
prune \({\cal J}\) or \({\cal P}\); a bounded implementation needs a separate
semantics-preserving garbage-collection proof.
The state transition never treats an empty message as presence at the height.
Doing so would allow everyone to emit empty messages and advance without any
chain evidence.

\begin{definition}[Certificates]\label{def:certificates}
A \emph{justification certificate} \(\mathsf{JC}(h,T)\) is a transferable set
of valid target decisions for \(T\) with weight at least \(q_h\), together with
an authenticated transition witness showing that the source state had
\(\nu_h=\mathsf{ordinary}\) and installed \((h,T)\) through the exact-target
branch.  A target quorum observed at a nonjustifiable height is not a JC.
A \emph{marker certificate} \(\mathsf{MC}(h)\) is a transferable set of valid
current-height target and/or timeout decisions from distinct validators with
weight at least \(q_h\).  When no exact target quorum is selected first, an
\(\mathsf{MC}(h)\) is the timeout certificate that advances height.
A \emph{finality certificate} \(\mathsf{FC}(h,T)\) is a transferable
\(\mathsf{JC}(h,T)\) together with commitments to \((h,T)\) of weight at
least \(q_h\).
Certificates include their base identifiers and the ancestry required to
validate every nonempty target.  Transferability means that a historical
certificate can be imported on compatible ancestry by the rule above; it does
not authorize a target or finality commitment on a conflicting chain.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Target uniqueness]\label{lem:target-uniqueness}
Two conflicting targets cannot both have justification certificates at one
height and base unless at least \(\iota({\cal B}_h)\) weight violates E2.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The signer sets of the two certificates intersect in weight at least
\(\iota({\cal B}_h)\) by Lemma~\ref{lem:quorum-intersection}.  Every signer in
the intersection signed both distinct targets at height \(h\).
\end{proof}

\section{Accountable safety}\label{sec:safety}

\begin{lemma}[Height progression]\label{lem:height-progression}
Every height transition increments the numeric height by exactly one and is
backed by a justification certificate or marker certificate under the unique
base for that height.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is immediate from the two height-advance branches of the closure rule and
Assumption~\ref{ass:base-commonality}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Opener freshness]\label{lem:opener-freshness}
If a target \(T\) is counted at height \(h\) on a chain ending at \(B\), then
\(T=O_h\preceq B\), and the chain's post-state at \(T\) has current height
\(h\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Target validity requires \(T\) to be the stored opener and a strict ancestor
of the including block.  The opener is set when the chain enters \(h\) (or at
the first non-genesis block for \(h=1\)) and is replaced by the next height
transition, so no height transition occurs between \(T\) and its use as
\(O_h\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Past a finalized height]\label{lem:past-finalized}
Let \(C\) be finalized at height \(h\).  Any block \(B\) whose chain has
advanced beyond \(h\) contains \(C\), unless at least
\(\iota({\cal B}_h)\) weight is E1-slashable.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A chain that advances beyond \(h\) contains either a justification certificate
with signer set \(Q_J\), or a marker certificate with signer set
\(Q_{\rm mark}\).  Finality of \(C\) supplies a finality signer set~\(Q_F\).
Either intersection has weight at least \(\iota({\cal B}_h)\).  Consider any
non-slashable signer in the relevant intersection.  Its height decision cannot
be a timeout, nor a target different from~\(C\), because either would conflict
by E1 with its finality commitment.  It therefore targeted~\(C\).  Target
validity says that a chain which counted that marker contains~\(C\).  Thus, if
\(B\) did not contain~\(C\), every member of the quorum intersection would be
E1-slashable, contradicting the bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finalized blocks form a chain]\label{lem:finalized-chain}
For any finalized pairs \((C,h)\) and \((C',h')\), if \(h\leq h'\) then
\(C\preceq C'\), unless the accountable bound is violated.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(h=h'\), intersect a finality certificate for \(C\) with the justification
certificate for \(C'\).  E1 forces every non-slashable intersection signer to
have \(C=C'\).

If \(h<h'\), the chain on which \(C'\) was finalized advanced beyond \(h\), so
Lemma~\ref{lem:past-finalized} puts \(C\) on that chain.  Both \(C\) and \(C'\)
are on it.  Opener freshness gives state-height \(h\) at \(C\) and
state-height \(h'\) at \(C'\); because state-height is nondecreasing along a
chain and \(h<h'\), we have \(C\prec C'\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Accountable safety]\label{thm:accountable-safety}
Conflicting blocks cannot both finalize unless, for some height \(h\), at least
\(\iota({\cal B}_h)\) weight is provably E1- or E2-slashable.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from Lemmas~\ref{lem:target-uniqueness} and
\ref{lem:finalized-chain}.
\end{proof}

This theorem is asynchronous.  It does not depend on the pointer protocol,
available-chain convergence, grades, confirmation, or nonjustifiable heights.

\section{Confluent evidence store}\label{sec:store}

\begin{definition}[Raw store]\label{def:raw-store}
A raw store contains:
\begin{itemize}
  \item blocks and their state witnesses;
  \item signed FG and head messages;
  \item round-scoped pointer proposals (roots only);
  \item deadline-qualified observation facts
    \(\mathsf{timely}(x,I)\), recording that object \(x\) was admitted for
    instance \(I\) before its deadline; and
  \item the local round clock.
\end{itemize}
Each component except the clock is a set.  Equivocation, certificates, the
current finalized root, accepted pointers, and fork-choice output are
\emph{derived} values.  A timely-observation fact is monotone execution
provenance, not a forgeable wire assertion: if either branch of one execution
recorded timely admission, merging later retains that fact.  A scalar
first-seen proposal, vote, or frozen snapshot is never authoritative.
\end{definition}

A derived block state is a deterministic function of its parent state and its
normalized message batch.  Parent-first evaluation therefore gives the same
state to every common block.  Among multiple certified target candidates in a
faulty execution, the deterministic hash rule is used.  The externally relevant
block set is quotiented by the greatest derived finalized root: blocks
conflicting with it have no semantic effect.

\begin{definition}[Store join]\label{def:store-join}
For stores \(S_1,S_2\), define
\[
 S_1\sqcup S_2 =
 \mathsf{derive}\bigl(
   \mathsf{events}(S_1)\cup\mathsf{events}(S_2),
   \max(\mathsf{clock}(S_1),\mathsf{clock}(S_2))
 \bigr).
\]
A proposer that signed distinct pointers for one round is represented by a
derived tombstone.  If only one store has a proposal it is retained; if their
union reveals equivocation, every proposal by that proposer for the round
is invalidated rather than giving one first-seen privilege.  Equivocal head
votes themselves remain in the event set because Definition~\ref{def:fresh-root}
credits their signer once.  Valid messages that reference unknown blocks
remain queued in the raw event set and are replayed when the ancestry arrives.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Store confluence]\label{thm:store-confluence}
Within the accountable fault bound, on the observational quotient that ignores
blocks conflicting with the merged finalized root, \(\sqcup\) is associative,
commutative, and idempotent.  It does not require access to a common ancestor
store.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set union is associative, commutative, and idempotent; maximum has the same
three properties; and \(\mathsf{derive}\) is deterministic.  Equivocations and
proposal tombstones are predicates of the union rather than processing order.
The event projection of a derived store is its canonical raw-event set, so a
second join does not discard provenance.
The accountable-safety theorem makes all derived finalized roots compatible
inside its fault bound, so selecting their greatest element is deterministic.
\end{proof}

Head messages are live only for their specified round window.  At joined time
\(t=\max(t_1,t_2)\), an event with expiry \(d\) is live exactly when
\(d>t_1\) and \(d>t_2\).  This remains true even if the event was physically
present in only one input.  Anything expired in either clock is therefore
expired in the merge, and physical garbage collection never needs to
resurrect it.  Deadline freezing is different: a
\(\mathsf{timely}(x,I)\) fact in either input survives by set union even after
the clock passes the deadline.

The theorem covers the finality and pointer state defined here.  To include a
concrete available-layer frozen committee, the raw instance descriptor must
retain the exact pinned walk-head/state provenance and ordered weighted seat
base from which that committee is derived.  If two stores contain different
descriptors, the implementation needs either a proof that they denote the same
committee or an explicit canonical conflict rule; a bare cached seat vector is
not enough to inherit this theorem.  Snapshot pruning below a finalized root
must retain the finalized state, quorum-base provenance, current interval
opener, the latched height class and its activation provenance,
ordinary-height installation witnesses for retained JCs, and unresolved
deadline facts.  Active
validators' durable signing histories are not part of the mergeable network
store and are not pruned.

\section{Derived fork-choice state and nonjustifiable heights}\label{sec:barrier}

The raw join of Section~\ref{sec:store} is the persistence layer.  Fork choice
uses the following deterministic projection of it.  Let \(\Sigma.\mathcal T\)
be the accepted block tree restricted to descendants of its greatest derived
finalized block~\(\Sigma.F\); accepted blocks conflicting with~\(\Sigma.F\)
remain raw evidence but are outside every semantic fork-choice aggregate.  Let
\((\Sigma.h_j,\Sigma.J)\) be the greatest retained justification key by
height and then target hash among targets descending \(\Sigma.F\).  Let
\(\Sigma.h_{\max}\) be the greatest state-height of any block in this restricted
tree.
Blocks outside the subtree of the final \(\Sigma.F\) may remain in raw storage
but have no semantic effect.

\begin{definition}[Viable subtree and cascade root]\label{def:cascade-root}
A block is \emph{viable} in~\(\Sigma\) when it has an accepted leaf descendant
whose state-height is at least \(\Sigma.h_{\max}-1\).  Write
\(\mathcal T'(\Sigma)\) for the viable subtree.  The cascade root is
\[
  R(\Sigma)=
  \begin{cases}
    \Sigma.J,&\Sigma.h_{\max}=\Sigma.h_j+1,\\
    \Sigma.F,&\text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
\]
Thus the frontier justification roots the walk only while it is exactly one
height behind the greatest reached state.  Once a timeout has moved some chain
one height farther, the walk falls back to the always-valid finalized root.
Every accepted pointed or fallback anchor below is required to lie in
\(\mathcal T'(\Sigma)\) and to descend from~\(R(\Sigma)\).
\end{definition}

The finalized root is viable: a block that raises \(h_{\max}\) is admitted
only on the local finalized chain, so it supplies a viable descendant witness.
When the cascade selects~\(J\), the justified target itself is at
state-height~\(h_{\max}-1\), so it is viable as well.  Every descent used below
therefore starts from a viable root.

\begin{lemma}[Finalized lock-in]\label{lem:finalized-lockin}
Inside the accountable fault bound, once an honest store has processed the
justification of a finalized block~\(F\), every later admitted block whose
state-height is strictly above~\(F\)'s height descends from~\(F\), and every
later walk output descends from~\(F\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:past-finalized} gives the first claim.  The store filters later
justifications against its finalized root.  If the cascade root is~\(J\), it
therefore descends~\(F\); if the cascade root is~\(F\), the claim is immediate.
Every anchor and every later walk phase is constrained to descendants of the
cascade root.
\end{proof}

Fix protocol constants \(K\geq2\) and \(D_{\rm debt}\geq1\).

\begin{definition}[Nonjustifiable height and common seal]\label{def:nonjustifiable}
A chain entering height~\(h\) is in \emph{finality debt} when
\(h-h_F>D_{\rm debt}\) in the entry transition's post-finality state.  It
latches \(\nu_h=\mathsf{nonjustifiable}\) exactly when it is in finality debt
and \(K\mid h\); otherwise it latches
\(\nu_h=\mathsf{ordinary}\).  The latch is part of authenticated consensus
state and is never recomputed at height~\(h\).  At a nonjustifiable state the
justification branch of the closure is disabled: fresh honest histories
produce timeout decisions, while an exact compatible target required by an
existing E1 lock remains valid only as a marker.  Every other height is
\emph{ordinary}.

A numeric height~\(H\) is \emph{commonly nonjustifiable}, or a \emph{common
seal}, for a recovery domain when every valid \(H\)-reaching chain that an
honest validator can process now or import later has the nonjustifiable latch.
The quantifier includes hidden historical branches and certificates, not only
the chains visible during the recovery interval.  Hence no valid pre-existing
or later assembled \(J(H)\) exists in that domain.  The numeric cadence supplies
candidates under continued debt; common nonjustifiability is the store-wide
provenance condition needed by the healing theorem and is not inferred merely
because one branch sees debt.  A concrete protocol must supply transferable
activation evidence or another common-state argument for this latch.

For a named aligned handoff, a height is \emph{commonly ordinary} when the
common proposal-pinned state used by every responsive honest validator has
latched \(\nu_h=\mathsf{ordinary}\).  This is a local property of that handoff,
not the all-importable-branches quantifier required for a nonjustifiable seal.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Nonjustifiable seal and FG non-interference]\label{lem:global-seal}
If \(H\) is commonly nonjustifiable, no \(J(H)\) can form.  Throughout the
period in which the store frontier is \(\Sigma.h_{\max}=H+1\), every honest
cascade root is its own \(\Sigma.F\); in particular, FG cannot reintroduce a
height-\(H\) justification as the walk root.  If an accepted hidden or visible
transition has already raised the frontier to~\(H+2\), the only newly eligible
justification root is \(J(H+1)\), not \(J(H)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The state transition never selects the justification branch at~\(H\), even if
compatible locked target repeats reach quorum, so no valid \(J(H)\) exists.
Every chain whose frontier is~\(H+1\) therefore has greatest justified height
at most \(H-1\).  While \(\Sigma.h_{\max}=H+1\), we have
\(\Sigma.h_{\max}\geq\Sigma.h_j+2\), so the cascade gate fails and
Definition~\ref{def:cascade-root} selects~\(\Sigma.F\).  The same reasoning
applies to every reveal that leaves the frontier at~\(H+1\), because a
\(J(H)\) remains impossible.  At frontier~\(H+2\), the gate can instead select
only \(J(H+1)\); Lemma~\ref{lem:cascade-compatible} proves that such a root is
compatible with the healing confirmation once that confirmation exists.
\end{proof}

Under continued debt a numeric candidate occurs at least once every~\(K\)
heights.  The bound \(K\geq2\) ensures a candidate has an ordinary successor;
otherwise every fresh height could be nonjustifiable and no new clean target
could ever justify.

\section{Latest-message grades and the available-chain walk}\label{sec:forkchoice}

The liveness theorem uses one uninterrupted recovery window.  To avoid hiding
reconfiguration inside threshold notation, we state its electorate premise
explicitly.

\begin{assumption}[Recovery electorate continuity]\label{ass:recovery-electorate}
Throughout one recovery window, the SG rounds and the FG heights they can vote
on use one common immutable weighted electorate
\({\cal B}^{\star}=(\mathsf{id}^{\star},V,w,W,q)\).  Adversarial weight is
\(b<\iota({\cal B}^{\star})\).  Responsive honest weight~\(H\) is at
least~\(q\).  In every honest SG view at a recovery action, let~\(a\) be the
total live latest-message weight outside that responsive honest set, including
both adversarial validators and any temporarily nonresponsive honest validator
whose stale message has not expired.  The recovery window satisfies the exact
integer inequality \(H>2a\) in every such view.
Every responsive honest validator remains online through the available vote,
SG pre-vote, FG decision, and relay actions of a productive round.  A reconfiguring
implementation may replace this fixed-window premise only with a proof that
the relevant old and new duties preserve the same quorum and intersection
claims.
Each responsive honest validator publishes exactly one SG pre-vote in every
round of the window, including a round whose FG decision is empty.  Each such
pre-vote remains live through the immediately following round's fresh-root and
fallback-anchor evaluations and is superseded by that validator's next honest
pre-vote before expiry.  The recovery proof never relies on an expired vote.
\end{assumption}

For the familiar exact \(2/3\) case, \(q=\lceil2W/3\rceil\) and
\(b<W/3\).  All statements below use integer comparisons against~\(q\) or
cross-multiplication; informal fractions are explanatory only.

\begin{definition}[Live latest head vote]\label{def:head-vote}
After the round's Goldfish confirmation action, validator~\(i\) signs one SG
head pre-vote \((i,r,H)\).  Its later FG message must repeat the same head and
round; either copy is a delivery path for one logical head vote.  Distinct
heads signed by one validator in one round are an equivocation.

For expiry window \(W_{\rm head}>0\), the live latest message of a
non-equivocating validator is its greatest-round unexpired head vote.  A known
equivocator contributes no latest vote.  In an honest view~\(u\), let
\[
 D_u=\sum_{i\in L_u}w_i,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{supp}_u(X)=
 \sum_{\{i\in L_u:X\preceq H_i\}}w_i,
\]
where \(L_u\) is the locally live, unslashed, non-equivocating set.  Support is
subtree-counted, and \(H_i\) denotes validator~\(i\)'s live latest head.
Latest messages are network objects: block inclusion is
neither required nor privileged.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The two SG grades]\label{def:grades}
The \emph{grade-1 fallback anchor} \(G_1(\Sigma,u)\) starts at the cascade
root~\(R(\Sigma)\) and repeatedly enters the unique viable child~\(c\) with
\(3\operatorname{supp}_u(c)\geq2D_u\), stopping when no such child exists.  If
\(D_u=0\), it returns the cascade root.

A block~\(C\) is \emph{grade-0 clear} in~\(u\)'s view when every block
conflicting with~\(C\) has
\(3\operatorname{supp}_u(X)<D_u\).  Equivalently, no conflicting block has
support at least one third of the local live denominator.  When \(D_u=0\),
grade-0 clearance holds by convention.  The \(2/3\) anchor and \(1/3\)
conflict veto are used at every height.
\end{definition}

At most one child of a block can meet the grade-1 threshold because the child
subtree supports sum to at most~\(D_u\).  The grade-0 object is a predicate,
not a uniquely selected block.

\begin{assumption}[Goldfish module]\label{ass:goldfish}
Write~\(\Omega\) for the proposal-pinned available-layer block and frozen-vote
snapshot consumed by one walk and confirmation evaluation.
The available layer supplies a deterministic, viability-restricted
\(\mathsf{goldfishHead}(A,\Omega)\) below an anchor~\(A\), and a floorless
available-confirmation rule using a strict majority of the locally frozen
participating committee.  Confirmation support is subtree-counted: a vote for
a descendant supports each ancestor back to the walk anchor.  The participating
set is frozen after the available vote and evaluated with one-slot delay.  The
module satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item blocks available-confirmed in honest views are pairwise compatible;
  \item if an honest view confirms~\(C\), and at one post-GST available-vote
    action every responsive honest voter starts from an anchor compatible with
    \(C\) and votes for a descendant of~\(C\), then by the delayed evaluation
    \(C\) is common; thereafter Goldfish from any compatible honest anchor
    returns a descendant of~\(C\), and this canonical-prefix property persists
    while the recovery participation and nonconflicting-anchor premises hold;
    and
  \item in an honest-opening slot, common online honest committee weight~\(H\)
    exceeds the committee's total adversarial weight~\(A\).
\end{enumerate}
Referenced blocks and their data are available before a vote or pointer naming
them is actionable.
\end{assumption}

The third clause is deliberately per-view.  Every honest frozen set contains
all common honest weight~\(H\) and may contain an arbitrary adversarial subset
of weight~\(a\leq A\).  Hence
\[
  H>A\geq a
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  H>\frac{H+a}{2}.
\]
The honest proposal therefore has a strict local relative majority in every
honest view.  The proof never asserts equal frozen sets or equal denominators.
General cross-view safety remains clause~1 of the module assumption.

\begin{definition}[Anchored walk]\label{def:walk}
At a round's available-vote action, validator~\(u\) first evaluates the
round-start proposal's pointed root as in
Definition~\ref{def:fresh-root}.  If the root~\(P\) is accepted,
\(R(\Sigma)\preceq P\), and \(P\) is viable, set the anchor~\(A=P\).
Otherwise set \(A=G_1(\Sigma,u)\).  From~\(A\), follow
\(\mathsf{goldfishHead}(A,\Omega)\) inside the viable subtree.  If the result
has state-height below \(\Sigma.h_{\max}-1\), continue through viable children
using the same previous-slot participating-vote plurality, with deterministic
ties, until that height bound is met.  The returned block is
\(\mathsf{walk}(\Sigma,\Omega,u)\).
\end{definition}

The final viability continuation has no confirmation significance; it only
makes the store postcondition total at the one-height seam.  A viable child
always exists until the bound is met, by the definition of viability.

\begin{definition}[Safe confirmation]\label{def:safe-confirm}
A block~\(C\) is \emph{safe-confirmed} in an honest view when it is an ancestor
of that view's Goldfish available-confirmed head and is grade-0 clear there.
The safe-confirmed head is the deepest such ancestor.  This is an internal FG
gate only.  The user-facing available-confirmed head is never filtered or
floored by FG or SG state.
\end{definition}

Safe confirmation is prefix-closed: an ancestor of a safe-confirmed block is
available-confirmed and every branch conflicting with that ancestor also
conflicts with the descendant.

\begin{assumption}[Post-GST delivery and honest opening]\label{ass:honest-opening}
After GST, honest blocks, available votes, SG pre-votes, FG messages, and their
available ancestry propagate within~\(\Delta\).  A round whose first-slot
proposer is honest has the following phase contract:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the preceding round's responsive honest SG pre-votes reach the proposer
    before it chooses its root, and its proposal-pinned merged
    block/available-vote snapshot and pointed root reach every honest validator
    at least one delivery bound before the available-vote action;
  \item the fresh-root transfer property of
    Lemma~\ref{lem:fresh-root-sync} holds for the proposer's direct support;
  \item every honest validator evaluates pointer ancestry and viability on the
    same pinned snapshot.  If the pointed root passes, all use it; if it fails,
    all use the same grade-1 fallback.  Thus every honest available voter uses
    one common viable anchor and votes for the proposal block or a descendant
    on its walk chain; and
  \item after delayed confirmation, every honest validator extends the same
    pinned snapshot and fixes an SG walk output on one chain, at one numeric
    state-height, with one interval opener.  No validator substitutes an
    unrelated locally accumulated snapshot between the available vote and SG
    actions.
\end{enumerate}
This is the exact abstract alignment property to be refined by a spread-duty
implementation.  It does not assume a height advance, a justification, or
finality.
\end{assumption}

\section{Root-only fresh-quorum syncing}\label{sec:pointer}

A pointer is a synchronization hint, not a certificate carried by the block.
Its support is evaluated locally from the immediately preceding round's signed
head messages.

\begin{definition}[Credited support and fresh root]\label{def:fresh-root}
Fix honest view~\(u\), support round~\(r\), and known block~\(P\).  Let
\(V_u^r(P)\) be the validators from which~\(u\) has a valid round-\(r\) head
vote descending from~\(P\), and let \(E_u^r\) be the validators for which~\(u\)
has two distinct valid round-\(r\) heads.  Both sets use only messages admitted
by the proposal instance's available-vote action boundary, represented in the
raw store by the corresponding \(\mathsf{timely}\) facts.  A message arriving
later may support a later round but does not retroactively change this
instance.  Define
\[
  \operatorname{credit}_u^r(P)
   =w\bigl(V_u^r(P)\cup E_u^r\bigr).
\]
Each validator is counted once.  An equivocator is credited to~\(P\) even when
none of the two copies in~\(u\)'s view descends from~\(P\).

The first proposer of round~\(r+1\) may point to one block root~\(P\).  View~\(u\)
accepts it as a \emph{fresh root} at its available-vote action exactly when
\(P\) and its ancestry are available,
\(\operatorname{credit}_u^r(P)\geq q\), the proposal is not tombstoned for
same-round proposer equivocation, and the walk checks in
Definition~\ref{def:walk} (cascade ancestry and viability) pass.  The proposal
carries only~\(P\), never the votes or an aggregate.
\end{definition}

For fixed~\(P\) before the action boundary, the credited numerator is monotone.
Learning a supporting vote adds the signer; learning another copy can only
preserve support or turn the signer into equivocation credit.  At the action,
deadline-qualified provenance fixes which observations belong to the instance.
The denominator~\(W\) is fixed and no later observation can revoke prior
credit, so no separately carried or negotiated fresh-quorum snapshot is needed.

\begin{lemma}[Fresh-support intersection]\label{lem:pointer-intersection}
If two conflicting roots~\(P,P'\) each have credited support at least~\(q\) in
possibly different honest views for the same support round, at least
\(\iota({\cal B}^{\star})\) weight actually equivocated in that round.  In
particular, two conflicting fresh roots cannot both be accepted inside the
fault bound.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The two credited sets are quorums, so their intersection has weight at least
\(\iota({\cal B}^{\star})\).  A signer explicitly present in either local
equivocation set has already equivocated.  Otherwise the two views contain one
signed head from that signer descending~\(P\) and one descending~\(P'\).
Because the roots conflict, the heads are distinct.  Thus every intersection
signer actually equivocated, regardless of which copies either verifier saw.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fresh-root transfer under an honest proposer]\label{lem:fresh-root-sync}
After GST, suppose the first proposer of round~\(r+1\) is honest and points to
a root~\(P\) for which it saw direct (non-equivocation-credit) round-\(r\)
support of weight at least~\(q\).  Suppose its evidence re-gossip precedes every
honest available-vote action by at least~\(\Delta\).  By every such action,
\(P\) has credited support at least~\(q\) in that validator's view.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For each signer counted directly by the proposer, the proposer re-gossips the
valid supporting head message immediately, though the proposal itself still
carries only the root.  The post-GST delivery schedule puts that message in
every honest view before the available-vote action.  If a receiver's relay
logic has already suppressed that particular copy because it retained two
distinct heads from the signer, the signer lies in the receiver's equivocation
set and is credited anyway.  Hence the proposer's direct support set is a
subset of every receiver's supporting-or-equivocating set, so every credited
numerator is at least~\(q\).
\end{proof}

This is precisely the benefit-of-the-doubt case: the proposer may have seen
validator~\(i\) vote for~\(P\), while another validator has only two other
equivocating copies.  The latter still credits~\(i\) to~\(P\).  Synchrony or
equivocation is enough; no receiver attempts to reconstruct the proposer's
exact vote set.

\begin{lemma}[A directly supported root is available to an honest proposer]\label{lem:fresh-root-exists}
After one honest SG delivery boundary, the highest common ancestor of all
responsive honest round-\(r\) heads has direct support of weight at least~\(q\)
and is known to an honest first proposer of round~\(r+1\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each responsive honest validator emits one round-\(r\) pre-vote and their total
weight is at least~\(q\).  Their highest common ancestor lies on every such
head.  Delivery makes all these messages and the ancestor available to the
honest proposer, which therefore sees direct quorum support.
\end{proof}

An honest proposer chooses the deepest directly quorum-supported root that
passes the cascade-ancestry and viability checks in its proposal-pinned view,
with a deterministic hash tie-break; if none passes, it omits the pointer and
the fallback is used.  It broadcasts one proposal.  A
Byzantine proposer may equivocate; the raw-store join then tombstones its
pointer for that round instead of assigning first-seen priority.  This can
delay liveness but cannot create two conflicting accepted fresh roots inside
the fault bound.  There is a new pointer opportunity every round, even when the
numeric height has not advanced; pointers are round-scoped rather than
one-shot height episodes.

\begin{lemma}[Honest opener aligns the fork-choice input]\label{lem:pointer-alignment}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:recovery-electorate} and
\ref{ass:honest-opening}, an honest first-slot proposer makes every responsive
honest validator use the same viable anchor at its available-vote action: the
pointed root if its common checks pass, otherwise the common grade-1 fallback.
At the later SG action their walk outputs lie on one chain and have one numeric
state-height~\(k\) and one interval opener~\(T\), even if their pre-round walks
were split between \(h_{\max}-1\) and \(h_{\max}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:fresh-root-exists} supplies a directly supported root and
Lemma~\ref{lem:fresh-root-sync} makes its credited numerator reach~\(q\)
everywhere.  Those two facts alone do not prove the cascade-ancestry and
viability checks.  The phase contract supplies their common pinned snapshot:
the proposed root either passes everywhere or fails everywhere, in which case
the same fallback is used.  It then makes all honest available voters follow
that common anchor's walk chain.  Its post-confirmation clause fixes all SG
walks on that chain.
The numeric height and opener are read from those fixed walk states rather
than from a receiver's pre-round height, so a prior one-height split does not
cause pointer rejection.  The contract states exactly the remaining
Goldfish/view-merge determinism needed to make the height and interval opener
common.
\end{proof}

\section{Staged rounds and round-close settlement}\label{sec:rounds}

FG time is grouped into rounds of 32 consecutive slots.  The paper does not
spread FG duties across those slots; it models one quorum-contributing FG
action per validator after the proposal has had time to be available-voted and
confirmed.  The strict order is
\[
\begin{array}{c}
\text{first-slot proposal and view merge}
\longrightarrow \text{Goldfish available vote}
\\[-1mm]
\longrightarrow \text{delayed Goldfish confirmation}
\longrightarrow \text{signed SG head pre-vote}
\\[-1mm]
\xrightarrow{\ \Delta\ }
\text{FG checkpoint/finality action}
\\[-1mm]
\longrightarrow \text{round-close outcome settlement}.
\end{array}
\]
An action processes every delivery due at its boundary before it reads state.
The proposal and available vote occupy the first available-layer opportunity;
the time-shifted confirmation is evaluated one slot later; the SG pre-vote is
then delivered for one full network bound before the FG action.  The remaining
slots provide inclusion and settlement capacity.

\begin{lemma}[Delivery before action]\label{lem:delivery-before-action}
After GST, every object published by an honest validator at one phase boundary
is in every honest view by the next phase boundary.  In particular, all honest
available votes are common by delayed confirmation, all honest SG pre-votes
are common before the FG action, and all honest FG messages are common within
one further~\(\Delta\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Successive consuming actions are separated by at least the post-GST delay
bound~\(\Delta\), and boundary deliveries are processed first.  Apply this
once to each arrow in the displayed schedule.  Byzantine objects may straddle
a freeze; the lemma claims commonality only for honestly published objects.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Round-close outcome settlement]\label{def:round-close}
A round boundary marks its current-height outcome due.  Timely messages for
that outcome are cumulatively processed through the prescribed inclusion
opportunity, and then exactly one closure is evaluated in the state-machine
order: finality first, exact-target justification second, marker timeout
third.  No branch is evaluated eagerly while only a strict subset of that
round's timely votes has arrived.

A suffix has \emph{timely settlement} when every due outcome in the suffix is
processed in the next round's first-slot block, or an equivalent common
transition state, early enough that the state (a new height-opening state when
the outcome advances height) is available-voted and confirmed before that
round's SG and FG actions.  At each
such handoff, all responsive honest validators extend the common transition
state through the proposal-pinned snapshot and therefore derive the same walk
chain, numeric height, and interval opener before voting.  This is an alignment
property of the staged handoff, not an assumption that the height advances.

A round is \emph{productive} when responsive honest weight at least~\(q\)
completes the scheduled available, SG, and FG actions and those validators'
current decisions are cumulatively recorded by the applicable round-close
settlement.  A \emph{productive honest opening round} is productive and has an
honest first-slot proposer whose proposal-pinned poststate is common before the
available-vote action.  Productivity means quorum participation and recording;
it does not assert that the outcome is a justification rather than a timeout.
\end{definition}

The safety transition of Section~\ref{sec:state-machine} is the closure invoked
by this refinement.  Safety is insensitive to whether the closure is called
eagerly or at round close; the liveness bounds use the latter because target
priority is meaningful only after the timely round electorate has been
accumulated.

\begin{lemma}[Latest-message concentration]\label{lem:head-concentration}
In the exact \(2/3\), less-than-\(1/3\) recovery regime, suppose all responsive
honest validators publish their next SG pre-vote on descendants of one
chain~\(C\).  One delivery bound later, every honest latest message from the
responsive set lies on~\(C\)'s chain in every honest view.  Every conflicting
branch then has support below one third of the local live denominator, and
remains below it while those honest latest messages stay on the chain.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(H\) be the common responsive honest weight and \(a\) the
view-dependent residual live weight outside that set.  The recovery assumption
gives \(H>2a\).  A conflicting branch has support at most~\(a\), while
\(D=H+a\); hence \(3a<H+a=D\).  Later honest messages only supersede
same-validator stale messages with another descendant of~\(C\).
\end{proof}

A deployment using a nonstandard rounded base must check \(H>2a\) directly
with integer weights; it must not infer the inequality from a rounded adjusted
denominator.

\section{Durable signer and confirmation-gated FG rule}\label{sec:signer}

For each height, an honest signer durably records
\[
  \mathsf{firstTarget}[h],\qquad
  \mathsf{timedOut}[h],\qquad
  \mathsf{lockTarget}[h].
\]
It also records its unique SG and FG messages for every round.  State is
committed atomically before signature release and recovered fail-closed.  A
lock entry retains the corresponding justification certificate and ancestry;
the first-target entry is write-once and \(\mathsf{timedOut}[h]\) is monotone.
There is no one-shot opening instance or target deadline: a stalled numeric
height receives a new round pointer opportunity, and history release is driven
by the next aligned, confirmation-ready FG action.

An honest signer makes a height-\(k\) current decision only from an SG walk
whose stored post-state has reached~\(k\).  In particular, signing at height
\(k\) is evidence that the signer had previously reached that numeric height;
this reach-before-sign discipline is durable across restart and checkpoint
sync.

\begin{definition}[Interval-first target]\label{def:target-release}
Let \(W_r\) be the walk output fixed by the round's SG pre-vote and let
\(k=\sigma[W_r].h\).  The round's target~\(T\) is the first block on
\(W_r\)'s chain whose post-state is at height~\(k\), equivalently that chain's
authenticated opener~\(O_k\).  The current-height decision is about~\((k,T)\),
not necessarily about the pointed root itself.  Thus validators first align on
the walk; only then do they derive one numeric height and interval target.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Finality gate]\label{def:finality-gate}
For every retained, not-yet-finalized pair
\((h_f,T_f)\in{\cal J}\) with \(h_f>h_F\) and \(F\preceq T_f\), an honest
validator includes \(({\cal B}_{h_f},h_f,T_f)\) in \(\Phi\) exactly when:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item it previously signed target \(T_f\) at \(h_f\);
  \item it has never signed timeout at \(h_f\);
  \item it knows the retained justification certificate for \(T_f\); and
  \item its lock entry is empty or already equals \(T_f\).
\end{enumerate}
Before releasing the message it durably sets every corresponding
\(\mathsf{lockTarget}[h_f]\gets T_f\) and retains each certificate.  A later
justification does not displace these commitments.  The finite set form is
the abstract rule; an implementation may aggregate or schedule commitments
only after proving that no eligible clean target can be starved by older
mixed-height history.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Confirmation-gated FG decision]\label{def:fg-rule}
At the FG action, let \((k,T)\) be derived from the already-fixed SG walk and
write~\(C_i\) for validator~\(i\)'s safe-confirmed head.  Define
\(\mathsf{into}(i,k)\) by \(\sigma[C_i].h\geq k\).  After independently
applying the finality gate, validator~\(i\) chooses its current-height decision
in this order:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item If \(\mathsf{lockTarget}[k]=T_L\neq\bot\), repeat target~\(T_L\) iff
    \(T_L=T\), \(C_i\succeq T\), and the message can be included on that
    ancestry; otherwise emit empty.  At a nonjustifiable height the exact
    repeat is marker-only.  A locked validator never signs timeout at~\(k\).
  \item If \(\mathsf{timedOut}[k]\) is true, repeat timeout iff
    \(\mathsf{into}(i,k)\); otherwise emit empty.
  \item If \(\mathsf{firstTarget}[k]\neq\bot\) without a lock, release that
    old target to timeout iff \(\mathsf{into}(i,k)\); otherwise emit empty.
    The release permanently forfeits finality eligibility for the old target.
  \item With no history at a nonjustifiable height, sign timeout iff
    \(\mathsf{into}(i,k)\); otherwise emit empty.
  \item With no history at an ordinary height, sign target~\(T\) iff
    \(C_i\succeq T\); otherwise sign timeout iff
    \(\mathsf{into}(i,k)\); otherwise emit empty.
\end{enumerate}
A new target durably writes \(\mathsf{firstTarget}[k]=T\); a timeout sets
\(\mathsf{timedOut}[k]\).  Every message repeats the already-signed SG
head~\(W_r\).  Empty has no height, sets no marker, but still carries that head
and every eligible finality commitment.
\end{definition}

Timeouts are confirmation-gated but not additionally grade-0-gated.  Once a
validator safe-confirms into the aligned interval, it always contributes
either a target or timeout marker.  If it has not confirmed into the interval,
it cannot safely identify a progress vote and emits empty; this does not exempt
it from a stall penalty.

\begin{lemma}[No retarget and honest non-slashability]\label{lem:signer-safety}
The signer of Definition~\ref{def:fg-rule} never violates E1 or E2.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first-target entry is write-once, so the signer never signs two distinct
targets.  It may add timeout after a losing target only while no lock exists;
doing so disables its finality gate forever at that height.  Once a finality
commitment creates a lock, the signer either repeats the exact locked target or
emits empty.  It never signs a conflicting target or timeout after the
commitment.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Historical evidence transport]\label{def:history-transport}
While a height can affect an honest fork choice, every honest validator
re-gossips:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item every unresolved durable target or timeout decision, including one
    from a past local height that can still be current on a live sibling;
  \item every retained justification or finality certificate and its ancestry;
    and
  \item every live SG head message needed for the latest-message and
    previous-round fresh-root views.
\end{enumerate}
Unknown referenced blocks and messages are queued and replayed.  A signature
does not affect branch-local consensus state until a block or certificate
containing it is processed.  An entry becomes resolved only once common
finalized state makes every competing branch at or below that height
semantically irrelevant; local height advance alone is not a pruning rule.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Locks preserve aligned markers]\label{lem:lock-alignment}
Suppose a round is aligned and confirmation-ready at height~\(k\), interval
opener~\(T\), and one chain.  If an honest validator has a lock at~\(k\), then
either the aligned chain has already advanced beyond~\(k\), or its saved target
is~\(T\) and its already processed or repeated target decision counts in the
marker quorum.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A lock at~\(k\) was created only after the validator targeted~\(T_L\) and
learned \(\mathsf{JC}(k,T_L)\).  If that certificate has been processed on the
aligned chain, height already advanced.  Otherwise the aligned walk at
state-height~\(k\) supplies the confirmation-ready target~\(T\), while the
original target gate made~\(T_L\) safe-confirmed in the lock holder's view.
Goldfish confirmation safety makes \(T_L\) and~\(T\) compatible.  Put them on
their common chain: both are authenticated interval-first targets at the same
numeric height, so opener uniqueness gives \(T_L=T\).  The original target
decision remains a marker whenever present in the aligned chain state, and the
signer rule repeats exactly~\(T\) when another copy is needed.  It never needs
to vote timeout.
\end{proof}

\section{Healing and finality liveness}\label{sec:healing}

The proof has two distinct conclusions.  \emph{Available-chain healing} says
that after the sealed two-height passage, neither FG nor the SG--available
pair can move honest canonical chains to a conflicting branch.  \emph{Finality
liveness} says that later fresh target and piggyback quorums actually advance
\(F\).  The first does not by itself imply the second.

\begin{assumption}[Recovery relay and proposer fairness]\label{ass:recovery-relay}
During the uninterrupted window, honest SG and FG messages are delivered as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:delivery-before-action}; their current-height decisions and
finality fields are cumulatively recorded on the common canonical chain by the
applicable round-close settlement.  The fixed proposer sequence contains a
future honest first-slot proposer.  From that productive opening round onward,
either:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item the execution has a timely-settlement suffix
    (Definition~\ref{def:round-close}); or
  \item honest first-slot proposers recur after each common transition state,
    and each such recurring opener's proposal block descends that state.
\end{enumerate}
Case~(a) gives the explicit round bounds below.  Case~(b) gives only eventual
height progress: each step waits for the next honest opener whose input state
is already common.  Isolated recurring honest openers do not, by themselves,
guarantee a clean justification: intervening rounds can create height-local
history before the next opener.  Finality in case~(b) therefore additionally
uses the clean-handoff condition of
Definition~\ref{def:drainage-frontier}.  A timely suffix supplies its delivery
and settlement clauses, but a post-healing drainage opener and drained common
snapshot are additional named premises rather than consequences of timing
alone.
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[Aligned and confirmation-ready round]\label{def:aligned-round}
For round~\(r\), let \(W_i^r\) be honest validator~\(i\)'s SG walk output,
\(k_i^r=\sigma[W_i^r].h\), and \(T_i^r\) its interval-first target.  The round
is \emph{aligned at \((k,T)\)} when every responsive honest validator has
\(k_i^r=k\), \(T_i^r=T\), and all \(W_i^r\) lie on one chain.  It is
\emph{confirmation-ready} when the current safe-confirmed head of every
responsive honest validator lies on that chain and has reached height~\(k\).
In particular, every such head descends~\(T\); the same-chain clause is
stronger than this prefix consequence.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[An honest opener becomes confirmation-ready in its round]\label{lem:confirmation-matures}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:goldfish} and~\ref{ass:honest-opening}, a post-GST
round with an honest first-slot proposer either observes an already completed
height transition or is aligned and confirmation-ready before that round's FG
action.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:pointer-alignment} gives one chain, numeric height~\(k\), and
opener~\(T\).  Let~\(B_r\) be the honest proposal block.  The proposal-pinned
poststate processes every due certificate and prior-round closure before the
available action.  Under round-close-only height transitions, no further
height closure occurs between that poststate and the round's SG action.  If
this processing already moves the chain beyond~\(k\), the first alternative
holds.  Otherwise \(T\preceq B_r\), and every honest available voter votes for
\(B_r\) or a descendant.  Write~\(H\) for their common frozen committee weight and
\(a\leq A\) for the adversarial weight present in one honest frozen view.
Assumption~\ref{ass:goldfish} gives \(H>A\), hence
\(H>(H+a)/2\).  Delayed evaluation therefore confirms~\(B_r\), and its
ancestor~\(T\), in every honest view without requiring equal denominators.

Every honest validator then fixes and publishes its SG pre-vote on that chain.
One delivery bound later Lemma~\ref{lem:head-concentration} makes every
conflicting branch strictly sub-\(1/3\), so~\(T\) is grade-0 clear as well as
available-confirmed.  Thus every safe-confirmed head has reached the interval
before the FG action.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Aligned confirmation readiness forces one height advance]\label{lem:aligned-height-advance}
If a round is aligned and confirmation-ready at~\((k,T)\), and the responsive
honest decisions and unresolved historical decisions they re-gossip are
cumulatively recorded before that round's closure, responsive honest weight at
least~\(q\) contributes a marker at~\(k\).  Round-close settlement therefore
advances height exactly once: by justification if one target reaches~\(q\),
otherwise by marker timeout.  At an ordinary height with no prior honest
timeout and no honest target other than~\(T\), the advance is specifically by
justification of~\(T\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Partition the responsive honest validators by their durable height-\(k\)
history.  A previously processed target or timeout already set a marker.
Timeout-first validators repeat timeout.  An unlocked old target releases to
timeout.  For a lock, Lemma~\ref{lem:lock-alignment} says either progress
already occurred or the saved target is the aligned~\(T\), and its old or
repeated vote is a marker.  A history-free validator targets~\(T\) at an
ordinary height and times out at a nonjustifiable height.  Confirmation
readiness makes every relevant gate pass, so none of these validators emits
empty.

Their total marker weight is at least~\(q\).  Historical transport and the
recording premise make each old marker visible to the same closure; a newly
emitted repeat or release covers the remaining cases.  The ordered closure
first selects any exact-target quorum and otherwise consumes the marker
certificate, and it increments height only once.  At an ordinary height with
the stated pure history, every old honest target is~\(T\), every history-free
responsive honest validator targets~\(T\), and no honest stake was lost to a
timeout-first history.  Target support itself therefore reaches~\(q\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[One honest opening round advances one height]\label{cor:honest-round-advance}
A post-GST round with an honest first-slot proposer whose honest and re-gossiped
decisions are recorded as in Assumption~\ref{ass:recovery-relay} advances the
common aligned numeric height by its round-close settlement, unless a timely
certificate has already advanced it.  It need not justify at a dirty height;
mixed compatible target and timeout histories may make only the marker quorum
common.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Combine Lemmas~\ref{lem:confirmation-matures} and
\ref{lem:aligned-height-advance}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[At most one catch-up round at a reached frontier]\label{cor:frontier-catchup}
Suppose a proposal-pinned common store has \(\Sigma.h_{\max}=H\), so the
height-filter postcondition permits an honest walk only at~\(H-1\) or~\(H\).
An honest opening round aligns all responsive honest validators on one of
those heights and advances it.  If it selects~\(H-1\), that single settlement
catches the common chain up to~\(H\); the next timely handoff, or the next honest
opening after the transition state is common, acts at~\(H\) unless a certificate
has already advanced~\(H\).  Thus a pre-round \(H-1/H\) split does not require
the sealed-height theorem to assume that every validator was already voting at
\(H\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:pointer-alignment} removes the split by deriving the numeric
height from the common post-proposal walk.  Corollary~\ref{cor:honest-round-advance}
advances that height exactly once.  The round-close handoff or recurring-opener
premise then applies to the resulting common state; in the recurring case the
next honest proposal descends that state rather than reopening its predecessor.
\end{proof}

This is the exact general liveness argument for a locked validator: it need not
``vote for timeout.''  Its vote for the unique lock target already counts as a
marker.  Per-height target uniqueness prevents several honest locks from
requiring incompatible targets on one aligned chain.

\begin{lemma}[A marker advance certifies an honest safe confirmation]\label{lem:safe-confirm-cert}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:base-commonality}, on every marker-based advance of
height~\(h\), at least one honest marker caster safe-confirmed a block in some
height-\(h\) interval in its signing view.  Empty votes cannot supply this
witness.  Because a timeout is intentionally portable between sibling chains
at the same height and base, this general certificate does \emph{not} by itself
identify the opener of the chain that later includes it.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The marker certificate has weight at least~\(q_h\), while adversarial weight is
strictly below the quorum-intersection bound and in particular below~\(q_h\).
It therefore contains an honest decision.  Every honest target decision
requires a safe-confirmed head descending the interval opener; every honest
timeout requires the safe-confirmed head to have reached a height-\(h\)
interval in the signing view.  Empty has no height and is excluded from the
marker set.  Portability of the timeout explains why the conclusion is
per-signer rather than chain-specific.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[An aligned marker advance certifies the common opener]\label{lem:aligned-safe-cert}
Suppose a round is aligned and confirmation-ready at~\((k,T)\), and the
responsive honest round-\(r\) decisions of weight at least~\(q\) are recorded
before settlement.  Every one of those current decisions is a target or
timeout marker made while its signer's safe-confirmed head descended~\(T\).
Consequently~\(T\) is safe-confirmed by every responsive honest signer, is
common, and remains the certified prefix whether settlement advances by exact
target justification or by marker timeout.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Confirmation readiness puts every responsive honest safe-confirmed head on the
aligned chain at height at least~\(k\), hence above its authenticated opener
\(T\).  For a lock, Lemma~\ref{lem:lock-alignment} gives either an already
completed transition or equality with~\(T\); in the nonadvanced case
Definition~\ref{def:fg-rule} makes it repeat that same~\(T\).  The rule makes a
timeout-first or released history emit a fresh timeout only after this current
confirmation, and a history-free validator emit the aligned target or timeout
according to the latched height class.  Thus each current message sets a marker
while its signer safe-confirms~\(T\).  Their recorded weight is at least~\(q\),
so they themselves form a marker quorum even if older portable markers are also
present.  Target-first versus timeout settlement changes the state transition,
not the common confirmed prefix witnessed by this fresh quorum.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Cascade roots remain compatible with an honest safe confirmation]\label{lem:cascade-compatible}
Let~\(C\) be safe-confirmed by an honest validator.  Inside the fault bound,
every current or later honest-store cascade root is compatible with~\(C\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every non-genesis justified block has a target quorum containing an honest
caster.  The target gate made that target safe-confirmed in the caster's view,
so Goldfish confirmation safety makes it compatible with~\(C\).  This applies
to justifications assembled before or after~\(C\) was learned.  Every
non-genesis finalized block was previously such a justified block.  Since a
cascade root is either~\(F\) or~\(J\), and genesis is trivially compatible,
the conclusion survives every later \(F/J\) update.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Concentration excludes conflicting future anchors]\label{lem:anchor-permanence}
Suppose~\(C\) is on the common safe-confirmed chain, all responsive honest
available voters perform one post-GST available-vote action on descendants
of~\(C\) from compatible anchors, and all responsive honest validators then
publish that round's SG pre-votes for descendants of~\(C\).  After their
delivery, no anchor adopted by an honest validator in that or a later round
conflicts with~\(C\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The concentrating walks descend both their fixed anchors and~\(C\), so those
anchors are compatible.  Lemma~\ref{lem:cascade-compatible} makes every later
cascade root compatible with~\(C\).  After delivery,
Lemma~\ref{lem:head-concentration} leaves every conflicting branch below the
grade-0 threshold.  A grade-1 fallback descent from a compatible root cannot
enter such a branch because every step needs at least two-thirds support.

For a pointed anchor, let~\(P_C\) be the highest common ancestor of the
preceding round's responsive honest pre-vote heads.  It descends from~\(C\) and
has direct support at least~\(q\).  If an honest view credited a conflicting
pointed root~\(P\) with weight~\(q\), intersect its credited signer set with
the direct signer set of~\(P_C\).  The proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:pointer-intersection} applies verbatim and forces at least
\(\iota\) actual equivocation; \(P_C\) need not itself have appeared in a
proposal.  Thus no conflicting pointer is accepted inside the fault bound.
Goldfish persistence keeps the resulting walks on~\(C\)'s chain, so later
honest pre-votes do the same.  Induct over rounds.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The sealed two-height passage heals the available chain]\label{thm:available-healing}
Let~\(H\) be commonly nonjustifiable, and consider either of two passage-entry
states:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item a productive round is aligned and confirmation-ready at~\(H\), with
    its responsive honest decisions recorded before settlement; or
  \item a valid marker certificate has already advanced~\(H\), and its
    resulting height-\(H+1\) transition state is common.
\end{enumerate}
Suppose from that entry onward the relay/fairness premise of
Assumption~\ref{ass:recovery-relay} continues.  An honest opening round at~\(H\)
supplies case~(a) unless it observes case~(b); an aligned timely handoff does the
same.  Then the frontier eventually advances
\[
  H\longrightarrow H+1\longrightarrow H+2.
\]
After the concentrating SG delivery there is a common post-seal safe-confirmed
prefix~\(C\).  Normally \(C=T_{H+1}\), the opener certified by the aligned
second advance.  If a previously completed certificate performed that advance
before the aligned action, take~\(C\) from the first aligned
confirmation-ready post-transition interval instead.  In either case no current or
future honest anchor conflicts with~\(C\).  Thus neither FG nor the
SG--available pair can subsequently move the canonical available chain to a
conflicting branch.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In entry case~(b), the first transition is already complete.  Because the
latched justification branch at~\(H\) is disabled, it was a marker timeout.
In case~(a), Lemma~\ref{lem:aligned-height-advance} supplies the marker quorum,
and settlement advances to~\(H+1\) by marker timeout (or learns the same valid
advance from a certificate that preempts its closure).  In either case, begin
the next step from the common \(H+1\) transition state.

Under a timely-settlement suffix, the common transition block opening~\(H+1\)
is available-voted and confirmed before the next SG/FG actions.  The handoff
clause of Definition~\ref{def:round-close} gives one chain, height, and opener;
the delivered SG pre-votes make the interval grade-0 clear before the FG
action.  Under recurring-opener fairness, wait instead for the next honest
opener after that transition state is common.  Its proposal descends the common
transition state by Assumption~\ref{ass:recovery-relay}, so absent a preempting
certificate its aligned height is~\(H+1\); Lemma~\ref{lem:confirmation-matures}
makes the round confirmation-ready.

If no intervening certificate has yet advanced~\(H+1\),
Lemmas~\ref{lem:aligned-height-advance} and
\ref{lem:aligned-safe-cert} advance it to~\(H+2\) and supply
\(C=T_{H+1}\).  If an intervening certificate has already made that
transition, use the next aligned timely handoff under the timely-settlement
mode, or that next honest opening action under recurring-opener fairness.  The
former is confirmation-ready by Definition~\ref{def:round-close}; the latter
is so by the lemma just cited.
Take its aligned opener as~\(C\).  This case has already supplied the two
numeric advances and needs only the same SG concentration boundary.

Lemma~\ref{lem:global-seal} gives the first safety reason: while the frontier is
\(H+1\), no \(J(H)\) exists and the cascade root is~\(F\), so FG cannot
interfere with the available walk.  Once the frontier reaches~\(H+2\), any
eligible \(J(H+1)\) root is compatible with honest safe confirmations by
Lemma~\ref{lem:cascade-compatible}.  The aligned certificate argument above
makes~\(C\) common and safe-confirmed; unlike the general portable-timeout
witness of Lemma~\ref{lem:safe-confirm-cert}, it names the common opener.
Goldfish persistence and the SG pre-votes keep votes on descendants of~\(C\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:anchor-permanence} gives the second safety reason: fallback
anchors cannot cross a sub-one-third conflict, and pointed anchors cannot
conflict without a fresh-root intersection violation.  Hence the confirmation
established at the \(H+2\) healing boundary is permanent.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[How many honest opening proposers are actually needed]\label{rem:honest-openers}
One honest opening round is sufficient for one height advance, not necessarily
for a justification (Corollary~\ref{cor:honest-round-advance}).  Therefore two
honest opening rounds, the second beginning after the first transition state is
common, suffice for the sealed passage \(H\to H+1\to H+2\) when no timely
handoff is otherwise available.  They need not be consecutive wall-clock
rounds, but Byzantine rounds between them do not count toward a fixed bound.

After healing, an honest opener at a clean ordinary height produces the clean
JC.  The following finality-piggyback round does \emph{not} require an honest
first-slot proposer: it requires only that the common JC state be known before
the FG action and that the piggybacks be included before finality-first
settlement.  Catch-up from a \(d-1\) walk and a fresh nonjustifiable height add
the explicit marker rounds counted in
Theorem~\ref{thm:finality-liveness}; this is why ``two consecutive honest
proposers, then one arbitrary proposer'' is a sufficient special case only
when the second opener is already the clean ordinary-height opportunity.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Reached-and-signed freshness frontier]\label{def:dirty-frontier}
For the first common store whose accepted frontier is
\(\Sigma.h_{\max}=H+2\) after the sealed passage, let~\(m\) bound both:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item every state-height previously reached by an honest validator's stored
    walk; and
  \item every height at which an honest validator previously signed a target,
    timeout, or finality commitment.
\end{enumerate}
The boundary is \emph{signing-fresh} when \(H+2>m\).  The quantifier ends
strictly before that first common frontier store, so it includes all old
pre-GST and post-GST sibling history but not the newly accepted frontier state
itself.  Honest walks at the boundary may still be at either \(H+1\) or
\(H+2\); the height filter permits that one-height lag.  The next honest opener
aligns the choice.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The signing-fresh \(H+2\) boundary exhausts dangerous hidden justifications]\label{lem:hidden-j}
Let~\(C\) be the permanent safe-confirmed prefix supplied by
Theorem~\ref{thm:available-healing}.  At a signing-fresh common
\(H+2\)-frontier store, no hidden justification can redirect the walk from
\(C\)'s healed chain:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item every \(J\) of height at most~\(H-1\) is too old for the cascade gate;
  \item \(J(H)\) is impossible;
  \item every already assembled or later completed \(J(H+1)\) is compatible
    with the healed chain; and
  \item no \(J(H+2)\) or higher can pre-exist the boundary.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At store frontier~\(\Sigma.h_{\max}=H+2\), a justification can pass the cascade
gate only at height~\(H+1\), proving~(i).  The common nonjustifiable seal
proves~(ii).  A
\(J(H+1)\) quorum contains an honest target caster; that target was
safe-confirmed and is therefore compatible with~\(C\) by Goldfish safety.
Target uniqueness excludes a conflicting certified target.  Any remaining
compatible height-\(H+1\) target lies on the same chain, and Opener Freshness
makes both targets that chain's unique \(O_{H+1}\); hence every \(J(H+1)\)
names the same compatible opener, proving~(iii).

Every quorum at height~\(x\) contains honest weight, and an honest validator
can sign at~\(x\) only after a stored walk reaches~\(x\).  Freshness says no
honest validator had reached or signed at any \(x\geq H+2\) before the
boundary, so no valid quorum justification at such a height can pre-exist.
This proves~(iv).
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Drainage frontier and clean handoff]\label{def:drainage-frontier}
After available-chain healing, choose a productive honest opening round whose
proposal performs the historical view merge.  Its \emph{drainage snapshot} is
the common proposal-pinned poststate after processing that proposal and every
due certificate or prior-round closure it carries, but before the available-vote
action.  Honest historical transport has made every previously reached block
and every unresolved honest decision that can affect fork choice available in
that snapshot.  Its \emph{drainage frontier}~\(d\) satisfies
\[
 d=\Sigma.h_{\max}.
\]
Moreover, \(d\geq x\) for every height~\(x\) that an honest validator reached
or at which it signed before the snapshot.
No further height closure occurs between this post-proposal snapshot and the
round's available/SG walk.  The height-filter postcondition therefore puts the
opening round's aligned decision height in \(\{d-1,d\}\).  Heights above~\(d\)
have no pre-snapshot honest target, timeout, finality, or lock history.

A \emph{timely drainage suffix} is a timely-settlement suffix beginning with
that honest opening round and lasting through the clean finality handoff.  A
\emph{qualitative clean handoff} is the weaker eventual event in which an
honest opener reaches an aligned, confirmation-ready, commonly ordinary height
\(u>d\) before any honest validator has made any height-\(u\) target or timeout
decision, all responsive honest decisions are recorded at settlement,
that settlement's outcome state becomes a common safe-confirmed prefix before
the next FG action, and all responsive honest messages from that action are
recorded.  These are delivery and scheduling conditions; the target quorum,
justification, and finality are conclusions below.  Recurrence of isolated
honest proposers is not asserted to imply this event.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Fresh ordinary height justifies]\label{lem:clean-justify}
Let~\(u>d\) be a commonly ordinary height reached in a timely drainage suffix,
or let~\(u\) be the height of a qualitative clean handoff.  If the aligned,
confirmation-ready honest decisions are recorded by settlement, the interval
opener~\(T_u\) justifies and the chain advances to~\(u+1\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Definition~\ref{def:drainage-frontier}, no honest validator had any
height-\(u\) history before the drainage snapshot.  Timely handoff makes the
common transition into~\(u\) available and aligned before the first
quorum-contributing FG action there; the qualitative definition states the
same no-prior-conflicting-history condition directly.  Every responsive honest
validator therefore takes the history-free ordinary branch and targets the
same confirmed opener~\(T_u\).  Their weight reaches~\(q\), so target-priority
settlement installs \(\mathsf{JC}(u,T_u)\) before considering the simultaneously
enabled marker timeout.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The next eligible round finalizes a clean justification]\label{lem:later-finalization}
Suppose \(\mathsf{JC}(u,T_u)\) has a responsive-honest target-signer subset of
weight at least~\(q\), none of whose members timed out at~\(u\), and the state
carrying that outcome is common and safe-confirmed before their next FG action.
Every one of those validators carries the finality
commitment~\((u,T_u)\) at that action.  If those messages are recorded by that
round's settlement, \(T_u\) finalizes there.  The round's first-slot proposer
need not be honest.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each quorum signer previously targeted~\(T_u\), never timed out there, and now
knows the retained justification.  It therefore passes the independent
finality gate and durably locks~\(T_u\), regardless of whether its new
current-height decision is target, timeout, or empty.  The recorded commitment
weight reaches~\(q\).  Finality is the first settlement branch and the repaired
state keeps per-pair finality tallies, so neither a simultaneous nor later
justification can erase or preempt the full old quorum.  The closure finalizes
\(T_u\), or a compatible descendant already selected by a higher certificate,
in which case \(T_u\) is in the finalized prefix.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Height drainage and finality liveness]\label{thm:finality-liveness}
Assume a signing-fresh healed \(H+2\) boundary and
Assumption~\ref{ass:recovery-relay}.  Finality eventually resumes provided
that, after available-chain healing, either a timely drainage suffix begins at
a snapshot with frontier~\(d\), or a qualitative clean handoff occurs, in the
sense of Definition~\ref{def:drainage-frontier}.  Under a timely drainage
suffix, from its first honest opening round:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item at most two marker rounds are needed to clear a possible
    \(d-1\) walk lag and enter the first history-fresh height~\(d+1\);
  \item at most one additional marker round is needed if that first fresh height is
    nonjustifiable; the next fresh height is ordinary because \(K\geq2\);
  \item that ordinary-height round forms a clean justification; and
  \item the following eligible FG round finalizes it.
\end{enumerate}
Thus finality occurs within at most five productive settled rounds after the
drainage opener, within four when the first fresh height is ordinary, and in
three in the best case where the opening walk starts at~\(d\).  Later rounds in
the timely suffix require common inclusion and settlement, not an honest
first-slot proposer.  Without that suffix, no fixed count is claimed and
recurring isolated honest openers prove only height progress; the qualitative
clean-handoff condition is the separately stated finality opportunity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:hidden-j} excludes every dangerous pre-healing cascade root, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:anchor-permanence} keeps later walks on the healed chain.  At the
drainage snapshot, historical transport makes~\(d\) both the common
\(h_{\max}\) and an upper bound on all prior honest reached and signed heights.
The walk postcondition and the honest opener therefore align the first decision
height~\(k\) in \(\{d-1,d\}\).  Lemmas~\ref{lem:confirmation-matures} and
\ref{lem:aligned-height-advance} advance~\(k\).  Timely handoff repeats the
aligned marker argument once more when \(k=d-1\), so after at most two rounds
the common chain enters~\(d+1\).

No honest height-\(d+1\) decision predates the drainage snapshot, and the
timely handoff places the common opener and confirmation before the first
quorum-contributing decision there.  If the latched class is ordinary,
Lemma~\ref{lem:clean-justify} forms its clean justification.  If it is
nonjustifiable, the marker lemma advances it by timeout; consecutive numeric
heights cannot both be multiples of~\(K\geq2\), so the next latched height is
ordinary and Lemma~\ref{lem:clean-justify} applies there.  Timely settlement
makes the clean outcome state a common safe-confirmed prefix before the next FG
action, and Lemma~\ref{lem:later-finalization} finalizes it.

If \(k=d\), entry into~\(d+1\), its ordinary justification, and its finality
use rounds 1, 2, and 3; an intervening nonjustifiable~\(d+1\) adds one round.
If \(k=d-1\), the catch-up round adds one more, giving the four- and five-round
cases.  In the qualitative branch, Definition~\ref{def:drainage-frontier}
states exactly the clean-height scheduling, common-outcome, and message-recording
premises; Lemmas~\ref{lem:clean-justify} and
\ref{lem:later-finalization} give eventual finality without a numerical bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Healing]\label{thm:eventual-healing}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:base-commonality},
\ref{ass:recovery-electorate}, \ref{ass:goldfish},
\ref{ass:honest-opening}, and~\ref{ass:recovery-relay}, suppose recovery reaches
a commonly nonjustifiable height~\(H\) in a common proposal-pinned store with
\(\Sigma.h_{\max}=H\), and a future round has an honest first-slot proposer on
that store.  Its aligned height is in \(\{H-1,H\}\).  If it selects~\(H-1\),
Corollary~\ref{cor:frontier-catchup} supplies one catch-up transition.  Under
timely-settlement mode, the next handoff acts aligned at~\(H\) unless a
certificate already advanced~\(H\); under recurring-opener fairness, wait for
the next honest opening after the transition state is common, which has the
same dichotomy.  If the first round selects~\(H\), that dichotomy applies
immediately.  Its two outcomes are respectively passage-entry cases~(a)
and~(b) of Theorem~\ref{thm:available-healing}.  Suppose the first common
\(H+2\)-frontier boundary is signing-fresh.
For the finality conclusion, additionally suppose that after healing either a
timely drainage suffix or a qualitative clean handoff of
Definition~\ref{def:drainage-frontier} occurs.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the frontier advances through \(H\to H+1\to H+2\);
  \item after the concentrating SG boundary the available chain never reorgs
    across the healed safe-confirmed prefix; and
  \item a later fresh ordinary target justifies and finalizes.
\end{enumerate}
Accountable safety remains Theorem~\ref{thm:accountable-safety} independently
of every synchrony and participation premise above.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Corollary~\ref{cor:frontier-catchup} establishes passage-entry case~(a) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:available-healing} immediately or after its one catch-up
round, unless a certificate has already supplied passage-entry case~(b).
That theorem then proves the two advances and available-chain
permanence.  Lemma~\ref{lem:hidden-j} proves that the fresh boundary removes the
remaining hidden-justification interference.  Theorem~\ref{thm:finality-liveness}
then supplies a clean justification and its next-round finality handoff.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Recurring healing]\label{cor:recurring-healing}
If every later finite disruption is followed by another uninterrupted window
satisfying the theorem's premises at a fresh common nonjustifiable height, then
finality recurs infinitely often.  Finitely many disruptions followed by one
stable suffix are the special case in which finality resumes.  Continued
ordinary finality additionally uses the protocol's standard ongoing
participation, delivery, and inclusion liveness premises, which are not proved
here.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:eventual-healing} after each finite disruption.  Each
clean target lies at a strictly greater numeric height than the preceding
finalized target, so every application strictly advances~\(h_F\).
\end{proof}

\section{Incentives and empty decisions}\label{sec:incentives}

The safety and healing theorems do not depend on rewards or the inactivity
leak.  The only required incentive-facing fact is structural: an empty decision
sets no timeout marker and cannot exempt its signer from a stall penalty.
Otherwise all validators could vote empty, avoid the penalty, and prevent
progress forever.

This draft makes no quantitative leak lower-bound claim.  A deployment may pay
FG target and finality participation once per round or once per height; that is
an accounting choice whose eligible electorate, rounding, fork-transition
settlement, and duty-epoch reward basis must be specified separately.  Paying
target/finality flags once per height avoids repeatedly rewarding the same
stalled checkpoint, but the practical gain is limited because the inactivity
leak quickly removes positive rewards during a stall.  The liveness proof is
independent of that choice.

\section{Refinement and deployment obligations}\label{sec:refinement}

The abstract theorem is complete only for the model above.  Claiming that an
executable specification implements it additionally requires:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a proof that every height has one immutable balance/validator base
    across forks and checkpoint sync;
  \item state-binding weak-subjectivity validation and a Store migration that
    preserves the current height, its latched ordinary/nonjustifiable class,
    base, finalized root, and exact interval opener, including a noncircular
    executable representation of the opener root or authenticated header;
  \item an authenticated JC installation witness proving that the source
    height was ordinary, so a quorum of marker-only locked repeats at a
    nonjustifiable height can never be reinterpreted as a historical JC;
  \item an executable first-slot proposer rule that selects a directly
    quorum-supported root, writes only that root, and re-gossips supporting SG
    messages without turning them into a carried certificate;
  \item mandatory queue/replay for valid messages with unknown referenced
    blocks;
  \item a refinement from spread validator duties to the staged order of
    Section~\ref{sec:rounds}, or a proof that a selected full duty round observes
    the required confirmation and SG concentration before its earliest
    quorum-contributing FG action;
  \item a concrete proof of the honest-opening phase contract: proposal-pinned
    view merge, local fresh-root transfer, Goldfish committee majority,
    height/target alignment, and confirmation before SG/FG;
  \item transferable provenance for the common nonjustifiable latch across all
    live and later-importable branches, rather than a branch-local debt test;
  \item a pacemaker/proposer-fairness proof instantiating the exact aligned
    opener at~\(H\), cumulative marker inclusion, and either a post-healing
    timely drainage suffix or the qualitative clean-handoff schedule.  Merely
    recurring isolated honest openers proves height drainage, not finality;
  \item proofs of Goldfish cross-view confirmation safety and persistence,
    committee sampling, ancestry/data availability, and recovery-electorate
    continuity and the residual-live inequality across activation, exit,
    expiry, and fork boundaries;
  \item raw timely-observation facts and frozen-committee provenance sufficient
    for the Store join, plus semantics-preserving bounded garbage collection;
  \item tests for accountable safety, target-plus-timeout marker closure,
    arbitrary locked/dirty histories, split \(h_{\max}-1/h_{\max}\) alignment,
    equivocation-credit pointer transfer, Store join laws, target quorums at a
    sealed height remaining marker-only, finality-first closure not reopening a
    latched height, portable-timeout versus aligned-opener witnessing, the full
    \(H\to H+1\to H+2\) trace, hidden-\(J\) exhaustion, the five-round worst
    drainage schedule, and next-round finality; and
  \item reward tests across round, epoch, activation/exit, and fork boundaries.
\end{enumerate}

User-facing available confirmation remains the ordinary floorless Goldfish
output.  The paper uses its delayed relative majority for honest-opening
liveness and relies on the Goldfish module for general cross-view safety; it
does not silently infer safety from equal local frozen sets.

\section{What was simplified}\label{sec:simplification}

The repaired dependency graph is:
\[
\begin{array}{c}
\text{immutable height base + E1/E2}\\
\Downarrow\\
\text{accountable safety}\\
\Downarrow\\
\text{root-only fresh quorum + Goldfish confirmation}\\
\Downarrow\\
\text{SG pre-vote concentration + confirmation-gated markers}\\
\Downarrow\\
\text{common nonjustifiable }H\to H+1\to H+2\\
\Downarrow\\
\text{SG/available-chain permanence + hidden-}J\text{ exhaustion}\\
\Downarrow\\
\text{fresh ordinary justification + next-round finality.}
\end{array}
\]
No temporary finality guard, carried pointer certificate, or record-based view
merge appears in this graph.  The load-bearing pieces are immutable bases,
durable no-retarget history, the \(2/3\)--\(1/3\) grades, root-only fresh-quorum
intersection, delayed Goldfish confirmation, empty-is-not-a-marker, the common
nonjustifiable seal, and finality-first settlement.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

The result is conditional, not unconditional:
\begin{itemize}
  \item below quorum online weight, finality need not progress;
  \item without the proposal-pinned honest-opening contract, a common fresh
    root need not by itself imply identical Goldfish walks, numeric heights, or
    interval targets;
  \item without an honest future first-slot proposer, synchrony alone does not
    create the initial alignment round;
  \item without a timely drainage suffix, recurring honest openers alone prove
    only height progress; finality additionally needs the stated qualitative
    clean handoff, and no round bound is claimed;
  \item without a common nonjustifiable seal, a branch-local debt predicate
    does not exclude every hidden \(J(H)\);
  \item without the reached-and-signed freshness bound, a hidden
    \(J(H+2)\) may predate the alleged fresh boundary;
  \item without a drained common frontier~\(d\), a later height cannot be
    declared history-fresh merely because it lies above the earlier
    \(H+2\)-boundary frontier~\(m\);
  \item without recovery-electorate continuity through the piggyback action, a
    clean justification need not retain an online finality quorum;
  \item without data and ancestry availability, certificates cannot be
    validated;
  \item without Goldfish confirmation safety/persistence, the per-view honest
    committee majority calculation is not a general cross-view safety theorem;
    and
  \item without base commonality, quorum intersection says nothing.
\end{itemize}
These are theorem premises rather than hidden proof steps.
