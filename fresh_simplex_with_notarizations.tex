\section{Model and definitions}

We consider a system of~$n$ validators of which at most~$f$ are adversarial, where $n \geq 3f + 1$.
A \emph{quorum} is any set of at least $2n/3$ validators.
Any two quorums overlap in at least $n/3 + 1 > f$ validators, so their intersection contains at least one honest validator.

\begin{definition}[Height]\label{def:height}
\emph{Height} is a counter for the finality gadget, decoupled from slots, rounds, and epochs.
The genesis height is $0$.
Height advances by exactly~$1$ via one of two mechanisms: fixed-target justification (Definition~\ref{def:justification}) or prefix notarization (Definition~\ref{def:notarization}).
There are no timeouts.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Block]\label{def:block}
A \emph{block} has a \emph{parent} (another block), a \emph{slot} field, and contains a set of votes (Definition~\ref{def:vote}).
The \emph{genesis block}~$\genesis$ has no parent and slot~$0$.
Slots are strictly increasing along any chain: for every non-genesis block~$B$, $B.\slot > B.\parent.\slot$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Chain and divergence]\label{def:chain}
A \emph{chain} is a sequence of blocks forming a path from the genesis block, where each block's parent is its predecessor in the sequence.
Since a chain is uniquely determined by its last block, we identify chains with their last block and use the two interchangeably.
Two chains \emph{conflict} if neither is a prefix of the other.
The \emph{divergence point} of two conflicting chains~$X$ and~$Y$ is the last block they share.
\end{definition}

Blocks form a tree rooted at~$\genesis$; each block has a unique path to the root.
We write $B \preceq B'$ (equivalently $B' \succeq B$) when $B$ is an ancestor of~$B'$ (or $B = B'$), and $B \sim B'$ when $B$ and $B'$ are \emph{compatible}: $B \preceq B'$ or $B' \preceq B$.
Among blocks on the same chain, \emph{higher} means further from genesis (i.e., $B' \succ B$).

\begin{definition}[Vote]\label{def:vote}
A \emph{vote} is a signed tuple
\[
  (\validator,\; \height,\; \slot,\; \target,\; \kind,\; \mathsf{finalize\_height},\; \mathsf{finalize\_target})
\]
where:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\validator$ identifies the signer;
  \item $\height$ is a height;
  \item $\slot$ is a slot number used for tiebreaking in the fork-choice store;
  \item $\target$ is a block (never~$\bot$);
  \item $\kind \in \{\just,\, \notar\}$ distinguishes the vote type;
  \item $\mathsf{finalize\_height}$ is either a height or~$\bot$;
  \item $\mathsf{finalize\_target}$ is either a block or~$\bot$, and is $\bot$ exactly when $\mathsf{finalize\_height} = \bot$.
\end{itemize}
A vote with $\mathsf{finalize\_height} = h_f$ and $\mathsf{finalize\_target} = T_f$ explicitly commits the signer to finalizing~$T_f$ at height~$h_f$.
A vote included on a chain is verified to reference a target (and, when present, a finalize target) that actually exists on that chain.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{State.}
The \emph{state after block~$B$}, written $\sigma[B]$, is the tuple
\[
  (L,\; s,\; h,\; s_h,\; \mathsf{jtargets},\; \mathsf{ntargets},\; J,\; h_j,\; N,\; h_n,\; F,\; P).
\]
A block~$B'$ is \emph{on the chain ending at~$B$} iff $B' \preceq B$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item $L$: the \emph{chain head}, equal to~$B$ after processing block~$B$.
  \item $s$: the \emph{current slot}, incremented once per slot by $\textsf{processSlot}$ (Figure~\ref{alg:state-machine}).
  \item $h$: the \emph{current height}.
  \item $s_h$: the \emph{slot at which the current height began on this chain} --- the slot of the block whose processing last advanced~$h$ on this chain (genesis's slot~$0$ if no advance has yet occurred).
  \item $\mathsf{jtargets}[1..n]$: for each validator~$i$, the block~$i$ voted for with a $\just$ vote at the current height ($\bot$ if no such vote has been processed).
  \item $\mathsf{ntargets}[1..n]$: for each validator~$i$, the block~$i$ voted for with a $\notar$ vote at the current height ($\bot$ if no such vote has been processed).
  \item $J$: the \emph{justified block}, from the most recent justification event.
  \item $h_j$: the height at which~$J$ was justified.
  \item $N$: the \emph{latest notarized block}, from the most recent justification or notarization event ($J$ is automatically also a notarization, as noted in Definition~\ref{def:notarization}).
  \item $h_n$: the height at which~$N$ was notarized (or justified).
  \item $F$: the most recently finalized block.
  \item $P \subseteq \{1, \ldots, n\}$: the validators who have cast a vote with $\mathsf{finalize\_height} = h_j$ and $\mathsf{finalize\_target} = J$.
\end{itemize}
The genesis state is
\[
  \sigma[\genesis] = (\genesis,\; 0,\; 0,\; 0,\; \bot^n,\; \bot^n,\; \genesis,\; 0,\; \genesis,\; 0,\; \genesis,\; \emptyset).
\]
When unambiguous we drop the chain prefix and refer to components of $\sigma[L]$ simply as $L$, $s$, $h$, $s_h$, $\mathsf{jtargets}$, etc.

\begin{definition}[State-height of a block]\label{def:sheight}
The \emph{state-height} of a block~$B$ (on its own chain) is $\sigma[B].h$, i.e.\ the height of the finality gadget after processing~$B$ as the head of its own chain.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Height freshness.}
Votes in this system are processed under a \emph{height-freshness} rule.
A vote~$v$ with target~$T$ at height~$v.\height$ is processed on a chain with current state $\sigma[L]$ only if
\[
  \sigma[L].h = v.\height \;\wedge\; T \preceq L \;\wedge\; T.\slot \geq \sigma[L].s_h.
\]
The first condition says the chain is currently at the right height; the second says the target lies on this chain; the third says $T$'s slot is no earlier than the slot at which the chain's current height began.
Intuitively, a fresh vote for target~$T$ at height~$h$ means ``the voter saw~$T$ as a chain of height~$h$''.

\begin{remark}[Freshness in terms of~$T$'s own chain]\label{rem:fresh-equiv}
The slot-based check is equivalent to the characterization ``$T$'s own chain reached state-height~$h$'', i.e., $\sigma[T].h = h$.
Reason: state-height is monotonically non-decreasing along any chain (Lemma~\ref{lem:slotmono}), it equals~$h$ on~$L$, and it advanced from~$h-1$ to~$h$ at the block whose slot is~$\sigma[L].s_h$.
So the set of blocks on~$L$'s chain at state-height exactly~$h$ is precisely $\{B \preceq L : B.\slot \geq \sigma[L].s_h\}$.
In particular, $T.\slot \geq \sigma[L].s_h$ and $T \preceq L$ together imply $\sigma[T].h = h$.
\end{remark}

This rule is central: it ensures every justification or notarization produced on a chain at height~$h$ is witnessed by a set of targets whose own chains reached exactly height~$h$.

\begin{definition}[Fixed-target justification]\label{def:justification}
A block~$T$ is \emph{justified at height~$h$} on a chain iff at least $2n/3$ validators have been recorded in $\mathsf{jtargets}$ with target~$T$:
\[
  \bigl|\{i \,:\, \mathsf{jtargets}[i] = T\}\bigr| \;\geq\; 2n/3.
\]
Only $\just$ votes whose target is~$T$ itself contribute; descendants of~$T$ do not count.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Prefix notarization]\label{def:notarization}
A block~$T$ is \emph{notarized at height~$h$} on a chain ending at~$B$ iff walking from~$B$ toward genesis and summing the recorded $\notar$ counts, the cumulative total first reaches $2n/3$ at~$T$:
\[
  T \;=\; \max_{\preceq}\Bigl\{ B' \preceq B \,:\, \textstyle\sum_{B'' \,:\, B' \preceq B'' \preceq B} |\{i : \mathsf{ntargets}[i] = B''\}| \;\geq\; 2n/3 \Bigr\}
\]
where the maximum is taken with respect to~$\preceq$ (i.e., the highest such~$T$), when the set on the right is non-empty; if the set is empty, no block is notarized at this height on this chain, and the notarization branch of $\textsf{processHeight}$ does not fire.
Equivalently, whenever notarization does fire, $|\{i : T \preceq \mathsf{ntargets}[i] \preceq B\}| \geq 2n/3$.
Unlike justification, notarization uses \emph{prefix counting}: $\notar$ votes for descendants of~$T$ contribute.

A justification of~$T$ is also a notarization of~$T$: a justification requires $2n/3$ $\just$ votes for~$T$ specifically, which trivially satisfies the prefix-count threshold at~$T$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Finality]\label{def:finality}
The justified block~$J$ is \emph{finalized} when $|P| \geq 2n/3$.
We say $J$ is \emph{finalized at height~$h_j$}, where $h_j$ is the height at which~$J$ was justified.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Slashing condition]\label{def:slashing}
A validator is \emph{slashable} (or an \emph{equivocator}) if it signed two votes~$a$, $b$ (possibly $a = b$) satisfying the \emph{target / finalize-target conflict} rule:
$b$ carries a finalize commitment ($b.\mathsf{finalize\_target} \neq \bot$), $a$ has been cast at $b$'s commit height, and $a$'s target disagrees with $b$'s finalize target,
\begin{align*}
  &a.\height = b.\mathsf{finalize\_height} \;\wedge\;
  a.\target \neq b.\mathsf{finalize\_target}.
\end{align*}
In words, a validator who signs $\mathsf{finalize\_target} = T_f$ at $\mathsf{finalize\_height} = h_f$ must not also sign \emph{any} vote (justification or notarization) with $\height = h_f$ and a different target.
We refer to this rule as~E1 in what follows.
\end{definition}

There is no direct slashing rule linking two $\just$ (or two $\notar$) votes at the same height; honest per-height uniqueness of $\just$ votes is instead imposed as a behavioral constraint on honest validators (Definition~\ref{def:voting-rule}).
A validator who never sets $\mathsf{finalize\_target} \neq \bot$ trivially avoids slashing.

\paragraph{State transitions.}
The state machine exposes two per-chain update routines (Figure~\ref{alg:state-machine}): $\textsf{processSlot}$ and $\textsf{processBlock}$.
A node invokes $\textsf{processSlot}$ once per slot on every chain it maintains, and $\textsf{processBlock}$ additionally whenever a block~$B$ is processed on that chain.

$\textsf{processSlot}(\sigma)$ advances the slot counter via $s \gets s + 1$ and then invokes $\textsf{processHeight}$ once.
$\textsf{processHeight}$ checks, in order: finality ($|P| \geq 2n/3 \Rightarrow F \gets J$); justification (if some $T$ has $|\{i : \mathsf{jtargets}[i] = T\}| \geq 2n/3$, set $(J, N) \gets (T, T)$, $(h_j, h_n) \gets (h, h)$, $P \gets \emptyset$, $h \gets h+1$, $s_h \gets L.\slot$, and reset the trackers); and finally prefix notarization (walk from~$L$ toward genesis summing $\mathsf{ntargets}$-counts and let $T^*$ be the highest block at which the cumulative total first reaches $2n/3$, then set $(N, h_n) \gets (T^*, h)$, $h \gets h+1$, $s_h \gets L.\slot$, and reset the trackers).
A single $\textsf{processHeight}$ invocation fires at most one height-advance: the justification and notarization branches each reset $\mathsf{jtargets}$ and $\mathsf{ntargets}$, so no further quorum can form without fresh votes being processed.

$\textsf{processBlock}(\sigma, B)$ is the block-driven update, with precondition $\sigma.s = B.\slot$: the caller advances $\sigma.s$ up to~$B.\slot$ via $\textsf{processSlot}$ first, then $\textsf{processBlock}$ sets $L \gets B$ and processes each vote~$v$ in~$B$ via $\textsf{processVote}$, which updates $\mathsf{jtargets}$, $\mathsf{ntargets}$, and~$P$.
On a fresh vote, $\mathsf{ntargets}[i]$ is updated to $\max_{\preceq}(\mathsf{ntargets}[i],\, v.\target)$ regardless of kind, so $\just$ votes also contribute to the prefix-notarization tally; additionally, if $v.\kind = \just$ then $\mathsf{jtargets}[i] \gets v.\target$.
The $\max_{\preceq}$ is well-defined because both arguments lie on $\sigma.L$ at state-height~$\sigma.h$ (treating $\bot$ as~$\genesis$).
The $P$-gate adds~$i$ to~$P$ iff $v.\mathsf{finalize\_height} = \sigma.h_j$ and $v.\mathsf{finalize\_target} = \sigma.J$; this uses only local $h_j$,~$J$, not freshness.
$\textsf{processBlock}$ does not itself advance~$h$: height advances are the responsibility of $\textsf{processSlot}$, which runs at every slot (including slots without a block).

\begin{algorithmic}[1]

\Function{$\textsf{processSlot}$}{$\sigma$} \Comment{Runs once per slot on every chain, even without a block}
  \State $\sigma.s \gets \sigma.s + 1$
  \State $\sigma \gets \textsf{processHeight}(\sigma)$
  \State \Return $\sigma$
\EndFunction
\vspace{3pt}

\Function{$\textsf{processBlock}$}{$\sigma,\; B$}
  \State \textbf{assert} $\sigma.s = B.\slot$ \Comment{Precondition: caller advanced $\sigma.s$ via $\textsf{processSlot}$}
  \State $\sigma.L \gets B$
  \For{each vote $v$ in $B$} $\sigma \gets \textsf{processVote}(\sigma,\, v)$ \EndFor
  \State \Return $\sigma$
\EndFunction
\vspace{3pt}

\Function{$\textsf{processVote}$}{$\sigma,\; v$}
  \State $i \gets v.\validator$
  \State $\mathsf{fresh} \gets (v.\height = \sigma.h) \land (v.\target \preceq \sigma.L) \land (v.\target.\slot \geq \sigma.s_h)$
  \If{$\mathsf{fresh}$}
    \State $\sigma.\mathsf{ntargets}[i] \gets \max_{\preceq}(\sigma.\mathsf{ntargets}[i],\, v.\target)$ \Comment{both kinds bump $\mathsf{ntargets}$}
    \If{$v.\kind = \just$}
      \State $\sigma.\mathsf{jtargets}[i] \gets v.\target$
    \EndIf
  \EndIf
  \If{$v.\mathsf{finalize\_height} = \sigma.h_j$ \textbf{and} $v.\mathsf{finalize\_target} = \sigma.J$}
    \State $\sigma.P \gets \sigma.P \cup \{i\}$
  \EndIf
  \State \Return $\sigma$
\EndFunction
\vspace{3pt}

\Function{$\textsf{processHeight}$}{$\sigma$}
  \If{$|\sigma.P| \geq 2n/3$} \Comment{Finality}
    \State $\sigma.F \gets \sigma.J$
  \EndIf
  \State $T \gets \arg\max_{T' \in \{\sigma.\mathsf{jtargets}[i] \,:\, \sigma.\mathsf{jtargets}[i] \neq \bot\}} |\{i : \sigma.\mathsf{jtargets}[i] = T'\}|$ \Comment{$T \gets \bot$ if the set is empty; the next guard then skips this branch via the $2n/3$ check}
  \If{$T \neq \bot$ \textbf{and} $|\{i : \sigma.\mathsf{jtargets}[i] = T\}| \geq 2n/3$} \Comment{Justification (also a notarization)}
    \State $\sigma.J \gets T$, \; $\sigma.h_j \gets \sigma.h$, \; $\sigma.P \gets \emptyset$
    \State $\sigma.N \gets T$, \; $\sigma.h_n \gets \sigma.h$
    \State $\sigma.h \gets \sigma.h + 1$, \; $\sigma.s_h \gets \sigma.L.\slot$
    \State reset $\mathsf{jtargets}, \mathsf{ntargets}$
    \State \Return $\sigma$
  \EndIf
  \State $\mathsf{count} \gets 0$ \Comment{Prefix notarization walk}
  \For{$B$ from $\sigma.L$ down to $\genesis$}
    \State $\mathsf{count} \gets \mathsf{count} + |\{i : \sigma.\mathsf{ntargets}[i] = B\}|$
    \If{$\mathsf{count} \geq 2n/3$} \Comment{Notarization fires at $B$}
      \State $\sigma.N \gets B$, \; $\sigma.h_n \gets \sigma.h$
      \State $\sigma.h \gets \sigma.h + 1$, \; $\sigma.s_h \gets \sigma.L.\slot$
      \State reset $\mathsf{jtargets}, \mathsf{ntargets}$
      \State \Return $\sigma$
    \EndIf
  \EndFor
  \State \Return $\sigma$
\EndFunction
\vspace{3pt}

\Function{$\textsf{finalizeConflict}$}{$v_1, v_2$} \Comment{Does $v_1$ conflict with $v_2$'s finalize commitment?}
  \State \Return $v_2.\mathsf{finalize\_target} \neq \bot \;\wedge\; v_1.\height = v_2.\mathsf{finalize\_height} \;\wedge\; v_1.\target \neq v_2.\mathsf{finalize\_target}$
\EndFunction
\vspace{3pt}

\Function{$\textsf{isSlashable}$}{$v_1, v_2$}
  \State \Return $v_1.\validator = v_2.\validator$
  \Statex \hspace{3em}$\land \;(\textsf{finalizeConflict}(v_1, v_2) \lor \textsf{finalizeConflict}(v_2, v_1))$
\EndFunction

\end{algorithmic}

\paragraph{Freshness as a structural invariant.}
If $v$ is recorded on chain~$L$ at state-height~$h$, then $v.\target \preceq L$ with $\sigma[v.\target].h = h$ (Remark~\ref{rem:fresh-equiv}).
Consequently any block justified or notarized at height~$h$ on some chain has $\geq 2n/3$ witnessing targets, each at state-height~$h$ on its own chain.

\section{Accountable safety}

\begin{lemma}[Height progression]\label{lem:heightprog}
$\textsf{processHeight}$ increments $h$ by at most~$1$ per invocation.
For any chain~$Y$ to reach height~$h' > h$, chain~$Y$ must have advanced through every intermediate height, each via some justification or notarization quorum.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The only assignments to $h$ are $h \gets h + 1$ in the justification and notarization branches, each guarded by the corresponding $\geq 2n/3$ quorum.
Reaching height~$h'$ from~$h$ requires $h' - h$ successive increments, each gated by such a quorum.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Checkpoint monotonicity]\label{lem:slotmono}
On any single chain: $s$, $h$, $h_j$, $h_n$, $s_h$, $J$, $N$, and $F$ are all monotonically non-decreasing along $\preceq$; and $F \preceq J \preceq N$ at all times.
Moreover $J$ and $h_j$ advance only on justification events (not on notarizations).
In particular, state-height is non-decreasing along any chain: for $B' \preceq B$ on a chain~$Y$, $\sigma[B'].h \leq \sigma[B].h$.
Also, $s_h$ is only updated on height-advances, and each update sets it to the slot of the advancing block; along any chain~$Y$, the interval of blocks at state-height exactly~$h$ is $\{B' \preceq Y : B'.\slot \geq \sigma[Y].s_h\}$ (whenever $\sigma[Y].h = h$).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$s$ is only updated in $\textsf{processSlot}$ via $s \gets s + 1$, hence non-decreasing; $h$ only increments by~$1$ (inside $\textsf{processHeight}$); $h_j$ (resp.~$h_n$) is set to the current~$h$ at each $J$- (resp.~$N$-)update, hence non-decreasing.
$F$ is only set via $F \gets J$, so $F \preceq J$ once we have $J$-monotonicity.
$s_h \gets L.\slot$ only inside the justification/notarization branches, paired with $h \gets h+1$; block slots strictly increase along~$\preceq$, so $s_h$ is non-decreasing.
The blocks at state-height exactly~$h$ on a chain~$Y$ (with $\sigma[Y].h = h$) are precisely the blocks on~$Y$ whose slot is in the interval starting at the slot $s_h$ of the advance that brought state-height to~$h$ and ending strictly before the slot of the next height-advance on~$Y$ (or the end of the chain if none); along $\preceq$ on~$Y$, state-height is non-decreasing.

\emph{Monotonicity of $N$, $J$.}
For any height-advance on~$Y$ with pre-state height~$h$, the advance sets $N \gets T$ (and also $J \gets T$ in the justification branch) for some firing target~$T$.
By Remark~\ref{rem:fresh-equiv}, $\sigma[T].h = h$: directly for $\just$ (each contributing vote is fresh with target~$T$), and for prefix-$\notar$ because every entry of $\mathsf{ntargets}$ on~$Y$ lies in the height-$h$ slot interval, so the prefix count can only first reach $2n/3$ inside that interval.
The previous value $N_{\text{old}}$ has $\sigma[N_{\text{old}}].h = h_n^{\text{old}} \leq h - 1$, so state-height monotonicity along~$Y$ gives $T \succ N_{\text{old}}$.
For $J$: each justification branch sets $J = N = T$ jointly, preserving the inductive $J \preceq N$ invariant; the notarization branch leaves $J$ unchanged and raises~$N$, also preserving it.
Combined with $T \succ N_{\text{old}} \succeq J_{\text{old}}$, both $N$ and $J$ advance strictly along~$\preceq$ at each justification, and $N$ strictly at each notarization.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Any chain past a finalized height contains the finalized block]\label{lem:mainsafety}
Unless $\geq n/3$ validators are slashable: if $C$ is finalized at height~$h$, then any chain~$Y$ that progresses past height~$h$ contains~$C$, i.e.\ $C \preceq Y$ at every state on~$Y$ with $\sigma.h > h$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Finality of~$C$ at height~$h$ gives a quorum $Q_F$ (size $\geq 2n/3$) of validators each signing a vote~$b$ with $b.\mathsf{finalize\_height} = h$ and $b.\mathsf{finalize\_target} = C$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:heightprog}, chain~$Y$ advanced past~$h$ via justification or prefix notarization.
In either case there is a height-$h$ quorum~$Q$ on~$Y$ where each $i \in Q$ signed a fresh vote~$a_i$ with $a_i.\height = h$ on~$Y$: for justification, directly from the $\just$ quorum; for notarization, from the $\mathsf{ntargets}$-update rule, which records only fresh votes, so each $i$ contributing to the prefix-notarization tally signed such an~$a_i$.

By quorum intersection, some $v \in Q_F \cap Q$ is not slashable.
$v$'s $Q_F$ vote $b$ has $b.\mathsf{finalize\_target} = C$ and $b.\mathsf{finalize\_height} = h$; $v$'s $Q$ vote $a$ has $a.\height = h$.
If $a.\target \neq C$, the pair $(a, b)$ triggers E1.
Since~$v$ is not slashable, $a.\target = C$; freshness of~$a$ on~$Y$ gives $C = a.\target \preceq Y$, which persists along extensions of~$Y$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finalized blocks form a chain]\label{lem:finchain}
Unless $\geq n/3$ validators are slashable: for any two finalized checkpoints $(C, h)$ and $(C', h')$, $C$ and $C'$ are compatible.
In particular, if $h \leq h'$ then $C \preceq C'$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
It suffices to show: if $h \leq h'$ then $C \preceq C'$; the case $h' \leq h$ is symmetric.

If $h = h'$: finality of~$C$ at height~$h$ gives a quorum $Q_F$ (size $\geq 2n/3$) each signing a vote with $\mathsf{finalize\_height} = h$ and $\mathsf{finalize\_target} = C$; justification of~$C'$ at height~$h$ on its chain gives a $\just$-quorum $Q'$ (size $\geq 2n/3$) each signing a vote with $\height = h$ and $\target = C'$.
By quorum intersection $|Q_F \cap Q'| \geq 2n/3 + 2n/3 - n = n/3$; each shared signer~$v$ either has $C = C'$ (so $C \preceq C'$ directly) or is slashable via E1 (finalize-target~$C$ at height~$h$ paired with a height-$h$ target~$C' \neq C$).
Unless $\geq n/3$ validators are slashable, the former must hold, giving $C \preceq C'$.

If $h < h'$: let~$Z$ be the chain on which~$C'$ was finalized, and consider~$Z$'s post-state at which the finalize-quorum for~$C'$ closed.
At that post-state, $\sigma[Z].h > h' > h$ and $C' \preceq Z$ with $\sigma[C'].h = h'$ (Remark~\ref{rem:fresh-equiv}, since $C'$ was justified at height~$h'$ on~$Z$).
By Lemma~\ref{lem:mainsafety} applied at the same post-state, $C \preceq Z$, and by Remark~\ref{rem:fresh-equiv} (applied on~$C$'s own chain, noting $C$ was justified at height~$h$), $\sigma[C].h = h$.
Both~$C$ and~$C'$ therefore lie on the linear chain ending at~$Z$, so they are comparable under~$\preceq$.
State-height is monotonically non-decreasing along~$\preceq$ on~$Z$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:slotmono}), and $\sigma[C].h = h < h' = \sigma[C'].h$, so the comparable pair $(C, C')$ must have $C$ strictly earlier along~$\preceq$: $C \prec C'$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Accountable safety]\label{thm:safety}
Unless $\geq n/3$ validators are slashable, no two conflicting blocks can be finalized.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from Lemma~\ref{lem:finchain}: for any two finalized $(C, h)$ and $(C', h')$, relabelling if necessary so that $h \leq h'$, we get $C \preceq C'$, so $C$ and $C'$ are compatible.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Ancestor notarizations are permitted]\label{rem:notarization-safety}
Because $\just$ votes also contribute to $\mathsf{ntargets}$ (Figure~\ref{alg:state-machine}), the prefix-notarization endpoint at height~$h$ on a chain containing a finalized block~$C$ may sit at some ancestor $T^* \preceq C$ on that chain.
This is consistent with safety, but means the per-chain invariants never rely on a strict $N \succeq C$ relation; the fork-choice store (Section~\ref{sec:store}) uses a greedy walk to handle this.
\end{remark}

\section{Fork-choice store}\label{sec:store}

A node may track multiple chains simultaneously.
The \emph{store} is the node-local data structure that mediates which chain the node follows.
It tracks two sets of checkpoint records: a primary set $C_N$ of \emph{notarized checkpoint records}, one per height advance (justification or prefix notarization), and a sub-layer $C_J \subseteq C_N$ of \emph{justified checkpoint records} --- those records whose block was justified on some chain.
The store's root $R_s$ is computed by a greedy walk from the store-finalized block~$F_s$, following the highest-height notarized record that strictly descends from the current walk position, with $C_J$-membership as a tiebreak at equal heights.
The walk guarantees $R_s \succeq F_s$ even when lower-height records for strict ancestors of finalized blocks remain in~$C_N$ (cf.\ Remark~\ref{rem:notarization-safety}).

\begin{definition}[Store]\label{def:store}
A node maintains a \emph{store}
\[
  \Sigma \;=\; (\sigma,\; R_s,\; F_s,\; \T,\; C_J,\; C_N),
\]
where:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\sigma$ is the \emph{state map}, mapping each accepted block to its per-chain post-state (Section~1);
  \item $R_s$ is the \emph{store root}, used as the root of the fork-choice subtree;
  \item $F_s$ is the \emph{store-finalized block};
  \item $\T$ is the \emph{block tree}, the set of accepted blocks;
  \item $C_N$ is the set of processed \emph{notarized checkpoint records} $(X, h, s)$, one per post-state of an accepted block: $X = \sigma[B].N$, $h = \sigma[B].h_n$, $s = X.\slot$;
  \item $C_J \subseteq C_N$ is the set of processed \emph{justified checkpoint records}: the subset of $C_N$ whose block was justified (not merely prefix-notarized) at height~$h$ on some chain.
\end{itemize}
We use the capital letter~$\Sigma$ for the store to distinguish it from the per-chain state~$\sigma$: $\Sigma$ aggregates a collection of per-chain states (one per accepted block) together with the fork-choice bookkeeping $(R_s, F_s, \T, C_J, C_N)$; $\sigma[B]$ refers to a single per-chain post-state.
Where unambiguous we write $\Sigma.R_s$, $\Sigma.F_s$, $\Sigma.\T$, $\Sigma.C_J$, $\Sigma.C_N$, $\Sigma.\sigma$ for the corresponding fields.
The genesis store has $R_s = F_s = \genesis$, $\T = \{\genesis\}$, $C_J = C_N = \{(\genesis, 0, 0)\}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Store-finalized invariant]\label{rem:fs-invariant}
At all times, $F_s$ is either~$\genesis$ or a block that was finalized on some chain (see below: $F_s$ is updated only to values~$F_B$ that appear as~$\sigma[B].F$ in a post-state, and $\sigma[B].F$ is set only by the finality branch of $\textsf{processHeight}$).
Moreover, the tuple $(F_s, h_{F_s}, F_s.\slot) \in C_J$ where $h_{F_s}$ is the height at which~$F_s$ was finalized: for the genesis case, $(\genesis, 0, 0)$ is inserted at initialization; for the finalized case, whenever $F_s \gets F_B$, some ancestor~$B'$ of~$B$ (possibly~$B$ itself) had post-state $(J_{B'}, h_j^{B'}) = (F_B, h_{F_s})$ and its processing inserted $(F_B, h_{F_s}, F_B.\slot)$ into~$C_J$.
\end{remark}

\emph{Accepting a block.}
A block~$B$ is accepted only if its parent is in~$\Sigma.\T$ and $\Sigma.F_s \preceq B$.
Starting from the parent's post-state, we first invoke $\textsf{processSlot}$ once for each slot from $\Sigma.\sigma[B.\parent].s + 1$ up to $B.\slot$ (the last invocation brings~$\sigma.s$ to~$B.\slot$ and fires any height advances enabled by the accumulated tally), then invoke $\textsf{processBlock}(\cdot, B)$ to fold in~$B$'s votes.
The resulting post-state is stored as $\Sigma.\sigma[B]$; we insert the tuple $(N_B, h_n^B, N_B.\slot)$ into~$\Sigma.C_N$ and $(J_B, h_B, J_B.\slot)$ into~$\Sigma.C_J$ (where subscripts denote the corresponding fields of $\Sigma.\sigma[B]$), recompute $\Sigma.R_s$, and update $\Sigma.F_s$.
A justification fires $(J, N) \gets (T, T)$ and $(h_j, h_n) \gets (h, h)$ jointly, preserving $C_J \subseteq C_N$.

\emph{Store root (greedy walk).}
$\Sigma.R_s$ is computed by $\textsf{computeRoot}(\Sigma)$ (Figure~\ref{alg:store}): starting from~$\Sigma.F_s$, at each step pick the tuple $(X, h, s) \in \Sigma.C_N$ with $X$ a strict descendant of the current walk position maximizing the lex key $\bigl(h,\; \mathbf{1}[(X, h, s) \in \Sigma.C_J],\; s,\; \mathsf{hash}(X)\bigr)$, and move to~$X$.
(In this section~$X$ denotes both the running walk position and a cert-block in a tuple $(X, h, s)$; the meaning is always clear from context.)
The walk terminates when no strict descendant exists in~$\Sigma.C_N$.
The bit after~$h$ makes a $C_J$-tuple strictly beat any $C_N \setminus C_J$-tuple at the same height.
Since each step moves to a strict descendant of~$\Sigma.F_s$, we have $\Sigma.R_s \succeq \Sigma.F_s$ by construction; the argmax is unique (hashes are unique per block) and the walk terminates in at most $|\Sigma.C_N|$ steps.

\emph{Updating finalized.}
If the post-state's $F_B \succ \Sigma.F_s$ and $F_B \preceq \Sigma.R_s$, then $\Sigma.F_s \gets F_B$.

\paragraph{Leaf-only variant.}
Define $C_N^{\mathrm{leaf}} \subseteq \Sigma.C_N$ to be the subset of tuples $(X, h, s)$ contributed by the post-state of a \emph{leaf} block of~$\Sigma.\T$ (a block with no processed descendant), and analogously $C_J^{\mathrm{leaf}} \subseteq \Sigma.C_J$.
Let $\textsf{computeRootLeaf}(\Sigma)$ be $\textsf{computeRoot}$ run over $(C_N^{\mathrm{leaf}}, C_J^{\mathrm{leaf}})$.

\begin{theorem}[Leaf-only equivalence]\label{thm:leafequiv}
$\textsf{computeRootLeaf}(\Sigma)$ returns the same block as $\textsf{computeRoot}(\Sigma)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix any non-leaf processed block~$B_i$ with post-state tuple $\tau_i = (X_i, h_i, X_i.\slot) \in C_N$.
Since~$B_i$ is non-leaf, pick any processed leaf $B_\ell$ descending from~$B_i$ (the block tree~$\T$ is finite, so some leaf exists on each branch through~$B_i$); $B_\ell$ contributes a tuple $\tau_\ell = (X_\ell, h_\ell, X_\ell.\slot) \in C_N^{\mathrm{leaf}}$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:slotmono} applied along the chain from~$B_i$ to~$B_\ell$, both $h_n$ and~$N$ are monotone non-decreasing, so $h_\ell \geq h_i$ and $X_\ell \succeq X_i$.
Whenever $h_\ell = h_i$, $h_n$-monotonicity combined with the state-machine invariant that $N$ advances only jointly with $h_n \gets h_n + 1$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:slotmono}) forces $X_\ell = X_i$; hence $\tau_\ell = \tau_i$ and $\tau_i \in C_N^{\mathrm{leaf}}$ directly.
Whenever $h_\ell > h_i$, $\tau_\ell$'s key strictly dominates $\tau_i$'s key by the leading height coordinate, regardless of the $C_J$-bit.

Now run both walks in lockstep from $X^* = F_s$.
At each step with current position~$X^*$, the full walk's lex-max over~$C_N$ is attained at some tuple~$\tau^\star$.
If $\tau^\star$ is itself a leaf tuple, the leaf walk makes the same choice.
If $\tau^\star = \tau_i$ for some non-leaf~$B_i$, then its leaf replacement~$\tau_\ell$ is itself a strict-descendant candidate in~$C_N$ with key at least as large as~$\tau_i$'s, so the lex-max already selects a tuple whose key equals $\tau_\ell$'s.
This forces $h_\ell = h_i$ (otherwise $\tau_\ell$'s key strictly dominates $\tau_i$'s, yet both are candidates), whence $\tau_\ell = \tau_i \in C_N^{\mathrm{leaf}}$: $\tau^\star$ coincides with a leaf tuple after all, and the leaf walk selects the same tuple.
By induction both walks terminate at the same block.
\end{proof}

The leaf-only variant bounds the walk's candidate pool by the number of leaves of~$\T$.

\subsection{Store invariants and safety}

\subsubsection*{Unconditional invariants}

\begin{theorem}[$F_s \preceq R_s$ unconditionally]\label{thm:fleqr}
The store maintains $F_s \preceq R_s$ at all times.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Initially $F_s = R_s = \genesis$.
$\textsf{computeRoot}$ starts at $X^* = F_s$ and only moves to strict descendants of the current walk position, so $R_s \succeq F_s$ holds just after $\textsf{computeRoot}$.
$\textsf{updateFinalized}$ sets $F_s$ only to values $F_B \preceq R_s$ (explicit guard), preserving the invariant.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Local irreversibility of finality]\label{thm:finperm}
Once a node sets $F_s = F$, $F_s$ descends from~$F$ at all future times.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$F_s$ is only updated in $\textsf{updateFinalized}$, which requires $F_B \succ F_s$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Fork-choice consistency]\label{thm:fcconsistency}
Once a node sets $F_s = F$, $\textsf{getHead}$ returns a block descending from~$F$ at all future times.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Theorems~\ref{thm:finperm} and~\ref{thm:fleqr}, $F \preceq F_s \preceq R_s$, and $\textsf{getHead}$ returns a descendant of~$R_s$.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection*{Further properties under $f < n/3$}

The key tool for the remaining store theorems is Lemma~\ref{lem:certchain}: every notarized checkpoint record whose height is at least that of a finalized block~$C$ is compatible with~$C$, and strictly descends from~$C$ when its height strictly exceeds that of~$C$.

\begin{lemma}[Checkpoint chain]\label{lem:certchain}
Unless $\geq n/3$ validators are slashable: if~$C$ is finalized at height~$h_F$ and $(X, h', s) \in C_N$ with $h' \geq h_F$, then $C$ and~$X$ are compatible; moreover, if $h' > h_F$ then $C \prec X$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $h_F = 0$ then $C = \genesis$ and compatibility is trivial; assume $h_F \geq 1$, so $h' \geq 1$ and the tuple~$(X, h', s)$ is not the initial genesis tuple.
Thus $(X, h', s)$ was inserted from the post-state of some processed block~$B$ with $(\sigma[B].N, \sigma[B].h_n) = (X, h')$; let~$Y$ denote the chain ending at~$B$, so $X \preceq Y$.
Every update to~$h_n$ is paired with $\sigma.h \gets \sigma.h + 1$, and subsequent advances only increase~$\sigma.h$, so $\sigma[B].h \geq h' + 1 > h_F$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:mainsafety} applied at~$B$'s post-state on~$Y$, $C \preceq Y$; combined with $X \preceq Y$, this gives $C \sim X$.

\emph{Strict descent when $h' > h_F$.}
By Remark~\ref{rem:fresh-equiv}, $\sigma[X].h = h'$ and $\sigma[C].h = h_F$.
State-height is non-decreasing along~$\preceq$ on~$Y$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:slotmono}); with $\sigma[C].h = h_F < h' = \sigma[X].h$ and both $C, X \preceq Y$, the comparable pair is ordered $C \prec X$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Upgrade property of the walk]\label{lem:upgrade}
Unless $\geq n/3$ validators are slashable: if~$F$ is finalized at height~$h_F$, $(F, h_F, F.\slot) \in C_J$, and $F_s \prec F$, then $\textsf{computeRoot}$ produces $R_s \succeq F$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The walk starts at $X^* = F_s \prec F$.
Since $(F, h_F, F.\slot) \in C_J \subseteq C_N$ is a strict-descendant candidate (because $F_s \prec F$ by hypothesis), the loop's first step picks some tuple $(X, h, s)$ with key $\geq (h_F, 1, F.\slot, \mathsf{hash}(F))$, so $h \geq h_F$.

If $h > h_F$, Lemma~\ref{lem:certchain} (strict descent) gives $F \prec X$.

If $h = h_F$, Lemma~\ref{lem:certchain} (compatibility) gives $F \sim X$, so $F \preceq X$ or $X \prec F$.
With $h = h_F$, the key lower bound $(h_F, 1, F.\slot, \mathsf{hash}(F))$ forces the bit coordinate to be~$1$, so $(X, h, s) \in C_J$ and the key is exactly $(h_F, 1, X.\slot, \mathsf{hash}(X))$; comparing with the lower bound yields $X.\slot \geq F.\slot$.
Case $X \prec F$ is then excluded: blocks on $F$'s chain have strictly increasing slots (Definition~\ref{def:block}), so $X \prec F$ would force $X.\slot < F.\slot$, contrary to the derived $X.\slot \geq F.\slot$.
Hence $F \preceq X$.

Either way $X \succeq F$, and subsequent steps move to strict descendants of~$X$, so $R_s \succeq F$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Local acceptance of finality updates]\label{thm:finlive}
Unless $\geq n/3$ validators are slashable: if a block~$B$ is processed by $\textsf{onBlock}$ and its post-state has finalized block~$F_B$, then after processing $F_s \succeq F_B$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $F_s \succeq F_B$ already, the guard $F_B \succ F_s$ fails and $F_s \succeq F_B$ is maintained.
Otherwise, $F_s$ is itself a finalized block or $\genesis$ (Remark~\ref{rem:fs-invariant}), so by accountable safety (Theorem~\ref{thm:safety}) $F_s$ and $F_B$ are compatible, giving $F_s \prec F_B$.
We show $F_B \preceq R_s$ after $\textsf{computeRoot}$, so the $\textsf{updateFinalized}$ guard passes.

Let~$h_F$ be the height at which~$F_B$ was finalized.
For~$B$'s post-state to have $F_B$ finalized, some ancestor~$B'$ of~$B$ (possibly~$B$ itself) had post-state $(J_{B'}, h_j^{B'}) = (F_B, h_F)$.
Blocks are processed parent-first, so $B'$ has been processed and $(F_B, h_F, F_B.\slot) \in C_J$.

Since $F_s \prec F_B$ and $(F_B, h_F, F_B.\slot) \in C_J$, Lemma~\ref{lem:upgrade} gives $R_s \succeq F_B$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Lock-in]\label{thm:lockin}
Unless $\geq n/3$ validators are slashable: if block~$F$ is finalized at height~$h$ on any chain, and some block whose post-state has $(F, h)$ as its justified checkpoint has been processed by the node, then $R_s \succeq F$ at all future times, and $\textsf{getHead}$ always returns a descendant of~$F$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Processing the block adds $(F, h, F.\slot)$ to~$C_J$; the candidate sets are monotone so this holds forever.
Fix any future moment.
If $F \preceq F_s$: Theorem~\ref{thm:fleqr} gives $R_s \succeq F_s \succeq F$.
If $F \npreceq F_s$: by Theorem~\ref{thm:finperm}, Remark~\ref{rem:fs-invariant} (so $F_s$ is finalized or $\genesis$), and Lemma~\ref{lem:finchain}, $F_s$ and~$F$ are compatible, so $F_s \prec F$; by Lemma~\ref{lem:upgrade}, $R_s \succeq F$.
In both cases $\textsf{getHead}$ (descending from~$R_s$) returns a descendant of~$F$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Order independence]\label{thm:orderindep}
Unless $\geq n/3$ validators are slashable: the store state after processing a set of blocks depends only on which blocks have been processed, not on their order.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\sigma$ maps each block to its post-state, which is a function of the block alone (given its parent's state).
Since each block is processed at most once and the post-state is parent-determined, $\sigma$ depends only on the processed set.

$C_J$ and $C_N$ are sets; inserting the same tuple twice is a no-op, and $C_J \subseteq C_N$ is preserved under inserts.
Both sets are recomputed deterministically from~$\sigma$, so they depend only on the processed set.

$R_s$ is the output of $\textsf{computeRoot}$, a deterministic function of $(C_N, C_J, F_s)$: at each step of the walk, the key $(h, \mathbf{1}[(X,h,s) \in C_J], s, \mathsf{hash}(X))$ is totally ordered (components are integers or unique hashes), so the argmax is a deterministic function of $C_N$ and the current walk position; the walk terminates after at most $|C_N|$ steps.

For $F_s$: by Lemma~\ref{lem:finchain}, the values $F_B$ across all post-states form a chain, with unique maximum $F_{\max}$.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:finlive}, after processing the block carrying $F_{\max}$ the guard passes and $F_s \gets F_{\max}$.
Since $F_s$ can only move to values $\preceq F_{\max}$ (every $F_B$ in a post-state has $F_B \preceq F_{\max}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:finchain}), the final value is $F_{\max}$ regardless of order.
Thus $R_s$, computed from the final $F_s$ and $C_N$ (with $C_J$ feeding the tiebreak bit), is also order-independent.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Cross-node agreement]\label{cor:agreement}
Unless $\geq n/3$ validators are slashable: two nodes that have processed the same set of blocks have the same $(R_s, F_s, C_J, C_N)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from Theorem~\ref{thm:orderindep}.
\end{proof}

\section{Inactivity leak}

The \emph{canonical chain}~$X$ is the chain selected by $\textsf{getHead}(\Sigma)$.
The leak section extends the per-chain state machine with a penalty counter $\sigma.\mathsf{penalties}[1..n]$ and a new transition function $\textsf{processInactivityPenalties}$ invoked each slot by an extended $\textsf{processSlot}$ (Figure~\ref{alg:leak-processslot}).
Tightness (Theorem~\ref{thm:tightness}) is purely state-logic: it reads $\sigma.\mathsf{penalties}$ on the canonical chain and bounds its cumulative increment over periods of non-finality, making no assumption about message delivery, validator honesty, or the size of the slashable set.
Fairness (Theorem~\ref{thm:fairness}) is a single-validator claim from its local perspective: the vote an honest~$i$ produces per the honest rule (Definition~\ref{def:voting-rule}) can be included on any extension of~$i$'s local canonical chain still at the same state-height, and once included exempts~$i$ from Layer~1 penalties at that state-height.
Layer-2 exemption is out of scope for fairness: avoiding Layer-2 penalties depends on synchrony (timely quorum formation on a common target) and has nothing to do with locking --- height advance clears $\mathsf{jtargets}$ and $\mathsf{ntargets}$ at the local state, so Layer-2 conditions at the next slot are independent of what~$i$ voted for at the previous height.

\subsection{Penalty units}

Penalties are recorded \emph{inside} the state machine: the leak section extends the per-chain state with a per-validator penalty counter, and adds a new transition function $\textsf{processInactivityPenalties}$ that updates it.
The base state machine of Section~1 (Figure~\ref{alg:state-machine}) is \emph{unchanged}; what follows is a state \emph{extension} active in the leak section only.

\paragraph{State extension.}
We augment the per-chain state tuple with a new field
\[
  \sigma.\mathsf{penalties}[1..n],
\]
where $\sigma.\mathsf{penalties}[i] \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$ is a non-negative counter for validator~$i$.
The genesis value is $\sigma.\mathsf{penalties}[i] = 0$ for every~$i$.
No other field is added or modified.

\paragraph{Extended $\textsf{processSlot}$.}
In the leak section we replace $\textsf{processSlot}$ (Figure~\ref{alg:state-machine}) with the version in Figure~\ref{alg:leak-processslot}, which additionally invokes $\textsf{processInactivityPenalties}$ on the pre- and post-height-advance snapshots.
$\textsf{processBlock}$, $\textsf{processHeight}$, and $\textsf{processVote}$ are unchanged from the base state machine, and the store of Section~\ref{sec:store} then calls the extended $\textsf{processSlot}$ in place of the base one.

    \EndFor
  \EndIf
  \If{$\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.J = \sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.J$} \Comment{Layer 2 target: justification did \emph{not} advance}
    \State $T^* \gets \arg\max_{T' \in \{\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.\mathsf{jtargets}[i] \,:\, \sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.\mathsf{jtargets}[i] \neq \bot\}} |\{i : \sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.\mathsf{jtargets}[i] = T'\}|$ \Comment{$T^* \gets \bot$ if the set is empty; ties broken deterministically}
    \For{each $i \in \{1, \ldots, n\}$}
      \If{$\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.\mathsf{jtargets}[i] \neq T^*$ \textbf{or} $\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.\mathsf{jtargets}[i] = \bot$}
        \State $\sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.\mathsf{penalties}[i] \gets \sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.\mathsf{penalties}[i] + 1$
      \EndIf
    \EndFor
  \EndIf
  \If{$\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.F = \sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.F$ \textbf{and} $\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.F \prec \sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.J$} \Comment{Layer 2 finalize: $F$ did not advance, pending finality}
    \For{each $i \in \{1, \ldots, n\}$}
      \If{$i \notin \sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.P$}
        \State $\sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.\mathsf{penalties}[i] \gets \sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.\mathsf{penalties}[i] + 1$
      \EndIf
    \EndFor
  \EndIf
  \State \Return $\sigma_{\mathrm{post}}$
\EndFunction

\end{algorithmic}
\end{boxedminipage}
\end{center}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Reading Figure~\ref{alg:leak-processslot}.}
Figure~\ref{alg:leak-processslot} is the authoritative specification.
Informally, $\textsf{processInactivityPenalties}$ checks three independent state-logic conditions; each may or may not fire on a given slot, and more than one may fire together.
\emph{Layer~1 (stall, $\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.h = \sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.h$)} fires when the slot failed to advance height; it bumps every~$i$ with $\sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.\mathsf{ntargets}[i] = \bot$, capturing failure to cast a fresh vote at the current height (note $\mathsf{ntargets}$ is not reset at a stall).
\emph{Layer~2 target (justification did not advance, $\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.J = \sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.J$)} fires when justification did \emph{not} fire this slot; $T^*$ is the plurality target across $\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.\mathsf{jtargets}$ (with a deterministic tiebreak), or $\bot$ if no~$\just$ votes have been registered, and every~$i$ with $\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.\mathsf{jtargets}[i] \neq T^*$ or $\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.\mathsf{jtargets}[i] = \bot$ is bumped (``did not $\just$-vote for the plurality target, or did not $\just$-vote at all''). Whenever the justification branch of $\textsf{processHeight}$ fires, its quorum condition gives $|\{i : \sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.\mathsf{jtargets}[i] = T\}| \geq 2n/3$ and the branch sets $\sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.J \gets T \neq \sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.J$ (justification strictly advances~$J$ along $\preceq$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:slotmono}), so Layer~2 target is automatically skipped in the good case. When the branch does not fire, $T^*$ has count $< 2n/3$ on $\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.\mathsf{jtargets}$ (taking the count as~$0$ when $T^* = \bot$), so $|\{i : \sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.\mathsf{jtargets}[i] \neq T^* \vee \sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.\mathsf{jtargets}[i] = \bot\}| > n/3$; in particular, when $T^* = \bot$ all~$n$ validators are bumped.
\emph{Layer~2 finalize (pending finality did not close, $\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.F = \sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.F$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.F \prec \sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.J$)} fires when there was a pending finality gap at the start of the slot and~$F$ did not advance during the slot; every $i \notin \sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.P$ is bumped (``did not commit to finalize the pending justified block'').
A slot on which the finality branch fires has $\sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.F = \sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.J \succ \sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.F$, so Layer~2 finalize is automatically skipped.
A vote~$v$ by~$i$ processed at the current slot on the canonical chain~$X$ at state-height $h_c = \sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.h$ bumps $\sigma.\mathsf{ntargets}[i]$ on~$X$ iff $v$ is \emph{canonical-fresh}, i.e.
\[
  v.\height = h_c \;\wedge\; v.\target \preceq X \;\wedge\; v.\target.\slot \geq \sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.s_h,
\]
equivalently $\sigma[v.\target].h = h_c$ with $v.\target \preceq X$ (Remark~\ref{rem:fresh-equiv}).

\subsection{Leak tightness}

\begin{theorem}[Amortized $n/3$ penalty units per slot]\label{thm:tightness}
During any period of non-finality on the canonical chain, the total penalty units assessed across all validators by $\textsf{processInactivityPenalties}$ is at least $n/3$ per slot on average.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix the canonical chain $X = \textsf{getHead}(\Sigma)$, and write $\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}^{(t)}, \sigma_{\mathrm{post}}^{(t)}$ for the pre/post-$\textsf{processHeight}$ snapshots on~$X$ at slot~$t$.
By hypothesis, $\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}^{(t)}.F = \sigma_{\mathrm{post}}^{(t)}.F$ at every slot~$t$ of the period (finality does not advance on~$X$).
We classify each slot of the period into three cases and show the total penalty charged is $> n/3$ per slot, after a simple attribution across cases.

\emph{Stall slot} ($\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.h = \sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.h$).
Neither height-advance branch of $\textsf{processHeight}$ fired, so the prefix-notarization walk summed to $< 2n/3$ across $\sigma.L$ down to genesis.
Every entry with $\sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.\mathsf{ntargets}[i] \neq \bot$ is an ancestor of $\sigma.L$ by freshness (Remark~\ref{rem:fresh-equiv}), so the walk visits it exactly once; hence $|\{i : \sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.\mathsf{ntargets}[i] \neq \bot\}| < 2n/3$, and the Layer~1 stall penalty charges $> n/3$ validators.

\emph{Advance slot, $J$ does not update} ($\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.h < \sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.h$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.J = \sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.J$).
This is a notarization-only advance.
Its hypothesis is exactly the Layer~2 target guard, so Layer~2 target fires with plurality target~$T^*$ (possibly~$\bot$).
The justification branch of $\textsf{processHeight}$ strictly advances~$J$ whenever it fires (Lemma~\ref{lem:slotmono}), so $\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.J = \sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.J$ forces that branch not to fire this slot, giving $|\{i : \sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.\mathsf{jtargets}[i] = T^*\}| < 2n/3$ (with the convention that the count is~$0$ when $T^* = \bot$).
Hence $|\{i : \sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.\mathsf{jtargets}[i] \neq T^* \vee \sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.\mathsf{jtargets}[i] = \bot\}| > n/3$, each charged~$1$ by Layer~2 target.

\emph{Advance slot, $J$ updates} ($\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.h < \sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.h$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.J \neq \sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.J$).
Call such a slot a \emph{checkpoint-update} slot.
The Layer~2 target penalty may be~$0$ (if $\geq 2n/3$ validators had $\mathsf{jtargets}[i] = \sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.J$).
However, since finality does not advance inside the period, we must have $\sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.F \prec \sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.J$ at the end of such a slot: pending finalization opens (or persists).
We attribute to this slot the Layer~2 finalize penalty of the \emph{next} slot $t + 1$ in the period on which $\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}^{(t+1)}.F \prec \sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}^{(t+1)}.J$ and the Layer~2 finalize branch fires.
At that slot, the finality branch did not fire (otherwise $F$ would advance, exiting the period), so $|\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}^{(t+1)}.P| < 2n/3$ and Layer~2 finalize charges $> n/3$ validators.

\emph{Attribution is well-defined and 1-to-1.}
The period is a maximal run of non-finality-advancing slots on~$X$.
At a checkpoint-update slot~$t$ the post-state has $\sigma_{\mathrm{post}}^{(t)}.F \prec \sigma_{\mathrm{post}}^{(t)}.J$, opening a pending-finality window that closes only when finality fires, which (by the period hypothesis) cannot happen before the period ends.
Thus every slot $t' > t$ in the period either carries $\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}^{(t')}.F \prec \sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}^{(t')}.J$ until a strictly later checkpoint-update replaces~$J$, and the Layer~2 finalize guard holds at each such~$t'$.
Distinct checkpoint-update slots $t_1 < t_2 < \cdots$ produce disjoint successor windows (each ending at the next checkpoint-update or at the period boundary), and the window starting at~$t_j$ contains at least one slot $t_j + 1, \ldots, t_{j+1}$ (or $t_j + 1, \ldots, t_1$) on which Layer~2 finalize fires, so we can injectively assign to each checkpoint-update slot a distinct Layer~2 finalize event within its own window, non-overlapping with any target-check attribution above.
The last checkpoint-update slot before the period ends is handled identically: the period, being maximal, must end either because the run of slots ends or because finality fires, and in the latter case the slot just before finality firing is a Layer~2 finalize slot (finality did not fire yet at that slot's $\textsf{processHeight}$ call, so $\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.P$ was sub-quorum) that can serve as the attributed slot; in the former case we simply exclude the last checkpoint-update from the tally (at most one slot of the period, a $O(1)$ correction negligible in the per-slot average).

In every case, each slot of the period is attributed $> n/3$ penalty units, either directly (stall, $J$-unchanged advance, or Layer~2 finalize slots serving their own position) or via attribution from a checkpoint-update slot.
Summing and dividing by the period length gives an average $> n/3$ penalty units per slot, whence $\geq n/3$ on average.
The bound is purely state-logic: the proof reads $\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{post}}$ on the canonical chain and counts penalty increments by direct inspection of $\textsf{processInactivityPenalties}$, with no hypothesis on synchrony, on the honest voting rule, or on the size of the slashable set.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Honest voting rule}

We state the honest rule explicitly, as it underpins the leak fairness proof.

\begin{definition}[Honest voting rule]\label{def:voting-rule}
Let~$X = \textsf{getHead}(\Sigma)$ be the canonical chain head, $\sigma[X]$ its post-state, $h_c = \sigma[X].h$ the current canonical height, and $h_j = \sigma[X].h_j$ the canonical justified height.
Write $\mathsf{currentSlot}$ for the node's protocol time at the voting opportunity: the common slot counter shared across chains, satisfying $\mathsf{currentSlot} = \sigma[X].s \geq \sigma[X].s_h$.
Let $T$ denote a target at height~$h_c$: any ancestor of~$X$ with $\sigma[T].h = h_c$. In particular, $X$ itself is a valid choice.

Let $J = \sigma[X].J$ denote the canonical justified block (paired with $h_j$).

\emph{Target selection.}
Let $T_c$ denote the target of~$i$'s vote at the current canonical height~$h_c$: if $i$ has previously cast a vote at~$h_c$ with target $T_{\mathrm{prior}}$, then $T_c = T_{\mathrm{prior}}$; otherwise $T_c = T$ (the canonical target).
That is,~$i$ reuses the target of its first vote at~$h_c$, and otherwise picks the canonical target.

A validator~$i$ acts at each voting opportunity at the current canonical height~$h_c$ as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(R1)] If $i$ has not yet cast any vote at height~$h_c$: cast a $\just$ vote
  \[ v = (i,\; h_c,\; \mathsf{currentSlot},\; T,\; \just,\; f_h,\; f_t) \]
  with target~$T$ (the canonical target; target selection gives $T_c = T$ since no prior vote at~$h_c$ exists) and finalize-fields
  \[
    (f_h,\, f_t) \;=\;
    \begin{cases}
      (h_j,\, J) & \parbox[t]{0.55\textwidth}{\raggedright if $i$'s prior vote at height~$h_j$ (if any) had target~$J$ (equivalently, $i$ is not pinned at~$h_j$ to a target other than~$J$);}\\[1.2em]
      (\bot,\, \bot) & \text{otherwise.}
    \end{cases}
  \]
  \item[(R2)] Else ($i$ has already cast a vote at height~$h_c$): cast a $\notar$ vote
  \[ v = (i,\; h_c,\; \mathsf{currentSlot},\; T_c,\; \notar,\; \bot,\; \bot) \]
  with target $T_c = T_{\mathrm{prior}}$ (the target of~$i$'s first vote at~$h_c$).
\end{enumerate}
The values $T$, $J$, and~$h_j$ used here are those of the current canonical state $\sigma[X]$ at the time of the voting opportunity.
Only (R1) produces a $\just$ vote and fires at most once per height, so an honest validator casts at most one $\just$ vote per height, with a single target; target selection then forces all of~$i$'s votes at any fixed height to share that target.
Because (R1)/(R2) are evaluated against the \emph{current} canonical height~$h_c$, voting at~$h_c$ stops naturally once canonical advances past~$h_c$.
\end{definition}

Under synchrony, $f < n/3$, and deterministic $\textsf{getHead}$, honest validators share the same canonical state (Corollary~\ref{cor:agreement}) and agree on~$T$, so the first-slot $\just$ quorum for~$T$ forms at~$h_c$ and justification fires.
Rule~(R2) is the \emph{resubmission} fallback for later slots at the same canonical height, when the (R1) $\just$ quorum did not form on canonical (heterogeneous (R1) targets across views): the $\notar$ vote's prefix counting may still notarize a common ancestor; its target~$T_{\mathrm{prior}}$ is fixed by target selection to the target of~$i$'s (R1) vote at~$h_c$, keeping all of~$i$'s height-$h_c$ votes consistent.
In particular, (R2) fires at every slot while canonical remains at~$h_c$: a validator that committed to finalize at~$h_c$ (via the finalize-fields of its (R1) vote) keeps resubmitting a fresh $\notar$ vote for $T_{\mathrm{prior}}$ each slot, so its $\mathsf{ntargets}$ entry stays populated on every chain that keeps processing its new votes.
This means processing old votes is never necessary for progress: validators re-emit their vote every slot while at height~$h_c$, and old votes can be discarded.
Each (R1) vote optionally commits to finalize the canonical justified block~$J$ at height~$h_j$ via $(\mathsf{finalize\_height},\, \mathsf{finalize\_target}) = (h_j,\, J)$, which places the validator in~$P$ once the $(J, h_j)$-state is processed.
The (R1) finalize-field conditional --- ``$i$'s prior vote at height~$h_j$ (if any) had target~$J$'' --- keeps the rule E1-safe: any prior target at height~$h_j$ already matches the $\mathsf{finalize\_target}$ being attached.

\paragraph{E1 safety of the rule.}
The rule is E1-safe: Lemma~\ref{lem:honest-e1-safety} records that honest validators never trigger E1 slashing.
Honest per-height $\just$-uniqueness is also ensured by the rule: only (R1) produces a $\just$ vote, and (R1) fires at most once per $h_c$ since the validator marks itself as having voted.

\begin{lemma}[Honest E1 safety]\label{lem:honest-e1-safety}
Under synchrony, $f < n/3$, and all honest validators following Definition~\ref{def:voting-rule}, an honest~$i$ never produces a vote-pair $(v_1, v_2)$ (possibly $v_1 = v_2$) satisfying $v_2.\mathsf{finalize\_target} \neq \bot$, $v_1.\height = v_2.\mathsf{finalize\_height}$, and $v_1.\target \neq v_2.\mathsf{finalize\_target}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Only (R1) produces a finalize-bearing vote, with $(f_h, f_t) = (h_j^{(v_2)}, J^{(v_2)})$ from the canonical state at~$v_2$'s firing.
We show every vote~$v_1$ by~$i$ with $v_1.\height = h_j^{(v_2)}$ has $v_1.\target = J^{(v_2)}$, by cases on the temporal relationship between~$v_1$ and~$v_2$.

\emph{Case~A ($v_1$ cast before~$v_2$).}
The (R1) finalize-field conditional at~$v_2$ attached $(h_j^{(v_2)}, J^{(v_2)})$, which fires only when~$i$'s prior vote at height~$h_j^{(v_2)}$ (if any) had target~$J^{(v_2)}$; by target selection, all of~$i$'s votes at~$h_j^{(v_2)}$ share that target, so $v_1.\target = J^{(v_2)}$.

\emph{Case~B ($v_1$ cast after~$v_2$).}
Under the hypotheses, each canonical advance is a justification firing witnessed by the $> 2n/3$ honest (R1) quorum (Corollary~\ref{cor:agreement}), so canonical state-height is monotone over time; since $v_2$ was cast at canonical state-height $h_c^{(v_2)} > h_j^{(v_2)}$, canonical cannot return to height $h_j^{(v_2)}$ for a later $v_1$, so this case is vacuous.

\emph{Case~C ($v_1 = v_2$).}
Also vacuous, since $v_2.\height = h_c^{(v_2)} \neq h_j^{(v_2)} = v_2.\mathsf{finalize\_height}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of Lemma~\ref{lem:honest-e1-safety}]\label{rem:honest-e1-scope}
All three hypotheses are load-bearing for Case~B.
A validator that wishes not to rely on them may always set $(f_h, f_t) = (\bot, \bot)$ at (R1), trivially avoiding E1 at the cost of not contributing to finalization.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Leak fairness}

Fairness is stated from a single honest validator's \emph{local} perspective: we fix one validator~$i$, its local store~$\Sigma$, and the canonical chain $X = \textsf{getHead}(\Sigma)$ it currently sees.
The claim is that the vote~$v$ produced by the honest rule (Definition~\ref{def:voting-rule}) at the current slot, once included on any extension of~$X$ that is still at the same state-height, exempts~$i$ from Layer~1 penalties at that state-height on that extension.
Layer-2 exemption is out of scope: avoiding Layer-2 target (matching the plurality target when justification does not advance) and Layer-2 finalize (pending finality closing) depends on synchrony --- timely quorum formation on a common target and timely propagation of the finalize-fields --- rather than on any local property of~$i$.
In particular, the freshness-reset of $\mathsf{jtargets}$ and $\mathsf{ntargets}$ at each height advance (Figure~\ref{alg:state-machine}) means that the Layer-2 state at slot~$s+1$ is independent of~$i$'s votes cast at heights strictly below the current height, so there is no ``lock'' issue obstructing fairness.

\begin{theorem}[Layer~1 leak fairness]\label{thm:fairness}
Let~$i$ be an honest validator following Definition~\ref{def:voting-rule}, $\Sigma$ its local store, $X = \textsf{getHead}(\Sigma)$ the canonical chain it sees, and $h_c = \Sigma.\sigma[X].h$ the current canonical state-height.
Let $v$ be the vote~$i$ produces at the current slot.
Let $\Sigma'$ be any store obtained by processing a set of additional blocks and votes on top of~$\Sigma$ such that $X' = \textsf{getHead}(\Sigma')$ is an extension of~$X$ with $\Sigma'.\sigma[X'].h = h_c$ (i.e., canonical on~$\Sigma'$ is still at state-height~$h_c$), and suppose~$v$ is included on~$X'$.
Then for every slot processed on~$X'$ from that point onward while canonical state-height remains~$h_c$, the Layer~1 increment to $\sigma.\mathsf{penalties}[i]$ on canonical is zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At the voting opportunity, (R1) or (R2) fires with target $T_c \preceq X$ satisfying $\sigma[T_c].h = h_c$ (Remark~\ref{rem:fresh-equiv}).
The produced vote~$v$ has $v.\height = h_c$, $v.\target = T_c \preceq X$, and $v.\target.\slot \geq \sigma[X].s_h$ (since $\sigma[T_c].h = h_c$).
State-height monotonicity along~$\preceq$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:slotmono}) and the hypothesis $\Sigma'.\sigma[X'].h = h_c$ give $\Sigma'.\sigma[X'].s_h = \Sigma.\sigma[X].s_h$ (the slot at which the current height began is the slot of the block that advanced to~$h_c$ on the common prefix; Lemma~\ref{lem:slotmono}), so~$v$ is canonical-fresh on~$X'$.
Processing~$v$ therefore sets $\Sigma'.\sigma[X'].\mathsf{ntargets}[i] \gets \max_{\preceq}(\Sigma'.\sigma[X'].\mathsf{ntargets}[i],\, T_c) \neq \bot$.

Layer~1 of $\textsf{processInactivityPenalties}$ (Figure~\ref{alg:leak-processslot}) fires at a slot iff $\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}.h = \sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.h$, in which case it bumps every~$j$ with $\sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.\mathsf{ntargets}[j] = \bot$.
$\mathsf{ntargets}$ is reset to~$\bot^n$ only when $\textsf{processHeight}$ advances~$h$; along the extension of~$X'$ while canonical state-height stays at~$h_c$, no height advance fires on the canonical post-state, so $\mathsf{ntargets}[i] \neq \bot$ persists on canonical from the slot at which~$v$ is included onward, for as long as canonical stays at~$h_c$.
Hence $\sigma_{\mathrm{post}}.\mathsf{ntargets}[i] \neq \bot$ from the slot at which~$v$ is included onward, for as long as canonical stays at~$h_c$, and Layer~1 does not bump~$i$.
\end{proof}

The statement makes no assumption about $f < n/3$, synchrony, determinism of $\textsf{getHead}$, or what other validators vote; it only requires that~$i$ follow Definition~\ref{def:voting-rule} locally and that~$v$ actually be included on the extension.
Layer~2 analysis would require additional hypotheses (synchrony, $f < n/3$, enough honest validators on the relevant chain at the relevant slot) that are genuinely orthogonal to fairness as a local guarantee.
