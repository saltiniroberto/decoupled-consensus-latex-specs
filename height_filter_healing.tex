\title{Fresh Simplex with Height Filter and Timeouts}
\author{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We describe Fresh Simplex and prove accountable safety and recovery after a
period of asynchrony.  Quorum checks at a given height use fixed integer
weights.  A current-height message counts toward height advancement when its
target is empty (an explicit timeout) or lies on the including chain.  Only a
vote for the exact current-height target counts toward justification.  An
empty vote has no height and counts toward neither tally.  The chain state
therefore needs only one target-participation bit and one timeout bit per
validator.  Signed votes are still kept as evidence for E1 and E2.

Recovery rounds use a time-shifted quorum (TSQ) over SG head votes.  Write
\(q=\lceil2W/3\rceil\).  Before the proposal, each validator freezes its
support-round view.  The proposer later chooses the deepest viable root with
current-view support at least~\(q\) and proposes a descendant of it.  At the available
vote, a validator chooses the deepest viable root supported by weight~\(q\)
through the same signed heads in both its frozen and current views.  This is
its lock and available-layer starting root.  The time shift makes every
honest lock visible in an honest proposer's later view, so the honest
proposal extends every lock.  The proposal itself is never installed as a
stable root merely because the proposer sent it.  A signer with two eligible
support-round heads is excluded and supplies no TSQ support, and no lower positive
synchronization threshold exists.

Let~\(H\) be nonjustifiable on every valid branch, including branches revealed
after recovery begins.  Once the protocol advances from~\(H\) through
\(H+1\) to~\(H+2\), Goldfish keeps the available chain in the confirmed
subtree.  If the confirming SG heads cross a TSQ freeze and remain usable at
an action completed before any responsive honest target or timeout signature
at height~\(H+2\), every later available-layer starting root is also in that
subtree.  Receiving the height-\(H+2\) state is permitted before this action;
signing from it is not.  In the delayed-\(C\) case, the source round's
height-\(H+2\) FG checkpoint action must be postponed until afterward.  This
freshness also excludes a pre-existing justification at height~\(H+2\) or
above.

Available-chain stability is not by itself a finality result.  Finality also
requires a later ordinary height at which no honest validator has previously
voted.  The votes at that height must be included on time, the resulting
justification must be known before the following finality votes, and it must
remain the latest justification until those votes are included.  Under these
conditions the ordinary height is justified and then finalized.
The cross-round compatibility of the SG and available-confirmation layer is
an explicit external graded-agreement composition premise; it is not derived
from TSQ or synchrony in this paper.
\end{abstract}

\section{Protocol overview}\label{sec:overview}

The finality state machine and the fork-choice rule are defined separately.
Goldfish gives the available-chain confirmation.  The stabilization gadget
uses latest head votes to keep later fork-choice walks on that chain, and the
finality gadget advances height and finalizes justified blocks.  The
\(G_0\) test defines a safe-confirmed prefix for target and timeout votes; it
does not alter Goldfish confirmation.  The recovery proof uses compatible
Goldfish confirmations during the recovery period.  It does not require
honest validators to have identical SG-message views or denominators.  TSQ
uses one fixed absolute quorum for synchronization; the ordinary \(G_1\)
root selection and \(G_0\) conflict veto retain their local \(2/3\) and \(1/3\)
tests.  In particular, one-third support is never a positive instruction to
adopt a root.

\section{Model and quorum weights}\label{sec:model}

\begin{definition}[Fixed validator weights]\label{def:validator-weights}
Let \(V\) be a fixed validator set and let \(w(i)\) be validator~\(i\)'s
fixed positive integer weight.  Write
\[
  W=\sum_{i\in V}w(i),
  \qquad
  q=\left\lceil\frac{2W}{3}\right\rceil.
\]
For \(S\subseteq V\), write \(w(S)=\sum_{i\in S}w(i)\).  A quorum is a set
\(Q\subseteq V\) with \(w(Q)\geq q\).
A quorum check against~\(q\) may equivalently use
\(3w(Q)\geq2W\).  Every other threshold check uses its stated exact integer
inequality; no rounded \emph{adjusted denominator} is part of the protocol.
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[Fixed electorate and fault bound]\label{ass:fixed-electorate}
Every chain and round uses the fixed identities and weights~\(V,w\).
Finality and TSQ checks use \(W,q\), while ordinary SG scoring uses the local
live subset and denominator defined below.  The Byzantine weight \(b\)
satisfies
\[
  b < 2q-W.
\]
\end{assumption}

The paper does not model validator churn.  An implementation with changing
validator sets or balances needs a separate overlap argument based on its
rate limits and weak-subjectivity assumptions; assigning a common but varying
electorate to each height would not prove that argument.

\begin{lemma}[Weighted quorum intersection]\label{lem:quorum-intersection}
Any two quorums \(Q,Q'\subseteq V\) satisfy
\[
  w(Q\cap Q')\geq 2q-W.
\]
Consequently their intersection contains non-Byzantine weight under
Assumption~\ref{ass:fixed-electorate}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since \(w(Q\cup Q')\leq W\),
\[
 w(Q\cap Q')=w(Q)+w(Q')-w(Q\cup Q')
 \geq 2q-W.
\]
The adversary controls strictly less weight than this lower bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Height and current-height target]\label{def:height}
Height is the finality-gadget counter and is separate from slot.  Genesis is treated
as justified and finalized at height \(0\), and the genesis state is at current
height \(1\) with \(T_1=\genesis\).  A justification certificate or timeout certificate
advances height by exactly one.

For \(h>1\), \(s_h\) is the start slot chosen by the transition that enters
height~\(h\).  If the outcome is consumed after processing a block~\(B\), then
\(s_h=B.\slot\); thus the transition block is the first block of the new height
in its post-state, even though its prestate was at the previous height.  If the
outcome is consumed after an empty slot~\(s\), then \(s_h=s+1\).  The
\emph{current-height target} \(T_h\) is a state field.  It is initially~\(\bot\).
After the first block processed at or after~\(s_h\), the following slot
transition records that block as~\(T_h\), and the field
then remains fixed until the next height transition.  For height~\(1\),
genesis is the target and carries the genesis start slot.  A later height's
first block cannot store or vote for its own canonical root: that root commits
to the block's post-state, so putting it in the same post-state would be
circular.  The following slot transition fills the cached header's state root
and can then record the block root; descendants can name it.  A
checkpoint-sync state in the middle of a height carries \(T_h\).  A checkpoint
exactly at the transition block may instead carry
\((h,s_h,T_h)=(h,B.\slot,\bot)\); it must authenticate~\(B\) so validator
actions derive the same target until the next slot materializes it.

The convention \(T_1=\genesis\) applies when the protocol is active
from genesis.  At a fork activation, the first Simplex height starts with an
empty target.  The first eligible post-fork block becomes the target once its
root can be computed; the pre-fork genesis or finalized root is not used.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Blocks and chains]\label{def:block-chain}
A block has a parent, a strictly increasing slot, a deterministic finite set
of individual signed FG attestations, and a post-state.  Its root is known when its post-state is
recorded; after that point we identify the block with its root.  Blocks form a
tree rooted at genesis.
Write \(B\preceq C\) when \(B\) is an ancestor of \(C\), and \(B\sim C\) when
they are compatible.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[FG message]\label{def:fg-message}
An FG message, also called an individual FG vote or attestation, is a signed
object~\(v\)
with fields
\[
  (v.\validator,\ v.\mathsf{round},\ v.\headf,\ v.\height,\ v.\target,\
   v.\finalizeHeight,\ v.\finalizeTarget).
\]
The head field \(v.\headf\) is a block root and is the SG latest message.
The pair \((v.\height,v.\target)\) is the voted checkpoint and has one of
three forms:
\[
  (h,T)\quad\text{with }T\neq\bot\ \text{(target vote)},\qquad
  (h,\bot)\ \text{(timeout vote)},\qquad
  (\bot,\bot)\ \text{(empty vote)}.
\]
The finalize pair \((v.\finalizeHeight,v.\finalizeTarget)\) is either
\((\bot,\bot)\) or a commitment \((h_f,T_f)\), counted only while the
signer's current chain state has latest justified pair
\((J,h_j)=(T_f,h_f)\).  An empty vote has no height and sets neither
participation bit.  Even then, \(v.\headf\) may support a later TSQ or ordinary
SG check, and its finalize pair may support finalization of the latest
justification.
\end{definition}

A target vote counts toward justification on a chain at height \(h\) only
when its target is the stored current-height target \(T=T_h\), and \(T\) is a
strict ancestor of the block containing the vote.  Independently, a message
counts toward timeout when it names the chain's current height and either has
an empty target or names any ancestor of the block containing it.  Thus a
noncanonical but on-chain target sets only the timeout bit; an off-chain
target sets neither participation bit but remains usable as E2 evidence.  At
a nonjustifiable height the state machine does not justify a target, but any
on-chain repeated target still sets the timeout bit.  This allows a validator
already locked at that height to contribute to height advancement without
violating E1.  A finalize pair equal to
\((h_f,T_f)\) is counted only while that pair is the chain's latest
unfinalized justification, using \(V\) and \(w\).

\begin{definition}[Slashing rules]\label{def:slashing}
Two signed messages by the same validator are slashable when either condition
below holds.  The two conflicting fields may also occur in the same signed
message.
\begin{description}
  \item[E1:] one message~\(b\) has
    \((b.\finalizeHeight,b.\finalizeTarget)=(h,T)\) with \(T\neq\bot\),
    while the other~\(a\) has \(a.\height=h\) and \(a.\target\neq T\); or
  \item[E2:] \(a.\height=b.\height=h\), both targets are nonempty, and
    \(a.\target\neq b.\target\).
\end{description}
An empty vote has \(a.\height=\bot\) and therefore does not conflict with a
finalize commitment at a real height.
\end{definition}

E2 makes two target votes at one height slashable.  E1 prevents a validator
that voted to finalize a target from helping a conflicting chain advance past
the same height.

\section{Deterministic finality state machine}\label{sec:state-machine}

A chain state is
\[
  \sigma=(L,s,h,s_h,T_h,\nj,\targetParticipation,\timeouts,
          \pendingHeightOutcome,
          J,h_j,F,h_F,P).
\]
Here \(L\) is the latest block and \(s\) is the current processed slot, which
may be greater than \(L.\slot\).  The value \(h\) is the current height,
\((s_h,T_h)\) the current height's start slot and target, and
\(\nj\in\{\mathsf{false},\mathsf{true}\}\) says whether that height is
nonjustifiable.  The flag is set when the chain enters~\(h\) and does not
change until the next height transition.  Finality processing does not change it.  The pair
\((F,h_F)\) is the greatest finalized root and height.  Initially \(h=1\),
\((J,h_j)=(F,h_F)=(\genesis,0)\),
\((s_1,T_1)=(0,\genesis)\), and \(\nj=\mathsf{false}\).  Initially every
\(\targetParticipation[i]\) and \(\timeouts[i]\) is false and
\(P=\varnothing\) and \(\pendingHeightOutcome=\mathsf{false}\).  Write
\(\sigma_{\rm gen}\) for this initial state.  A height advance consumed after
block~\(B\)'s operations sets \(s_{h+1}=B.\slot\); one consumed after an empty
slot~\(s\) sets \(s_{h+1}=s+1\).  Both start with
\(T_{h+1}=\bot\).  The following slot processing records the first block at or
after~\(s_{h+1}\) once its root is available.  Thus a transition block~\(B\)
becomes the new target, while an empty transition waits for the first later
block.

The bit \(\targetParticipation[i]\) records whether validator~\(i\) has made
a current-height vote for the exact stored target~\(T_h\), included strictly
above that target.  Independently, \(\timeouts[i]\) records whether it has
made a current-height message whose target is empty or is an ancestor of its
including block.  Thus
\[
  Q_{\rm target}(\sigma)=\{i:\targetParticipation[i]=\mathsf{true}\},
  \qquad
  Q_{\rm timeout}(\sigma)=\{i:\timeouts[i]=\mathsf{true}\}.
\]
The state does not store a target value per validator: the only value that can
set \(\targetParticipation[i]\) is the stored target~\(T_h\).  The signed
messages are kept so that E1 and E2 violations can still be proved.  A
noncanonical target may set \(\timeouts[i]\) when it lies on the including
chain, but it cannot set \(\targetParticipation[i]\); an off-chain target sets
neither bit.  Different branches may have different current-height targets,
so their participation bits cannot be combined directly.  Each block state is
derived only from its parent and the attestations included in that block, as
specified in Section~\ref{sec:store}.

The pair \((J,h_j)\) is the chain's latest justified block and height.  The
set~\(P\) contains validators whose finalize pairs were accepted while this
pair was the latest unfinalized justification.  After it is finalized, the
set is ignored.  A newer justification replaces \((J,h_j)\) and resets~\(P\).
The state does not keep finality tallies for older justifications.

The attestations in a block \(B\) are treated as a set.  An exact
current-height target vote sets \(\targetParticipation[i]\).  Independently,
any current-height message with an empty or on-chain target sets
\(\timeouts[i]\).  A valid finalize commitment to the prestate's current
\((J,h_j)\) adds its signer to~\(P\).  Setting bits and adding elements to a
set are independent of message order.  A finality commitment to a
justification created by the same block is not counted.  It must appear in a
descendant whose prestate already contains that justification.  A certificate
received from another branch also does not replace the justified block in the
current branch's state.  Only the justification rule changes \((J,h_j)\)
below.

Each scheduled round boundary makes one height outcome due.  It is evaluated
exactly once after the following slot's operation phase: after that slot's
block attestations when it has a block, or on the empty-slot path otherwise.
This gives the round's last-slot votes one inclusion opportunity.  The outcome
is applied in this order:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item if \(h_j>h_F\), \(F\preceq J\), and
    \(w(P)\geq q\), set \((F,h_F)\gets(J,h_j)\);
  \item if \(\nj=\mathsf{false}\) and
    \(w(Q_{\rm target})\geq q\), set
    \((J,h_j)\gets(T_h,h)\), reset \(P\), and then advance \(h\), set
    \((s_h,T_h)\gets(s_\ast,\bot)\), set the new height's
    nonjustifiable flag by Definition~\ref{def:nonjustifiable}, and reset
    \(\targetParticipation\) and \(\timeouts\);
  \item otherwise, if \(w(Q_{\rm timeout})\geq q\), advance \(h\) and
    reset \(\targetParticipation\) and \(\timeouts\), again setting
    \((s_h,T_h)\gets(s_\ast,\bot)\) and setting the new height's
    nonjustifiable flag.  This is the
    timeout branch even when some members of the timeout quorum cast
    current-height target votes.
\end{enumerate}
Here \(s_\ast\) is the current block's slot on the block path and the slot
after the empty inclusion opportunity on the empty path.  Finality is
processed before a justification or timeout enabled by the same due outcome.
Since a chain stores one current-height target, all votes counted toward
justification increment the same bitlist.

An exact current-height target vote satisfies both predicates and therefore
sets both bits.  A noncanonical on-chain target sets only the timeout bit, as
does an explicit timeout.  A validator is counted once in
\(Q_{\rm timeout}\), even if the stored messages later reveal an equivocation.
Justification has priority when the target and timeout quorums are
simultaneously present; otherwise the timeout quorum advances by timeout.  A
validator already locked on an on-chain target at~\(h\) need not vote timeout:
its existing or repeated target vote already counts toward the timeout branch.

When a round's due outcome is consumed, finality is checked before
justification and timeout.
Thus a full quorum of finality votes for the latest justification is processed
before a new justification can replace it and reset~\(P\).  A partial tally is
discarded when a newer justification replaces it; the protocol finalizes
only the latest justified block.  It needs neither a fork-choice rule for
pending finality nor a per-height finality map.  An empty vote sets neither
participation bit.  Otherwise every validator could submit an empty vote
and advance the height without voting for a target or timeout.

Figure~\ref{alg:state-machine} gives the same rules as self-contained
pseudocode.  The routine \(\mathsf{process\_slot}\) is the transition that
advances beyond the current slot after that slot has been resolved; it is not
an action at the start of an arbitrary slot.  A live validator applies it to
an empty slot once that slot is known to be empty, and
\(\mathsf{state\_transition}\) replays it for every intervening empty slot
before a later block.  A round boundary sets
\(\pendingHeightOutcome\); the following slot consumes it after block
operations, or \(\mathsf{process\_slot}\) consumes it once that slot is known
to be empty.  In the latter case the invariant
\(\sigma.L.\slot<\sigma.s\), together with the fact that slot
\(\sigma.s-1\) ended a round, proves that no block was processed in the
inclusion-opportunity slot; otherwise \(\mathsf{process\_block}\) would
already have cleared the pending outcome.  A block never supplies a separate close flag.  The
\(\mathsf{state\_transition}\) interface starts from the authenticated
post-state cached for \(B.\parent\), not from a live action state that has
already processed later empty-slot ticks; this prevents replaying a slot
transition twice.  Every vote classification and quorum check used by the
state transition is written directly in the figure; it does not import helper
predicates or aggregate-attestation machinery from the executable
specification.

\begin{figure}[H]
\caption{Finality state machine}\label{alg:state-machine}
\begin{center}
\begin{boxedminipage}{\textwidth}
\scriptsize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]

\Statex \textbf{Fixed inputs:} \(V,w,q\); constants \(K\geq2\) and
  \(D_{\rm debt}\geq1\); and a schedule that identifies each round's final slot.
\Statex An attestation \(a\) is an individual signed message with fields
  \((a.\validator,a.\height,a.\target,a.\finalizeHeight,a.\finalizeTarget)\);
  \(B.\mathsf{attestations}\) is a finite set of them.
\Statex Its checkpoint pair is a target vote \((h,T)\), a timeout \((h,\bot)\),
  or empty \((\bot,\bot)\); its finalize pair is a commitment \((h_f,T_f)\)
  or empty \((\bot,\bot)\).
\Statex Its round and head fields are SG metadata and do not alter this
  per-chain finality state.
\vspace{3pt}

\State \textbf{Genesis state} \(\sigma\):
\Statex \hspace{1em}\(L\gets\genesis,\ s\gets0,\ h\gets1,\
  \currentHeightStartSlot\gets0,\ \currentHeightTarget\gets\genesis\)
\Statex \hspace{1em}\(\currentHeightNonjustifiable\gets\mathsf{false},\
  \targetParticipation\gets\mathsf{false}^{V},\
  \timeouts\gets\mathsf{false}^{V},\
  \pendingHeightOutcome\gets\mathsf{false}\)
\Statex \hspace{1em}\((J,h_j)\gets(\genesis,0),\
  (F,h_F)\gets(\genesis,0),\ P\gets\varnothing\)
\vspace{3pt}

\Function{state\_transition}{$\sigma,B$}
  \State \textbf{assert} \(B.\parent=\sigma.L\) and
    \(B.\slot>\sigma.s\)
  \State \(\sigma\gets\Call{process\_slots}{\sigma,B.\slot}\)
  \State \Return \(\Call{process\_block}{\sigma,B}\)
\EndFunction
\vspace{3pt}

\Function{process\_slots}{$\sigma,\mathit{slot}$}
  \While{\(\sigma.s<\mathit{slot}\)}
    \State \(\sigma\gets\Call{process\_slot}{\sigma}\)
  \EndWhile
  \State \Return \(\sigma\)
\EndFunction
\vspace{3pt}

\Function{process\_slot}{$\sigma$}
  \If{\(\sigma.\currentHeightTarget=\bot\) and
       \(\sigma.L.\slot\geq\sigma.\currentHeightStartSlot\)}
    \State \(\sigma.\currentHeightTarget\gets\sigma.L\)
      \Comment{Its canonical root is now noncircular}
  \EndIf
  \If{\(\sigma.\pendingHeightOutcome\)}
    \State \textbf{assert} \(\sigma.L.\slot<\sigma.s\) and slot
      \(\sigma.s-1\) ended a round
      \Comment{This first post-round slot was empty}
    \State \(\sigma\gets
      \Call{process\_justification\_and\_finalization}{\sigma,\sigma.s+1}\)
    \State \(\sigma.\pendingHeightOutcome\gets\mathsf{false}\)
  \EndIf
  \If{slot \(\sigma.s\) is the final slot of its round}
    \State \(\sigma.\pendingHeightOutcome\gets\mathsf{true}\)
  \EndIf
  \State \(\sigma.s\gets\sigma.s+1\)
  \State \Return \(\sigma\)
\EndFunction
\vspace{3pt}

\Function{process\_block}{$\sigma,B$}
  \State \textbf{assert} \(B.\parent=\sigma.L\) and
    \(B.\slot=\sigma.s\)
  \State \(\sigma.L\gets B\)
    \Comment{The next slot materializes its canonical root}
  \ForAll{\(a\in B.\mathsf{attestations}\)}
    \State \(\sigma\gets\Call{process\_attestation}{\sigma,a}\)
  \EndFor
  \If{\(\sigma.\pendingHeightOutcome\)}
    \State \(\sigma\gets
      \Call{process\_justification\_and\_finalization}{\sigma,\sigma.s}\)
    \State \(\sigma.\pendingHeightOutcome\gets\mathsf{false}\)
      \Comment{The transition block opens the new height}
  \EndIf
  \State \Return \(\sigma\)
\EndFunction
\vspace{3pt}

\Function{process\_attestation}{$\sigma,a$}
  \State \(i\gets a.\validator\); \quad
    \textbf{assert} \(i\in V\) and \(a\) is well formed and validly signed by \(i\)
  \State \(\mathit{target\_vote}\gets
    (a.\height=\sigma.h)\wedge
    (a.\target=\sigma.\currentHeightTarget)\wedge
    (a.\target\neq\bot)\wedge
    (a.\target\prec\sigma.L)\)
  \State \(\mathit{counts\_for\_timeout}\gets
    (a.\height=\sigma.h)\wedge
    ((a.\target=\bot)\vee(a.\target\preceq\sigma.L))\)
  \If{\(\sigma.h_j>\sigma.h_F\), \(\sigma.F\preceq\sigma.J\),
      \(a.\finalizeHeight=\sigma.h_j\), and
      \(a.\finalizeTarget=\sigma.J\)}
    \State \(\sigma.P\gets\sigma.P\cup\{i\}\)
  \EndIf
  \If{\(\mathit{target\_vote}\)}
    \State \(\sigma.\targetParticipation[i]\gets\mathsf{true}\)
  \EndIf
  \If{\(\mathit{counts\_for\_timeout}\)}
    \State \(\sigma.\timeouts[i]\gets\mathsf{true}\)
  \EndIf
  \State \Return \(\sigma\)
\EndFunction
\vspace{3pt}

\Function{process\_justification\_and\_finalization}{$\sigma,\mathit{height\_start\_slot}$}
  \If{\(\sigma.h_j>\sigma.h_F\), \(\sigma.F\preceq\sigma.J\), and
      \(w(\sigma.P)\geq q\)}
    \State \((\sigma.F,\sigma.h_F)\gets(\sigma.J,\sigma.h_j)\)
  \EndIf
  \If{\(\neg\sigma.\currentHeightNonjustifiable\),
      \(\sigma.\currentHeightTarget\neq\bot\), and
      \(w(\{i\in V:\sigma.\targetParticipation[i]\})\geq q\)}
    \State \Return \(\Call{advance\_height}{\sigma,
      \sigma.\currentHeightTarget,\mathit{height\_start\_slot}}\)
  \EndIf
  \If{\(w(\{i\in V:\sigma.\timeouts[i]\})\geq q\)}
    \State \Return
      \(\Call{advance\_height}{\sigma,\bot,\mathit{height\_start\_slot}}\)
  \EndIf
  \State \Return \(\sigma\)
\EndFunction
\vspace{3pt}

\Function{advance\_height}{$\sigma,\mathit{justify\_target},\mathit{height\_start\_slot}$}
  \If{\(\mathit{justify\_target}\neq\bot\)}
    \State \((\sigma.J,\sigma.h_j)\gets
      (\mathit{justify\_target},\sigma.h);\quad \sigma.P\gets\varnothing\)
  \EndIf
  \State \(\sigma.h\gets\sigma.h+1\)
  \State \(\sigma.\currentHeightStartSlot\gets\mathit{height\_start\_slot};\quad
    \sigma.\currentHeightTarget\gets\bot\)
  \State \(\sigma.\currentHeightNonjustifiable\gets
    (\sigma.h-\sigma.h_F>D_{\rm debt})\mathbin{\wedge}(K\mid\sigma.h)\)
  \State \(\sigma.\targetParticipation\gets\mathsf{false}^{V}\)
  \State \(\sigma.\timeouts\gets\mathsf{false}^{V}\)
  \State \Return \(\sigma\)
\EndFunction

\end{algorithmic}
\end{boxedminipage}
\end{center}
\end{figure}

\begin{definition}[Certificates]\label{def:certificates}
A \emph{justification certificate} \(\mathsf{JC}(h,T)\) consists of signed
target votes of weight at least \(q\), together with state data showing that
the votes were counted while \(\nj=\mathsf{false}\) and \(T_h=T\), and
that the justification rule set \((J,h_j)=(T,h)\).  A target quorum observed
at a nonjustifiable height is not a JC\@.
A \emph{timeout certificate} \(\mathsf{TC}(h)\) is a set of signed messages
from distinct validators with weight at least \(q\), each processed at
height~\(h\) with either an empty target or a target on the including chain.
When no target quorum is selected first, a
\(\mathsf{TC}(h)\) advances height by timeout.
A \emph{finality certificate} \(\mathsf{FC}(h,T)\) is a
\(\mathsf{JC}(h,T)\) together with finality commitments to \((h,T)\) of weight
at least \(q\), collected while \((T,h)\) was the chain state's latest
justification pair.
Certificates include the ancestry required to validate every nonempty target
that contributes to either tally.
Other validators can verify them, but
receiving a certificate does not change a branch's latest justified block or
authorize a vote on a conflicting chain.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Target uniqueness]\label{lem:target-uniqueness}
Two conflicting targets cannot both have justification certificates at one
height unless validators of total weight at least~\(2q-W\) violate E2.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The signer sets of the two certificates intersect in weight at least
\(2q-W\) by Lemma~\ref{lem:quorum-intersection}.  Every signer in
the intersection signed both distinct targets at height \(h\).
\end{proof}

\section{Accountable safety}\label{sec:safety}

\begin{lemma}[Height progression]\label{lem:height-progression}
Every height transition increments the height by exactly one and requires a
justification certificate or timeout certificate counted using the fixed
validator weights.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The justification and timeout rules are the only rules that advance height.  Both
increment it by one, and both use the weights required by
Assumption~\ref{ass:fixed-electorate}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Current-height target-participation validity]\label{lem:height-target-freshness}
If a target \(T\) is counted in \(Q_{\rm target}\) at height \(h\) on a chain
ending at \(B\), then \(T=T_h\prec B\), and the chain's post-state at \(T\)
has current height \(h\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The target-participation predicate requires \(T\) to be the stored
current-height target and a strict ancestor of the block containing the vote.
The target is initialized
to genesis for \(h=1\).  At each later height, slot processing records the
first block at or after the height start slot and replaces it only at the next
height transition.  The strict-ancestor check ensures that this recording step
has occurred before a vote naming the block can be counted: while the field is
empty the target bit cannot be set, and before a descendant can include the
vote, its slot transition materializes the target root.  If a block~\(T\)
consumed the preceding height's due outcome, its post-state is already at
height~\(h\) and its slot is the new start slot.  If that outcome was consumed
on an empty-slot path, the target is the first later block and likewise has a
height-\(h\) post-state.  No height transition occurs between that post-state
and installing~\(T\) as~\(T_h\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Target uniqueness on a chain]\label{lem:chain-target-uniqueness}
Each reached height has at most one current-height target on a given chain;
once nonempty, it is the chain's unique first block at that height.  Suppose a
vote for target~\(T\) counts toward target participation on one branch at
height~\(h\).  If another
branch at height~\(h\) has processed beyond~\(T\) and contains~\(T\), then its
current-height target is also~\(T\), and it counts the vote toward target
participation.  Distinct targets can be counted toward target participation
only on conflicting branches.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Height never decreases and each transition increments it by one.  A chain
therefore has at most one first block at or after the start of height~\(h\), which
Definition~\ref{def:height} stores after its root becomes available and until
the next transition.  The authenticated post-state of the transition block
fixes the same start slot on every extension containing it.  If another
height-\(h\) branch has processed beyond target block~\(T\), it inherits that
state and has already recorded~\(T\).  The target-participation rule then
counts the vote.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Target participation bits]\label{lem:target-bit-compression}
For a given chain state, all information needed for the justification and
timeout rules is contained in the two Boolean arrays
\[
  \targetParticipation
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \timeouts.
\]
Retaining the signed messages is sufficient to prove E1 and E2 violations.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By the target-participation rule and
Lemma~\ref{lem:chain-target-uniqueness}, every vote counted toward
justification names~\(T_h\).  Its signer set is therefore
\(\{i:\targetParticipation[i]=\mathsf{true}\}\).  The set of validators that
supplied a current-height message whose target was empty or on the including
chain is
\(\{i:\timeouts[i]=\mathsf{true}\}\), with each validator counted once.
These are the only two signer sets used by the height transition.  E1 and E2
are checked from pairs of signed messages, so they do not require target roots
in the participation state.
\end{proof}

Counting a noncanonical on-chain target only for timeout cannot move the chain
behind that target.  The message is processed in a block~\(B\) only when
\(T\preceq B\).  If the resulting due outcome advances the height, the next
height's target is~\(B\) or a later descendant.  The transition need not
install \(T\) as justified, but it preserves \(T\) as a chain prefix.

\begin{lemma}[Past a finalized height]\label{lem:past-finalized}
Let \(C\) be finalized at height \(h\).  Any block \(B\) whose chain has
advanced beyond \(h\) contains \(C\).  For \(h\geq1\), the only exception
requires E1-slashable validators of total weight at least~\(2q-W\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(h=0\), then \(C\) is genesis and the result is immediate.  Assume
\(h\geq1\).
A chain that advances beyond \(h\) contains either a justification certificate
with signer set \(Q_J\), or a timeout certificate with signer set
\(Q_{\rm timeout}\).  The finality certificate for \(C\) has signer set~\(Q_F\).
Either intersection has weight at least \(2q-W\).  Consider any
non-slashable signer in the relevant intersection.  If its height message has
an empty target or a target different from~\(C\), it conflicts by E1 with its
finality commitment.  The only non-slashable possibility is therefore a
message targeting~\(C\).  Such a message can set the timeout bit on this chain
only if \(C\) is an ancestor of its including block.  Thus, if \(B\) did not
contain~\(C\), every member of the quorum intersection would be E1-slashable,
contradicting the bound.  This argument does not require timeout support to be
restricted to the chain's canonical current-height target.
\end{proof}

The uniform E1 rule is essential here: a finality commitment to~\((h,C)\)
conflicts with every height-\(h\) message targeting \(X\neq C\), including
\(X=\bot\).  This is what permits noncanonical on-chain targets to share the
timeout tally.  Target-target E2 alone is insufficient: an intersection signer
can commit to finalize~\(C\) and later target nonempty~\(X\) without the
commitment itself supplying a signed target-\(C\) half of an E2 pair.  E1 makes
that combination directly slashable.

\begin{lemma}[Finalized blocks form a chain]\label{lem:finalized-chain}
For any finalized pairs \((C,h)\) and \((C',h')\), if \(h\leq h'\) then
\(C\preceq C'\), unless the fault bound in
Assumption~\ref{ass:fixed-electorate} is violated.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(h=0\), then \(C\) is genesis and the result is immediate.  Assume
\(h\geq1\).
If \(h=h'\), intersect a finality certificate for \(C\) with the justification
certificate for \(C'\).  E1 forces every non-slashable intersection signer to
have \(C=C'\).

If \(h<h'\), the chain on which \(C'\) was finalized advanced beyond \(h\), so
Lemma~\ref{lem:past-finalized} implies that this chain contains \(C\).  Both
\(C\) and \(C'\) are therefore on it.  Current-height target validity gives state-height \(h\) at \(C\)
and state-height \(h'\) at \(C'\); because state-height is nondecreasing along a
chain and \(h<h'\), we have \(C\prec C'\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Accountable safety]\label{thm:accountable-safety}
Conflicting blocks cannot both finalize unless, for some height \(h\), at least
\(2q-W\) weight is provably E1- or E2-slashable.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This follows from Lemmas~\ref{lem:target-uniqueness}
and~\ref{lem:finalized-chain}.
\end{proof}

The proof does not require synchrony.  It is independent of TSQ,
the available chain, graded agreement, confirmation, and nonjustifiable
heights.

\section{Store merging}\label{sec:store}

\begin{definition}[Stored data]\label{def:stored-data}
A node's raw store is
\[
  S=(\mathsf{blocks},\mathsf{attestations},\mathsf{timely},
     \mathsf{completed\_steps},\mathsf{time}),
\]
with:
\begin{itemize}
  \item received signed blocks in \(\mathsf{blocks}\), including blocks whose
    parent is not yet known and finalized-conflicting blocks retained as
    evidence;
  \item signed \(\mathsf{attestations}\), including standalone SG pre-votes,
    FG messages received directly, and FG messages extracted from received
    blocks;
  \item \(\mathsf{timely}\) facts, where
    \(\mathsf{timely}(x,I)\) records that object \(x\) was received by the
    deadline of scheduled step~\(I\);
  \item \(\mathsf{completed\_steps}\), each recording the step, its exact
    input, the preceding records on which it depended, and its fixed result;
    and
  \item the local clock \(\mathsf{time}\).
\end{itemize}
The first four components are sets.  A timely fact is local metadata, not a
signed network message.  Step~\(I\) uses the objects marked timely for~\(I\)
when its deadline is reached.  Its result is then fixed.  A later message may
be evidence or input to a later step, but it does not change a completed step.
The post-state map \(\sigma_S[B]\), pending-reference indices, and the
fork-choice state~\(\Sigma\) are derived from these raw sets.  They may be
cached, but are not independent merge inputs.

A completed TSQ freeze records the exact signed SG heads and equivocation
evidence received at its deadline, together with which named blocks and
ancestries were then available.  This fixes frozen support eligibility; later
ancestry does not change it.  The corresponding available-vote action records its later current
view, its computed lock, and the proposal, if any, that was eligible from that
lock.  These are completed-step records, not mutable fields on a proposal
block.  A later head or equivocation may affect a later TSQ instance, but it
does not rewrite a completed freeze or lock.
\end{definition}

A block's post-state is obtained from its parent's post-state by replaying
intervening slot transitions, processing the attestations included in that
block, and then consuming any due outcome.  When two stores are merged, each
block is replayed with its own included attestation batch.  In particular, target,
timeout, and finality bitlists from sibling blocks are never combined.  A
chain counts only its stored current-height target toward justification.  A
vote for another target can still set the timeout bit when that target is on
the including chain, and can independently provide slashing evidence.  An
off-chain target changes neither participation bit.  Blocks that conflict
with the latest finalized block do not affect the protocol state.

\begin{definition}[Store merge]\label{def:store-join}
For stores \(S_1,S_2\), their merge \(S_1\sqcup S_2\) contains the union of
their blocks, attestations, timely facts, and completed-step records.
Its clock is the greater of the two clocks.  A scheduled step that neither
copy has completed uses every input object marked timely for that step in
either copy.  If the timely input contains two distinct round-start blocks
signed by the same proposer, the proposer has equivocated and the
synchronization action treats neither as its proposal.  This does not alter
the action's independently computed TSQ lock.

A merge does not recompute a completed step.  A second proposal learned after
the action deadline is retained as equivocation evidence.  If the two copies completed the same
step with different local inputs, the merge retains both histories; it does
not replace them with a single new version of that past step.  A node can use
the merged store for a common continuation after all later steps that depend
on either result have completed.  Equivocating head messages remain stored as
evidence; Definition~\ref{def:synchronization-root} excludes their signer
from TSQ support when two heads are eligible for that TSQ instance.  A valid attestation
whose head is unknown remains stored until that block and its ancestors
arrive.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Order independence of evidence merge]\label{thm:store-merge}
Under the fault bound in Assumption~\ref{ass:fixed-electorate}, and after
ignoring blocks that conflict with the merged finalized root, the union of the stored evidence and the inputs to
future steps is associative, commutative, and idempotent.  Merging \(S_1\) and
\(S_2\) does not require the store from which they were copied.  This statement
does not identify two different completed executions of the same step.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set union is associative, commutative, and idempotent; maximum has the same
three properties; and future steps are deterministic functions of their
complete stored input: blocks and attestations received by their deadlines,
their derived state, and retained results of completed steps.  Completed
results are retained as history
rather than recomputed.  By accountable safety, all finalized roots are
compatible under the same bound and therefore have a unique greatest element.
\end{proof}

The last sentence is a real boundary on the theorem.  For example, suppose
\(S_1\) has already completed a freeze containing head~\(A\), while \(S_2\)
has received a different timely head~\(B\) but has not completed that freeze.
Their merge retains the completed frozen view~\(\{A\}\); it does not rerun the
past action.  Merging the raw evidence before either copy completed the freeze
would instead produce~\(\{A,B\}\).  Thus evidence and future inputs merge
without their common ancestor, but stores that have executed different past
actions are not claimed to become the same completed execution.

Head messages are live only for their specified round window.  After merging,
the clock is
\(t=\max(t_1,t_2)\).  An event with expiry \(d\) is live exactly when
\(d>t_1\) and \(d>t_2\).  This remains true if the event is present in only one
input.  An event that has expired in either store is therefore expired in the
merged store, so deleted expired data need not be restored.  A timely fact is
different: \(\mathsf{timely}(x,I)\) remains in the union after the deadline has
passed.

For an available-layer committee selected for a given available vote, the store must retain
the walk head and state from which the committee was chosen, together with the
ordered weighted validator list.  If two stores contain different inputs, the
implementation must either show that they produce the same committee or apply
a deterministic conflict rule.  A cached committee list does not record how
the committee was selected.  These values are part of that available-vote
step's completed result.

Pruning data below a finalized root must retain the finalized state and the
fixed validator weights, the
\(\mathsf{current\_height\_target}\), the
\(\mathsf{current\_height\_nonjustifiable}\) value and the data used to
determine it, the latest justified pair and evidence that it was justified
at an ordinary height, and any unresolved timely facts.  Validators' signing
histories are local data rather than part of the network store, and are not
pruned by this rule.

Figure~\ref{alg:store} gives the \(\mathsf{on\_block}\) and
\(\mathsf{on\_attestation}\) store operations.  The
\(\mathsf{on\_attestation}\) entry point accepts either a standalone
signed SG pre-vote or a signed FG message; only FG messages can appear in a
block's state-transition batch, while both kinds can support fork choice.
An attestation with an unknown head remains in the raw store.  It
becomes usable by an uncompleted step when the block and ancestry are known
and were timely for that step; it never changes a completed step.
The derived state map is rebuilt after a merge.  It processes each block from
its own parent state and its own included attestations, and never combines sibling
states.  If two completed records for the same step remain after a merge, a
later operation that requires one predecessor result does not run until the
protocol has a common continuation.  The completed available-vote record stores
the round's stabilization root, called its \emph{stable root}.  Every later
\textsf{get\_head} call in that round reads this stable root; later votes do not
change it.  A synchronization action first reads the completed frozen view and
then computes the fixed-\(q\) TSQ lock from that view and its timely current
view.  A
completed result is usable only when its predecessor chain
matches the current history and it is the unique such result; otherwise the
dependent operation waits.

\section{Fork-choice state and nonjustifiable heights}\label{sec:fork-choice-state}

Fork choice is computed from the store of Section~\ref{sec:store} as follows.
Let \(\Sigma.\T\) be the accepted block tree restricted to descendants
of the greatest finalized block~\(\Sigma.F\).  Accepted blocks that conflict
with~\(\Sigma.F\) may remain in the store as evidence, but their heads supply
no branch support to fork choice.  Signer-level slashing and round-equivocation
evidence remains valid independently of the referenced branch.  Among latest
justifications in accepted block states whose
blocks descend from~\(\Sigma.F\), let \((\Sigma.J,\Sigma.h_j)\) have the greatest
height, breaking ties by target hash.  Let
\(\Sigma.\hmax\) be the greatest state-height of any block in this restricted
tree.  Blocks outside the subtree of \(\Sigma.F\) do not supply branch,
height, or finality support.

\begin{definition}[Viable subtree and Simplex root]\label{def:simplex-root}
A block is \emph{viable} in~\(\Sigma\) when it has an accepted leaf descendant
whose state-height is at least \(\Sigma.\hmax-1\).  Write
\(\viableTree(\Sigma)\) for the viable subtree.  The \emph{Simplex root}
is the derived value
\[
  \simplexRoot(\Sigma)=
  \begin{cases}
    \Sigma.J,&\Sigma.\hmax=\Sigma.h_j+1,\\
    \Sigma.F,&\text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
\]
Thus the latest justification roots the walk only while it is exactly one
height behind \(\Sigma.\hmax\).  Once a timeout moves a chain one height
farther, the walk starts from the finalized root instead.
Every stable root defined below is required to lie in
\(\viableTree(\Sigma)\) and to descend from~\(\simplexRoot(\Sigma)\).
\end{definition}

The finalized root is viable because a block that raises \(h_{\max}\) is
accepted only if it descends from the local finalized block.
When the Simplex-root rule selects~\(J\), the justified target itself is at
state-height~\(h_{\max}-1\), so it is viable as well.  Every descent used below
therefore starts from a viable root.

\begin{lemma}[Finalized lock-in]\label{lem:finalized-lockin}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:fixed-electorate}, once an honest store has processed
the finalization of block~\(F\), every later accepted block and every later
walk output descends from~\(F\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The accepted tree is restricted to descendants of the store's greatest
finalized block.  Lemma~\ref{lem:finalized-chain} makes every later finalized
block descend from~\(F\), so this restriction never moves behind or across
\(F\).  Every Simplex root, stable root, and walk output lies in that accepted
tree.
\end{proof}

Fix protocol constants \(K\geq2\) and \(D_{\rm debt}\geq1\).

\begin{definition}[Nonjustifiable height]\label{def:nonjustifiable}
When a chain enters height~\(h\), it first processes finality.  It is in
\emph{finality debt} when the resulting state has
\(h-h_F>D_{\rm debt}\).  The chain sets
\(\nj=\mathsf{true}\) exactly when it is in finality debt
and \(K\mid h\); otherwise it sets
\(\nj=\mathsf{false}\).  This value is part of the authenticated
consensus state and remains fixed while the chain is at height~\(h\).  At a
nonjustifiable height, the justification branch of the state transition is
disabled.  Once it has confirmed the height, an honest validator with no
earlier vote at that height votes for timeout.  A validator locked on the
chain may repeat its saved target; whenever that target lies on the including
chain, the vote sets the timeout bit.  It can justify only if it is also the
exact current-height target, but justification is disabled at this height in
either case.
Every other height is
\emph{ordinary}.

The recovery proof requires a height~\(H\) for which every valid chain reaching
\(H\) that an honest validator can process now or import later has
\(\nj=\mathsf{true}\) in its height-\(H\) state.
This includes hidden historical branches and their authenticated states and
transitions, not only the chains visible during recovery.  Consequently no
valid pre-existing or subsequently assembled justification at height~\(H\)
exists.  While finality
debt continues, one height divisible by~\(K\) occurs every~\(K\) heights, but
seeing one branch enter a nonjustifiable height is not enough.
A concrete protocol must show from authenticated state or transition evidence
that all valid chains entering~\(H\) assign it the same value.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[A nonjustifiable height prevents FG interference]\label{lem:nonjustifiable-fg}
If every valid chain reaching~\(H\) has
\(\nj=\mathsf{true}\) in its height-\(H\) state, no justification at~\(H\)
can form.  Throughout the
period in which \(\Sigma.h_{\max}=H+1\), every honest
Simplex root is its own \(\Sigma.F\); in particular, FG cannot reintroduce a
height-\(H\) justification as the walk root.  If the store already contains,
or later imports, a branch at height~\(H+2\), the only newly eligible
justification root is at height~\(H+1\), not height~\(H\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The state transition never selects the justification branch at~\(H\), even if
compatible locked target repeats reach quorum, so no valid height-\(H\)
justification exists.
Every chain with \(\Sigma.h_{\max}=H+1\) therefore has greatest justified height
at most \(H-1\).  While \(\Sigma.h_{\max}=H+1\), we have
\(\Sigma.h_{\max}\geq\Sigma.h_j+2\), so the condition for selecting~\(J\) is
not met and Definition~\ref{def:simplex-root} selects~\(\Sigma.F\).  The same
reasoning applies after importing any branch that leaves
\(\Sigma.h_{\max}=H+1\), because a height-\(H\) justification remains
impossible.  When \(\Sigma.h_{\max}=H+2\), the Simplex-root rule can instead select only
a height-\(H+1\) justification.  Lemma~\ref{lem:simplex-root-compatible} shows that any such root
used during recovery is compatible with the common safe-confirmed chain once
it has been established.
\end{proof}

While finality debt continues, one height divisible by~\(K\) occurs every
\(K\) heights.  The bound \(K\geq2\) ensures that its successor is ordinary;
otherwise every new height could be nonjustifiable and no new target could be
justified.

\section{SG latest messages and fork choice}\label{sec:forkchoice}

The following assumption states the participation and live-view bounds used by
the liveness proof.

\begin{assumption}[Participation during recovery]\label{ass:recovery-weights}
Let~\(V_{\rm resp}\subseteq V\) be a responsive honest set and write
\(W_{\rm resp}=w(V_{\rm resp})\), with \(W_{\rm resp}\geq q\).
For each round used by the recovery proof, let~\(A\subseteq V\setminus
V_{\rm resp}\) contain every validator outside the responsive set whose data
can affect any honest SG or TSQ view in that round.  Write \(a=w(A)\).  The
exact integer inequality
\(W_{\rm resp}>2a\) holds in every such round.
Every responsive honest validator remains online through the available vote,
TSQ freeze, proposal, SG pre-vote, FG decision, and message relay in every
round used by the recovery proof.
Each responsive honest validator publishes exactly one SG pre-vote in every
round during recovery, including a round whose FG decision is empty.  Each such
pre-vote and its ancestry reach every responsive honest validator before the
freeze that uses it.  It remains live through the corresponding proposal and
available-vote action and is superseded by that validator's next honest
pre-vote before expiry.  The proof never relies on an expired vote.
\end{assumption}

When \(W\) is divisible by three, \(q=2W/3\) and the quorum-intersection
bound is \(W/3\).  All
statements below use the exact integer values or cross-multiplication;
informal fractions are explanatory only.

\begin{definition}[Live latest head vote]\label{def:head-vote}
After the round's Goldfish confirmation step, validator~\(i\) signs one SG
head pre-vote \((i,r,B_i)\).  Its later FG message has
\(v.\headf=B_i\) and repeats the same round.
These are two copies of the same logical head vote, not two SG votes.
For equivocation, however, the protocol compares complete signed messages.  A
validator equivocates by signing two distinct SG pre-votes in one round, or two
distinct FG messages in one round, even if the messages name the same head.  It
also equivocates if its SG pre-vote and FG message name different heads.

Before SG scoring, discard every head vote whose head does not descend from
the store's finalized root.  Retain the signed message as evidence, but do not
use it to determine equivocation, the latest message, or the SG denominator.
Among the remaining messages, for expiry window \(W_{\rm head}>0\), the live
latest message of a non-equivocating validator is its unexpired head vote from
the highest round.  A validator that equivocates among these messages
contributes no latest vote.  In an honest view~\(u\), let
\[
 \Dvote_u=\sum_{i\in\mathcal L_u}w(i),
 \qquad
 \Supp_u(B)=
 \sum_{\{i\in\mathcal L_u:B\preceq\headf_i\}}w(i),
\]
where \(\mathcal L_u\) is the locally live, unslashed, non-equivocating set and
\(\headf_i\) is validator~\(i\)'s live latest head.  Support is subtree-counted.
Latest messages are processed when received and need not be included in a
block.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The two SG grades]\label{def:grades}
The \emph{grade-1 fallback root} \(\Gone(\Sigma,u)\) starts at the
Simplex root~\(\simplexRoot(\Sigma)\) and repeatedly enters the unique viable child~\(c\) with
\(3\Supp_u(c)\geq2\Dvote_u\), stopping when no such child exists.  If
\(\Dvote_u=0\), it returns the Simplex root.

A block~\(C\) is \emph{\(\Gzero\)-clear} in~\(u\)'s view when every block~\(B\)
conflicting with~\(C\) has
\(3\Supp_u(B)<\Dvote_u\).  Equivalently, no conflicting block has
support at least one third of the local live denominator.  When \(\Dvote_u=0\),
grade-0 clearance holds by convention.  The \(2/3\) fallback root and \(1/3\)
conflict veto are used at every height.
\end{definition}

At most one child of a block can meet the grade-1 threshold because the child
subtree supports sum to at most~\(\Dvote_u\).  \(\Gzero\)-clearance is a predicate,
not a selected block.

\begin{assumption}[SG/AC composition safety and Goldfish properties]\label{ass:goldfish}
Write~\(\Omega\) for the available-layer blocks and votes used for one round's
walk and confirmation.  The function
\(\mathsf{goldfishHead}(S,\Omega)\) deterministically chooses a viable
descendant of stable root~\(S\).  Goldfish confirmation is separate from SG and
safe confirmation.  It requires a strict majority of the participating
committee.  A vote for a descendant supports every ancestor back to~\(S\).
The participating set is fixed after the available vote, and confirmation is
evaluated at a later action after one network-delivery bound.  During
recovery, Goldfish satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the available-confirmed heads used when honest validators make the SG
    and FG votes considered here are pairwise compatible, including across
    rounds.  This includes the confirmations underlying every saved target,
    active lock, and pre-existing justification or finalization that can later
    become a Simplex root during the recovery window;
  \item if an honest view confirms~\(C\), and at one post-\(\GST\) available-vote
    step every responsive honest voter starts from a stable root compatible with
    \(C\) and votes for a descendant of~\(C\), then the delayed evaluation
    makes~\(C\) common to all honest views.  At any later step, if every
    responsive honest latest available vote entering the step descends
    from~\(C\), then Goldfish from any honest stable root compatible
    with~\(C\) returns a descendant of~\(C\), and every responsive honest next
    available vote does the same, provided the responsive honest
    participating weight strictly exceeds the adversarial participating
    weight.  This holds regardless of the current proposal.  It therefore
    preserves the premise inductively; in particular, starting from a
    compatible ancestor does not let an arbitrary proposal displace~\(C\);
    and
  \item in a slot with an honest proposer and a common timely proposal
    block~\(B_r\) that descends from every responsive honest stable root and
    from every available-confirmed prefix common to those honest views,
    every responsive honest available voter votes for~\(B_r\).  Delayed
    Goldfish evaluation from any of those roots returns~\(B_r\), or the same
    deterministic descendant when the profile permits one before evaluation.
    If \(c_{\rm honest}\) is the common online honest committee weight and
    \(c_{\rm adv}\) is the committee's total adversarial weight, then
    \(c_{\rm honest}>c_{\rm adv}\).
\end{enumerate}
Any block named by a vote, together with the data needed to validate it, is
available before the vote is processed.  The compatibility
assumption applies only to the heads listed in the first condition.  If the
recovery argument is extended to later rounds, the same conditions must hold
in those rounds as well.
\end{assumption}

The first clause is an external composition-safety premise for the SG and
available-confirmation pair, not a consequence of the healing theorem.  It is
the graded-agreement obligation obtained after ruling out incompatible FG
interference.  A concrete composition proof must discharge it for hidden
certificates as well as newly formed ones; synchrony or a common message view
alone does not imply it.  The results below are conditional on this obligation.
The other clauses are Goldfish liveness and persistence properties.  In
particular, the paper never infers the first clause merely from the existence
of a nonjustifiable height or from TSQ.

The third condition holds separately in each view.  Every honest participating
set contains all common honest committee weight~\(c_{\rm honest}\) and may
contain an arbitrary adversarial subset of weight~\(c_u\leq c_{\rm adv}\).  Hence
\[
  c_{\rm honest}>c_{\rm adv}\geq c_u
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  c_{\rm honest}>\frac{c_{\rm honest}+c_u}{2}.
\]
The honest proposal therefore has a strict local majority in every honest
view.  The participating sets and their denominators need not be identical.
The first condition above provides the only compatibility assumption between
different views.

\begin{definition}[Stable root and walk]\label{def:walk}
At an ordinary round's available-vote action, validator~\(u\) sets
\(\stableRoot_u^r=\Gone(\Sigma,u)\).  At a TSQ synchronization action it
instead uses the frozen/current lock of
Definition~\ref{def:synchronization-root} when that lock is viable in the
action state~\(\Sigma\) and descends from \(\simplexRoot(\Sigma)\); otherwise
it uses \(\Gone(\Sigma,u)\).  When no strict descendant has fixed-\(q\)
support in the selection state, the computed lock is that selection state's
Simplex root.  This
per-round stabilization root is the \emph{stable root}; it always descends
from the Simplex root.  A proposal is eligible only when it descends from the
stable root.  Only an eligible proposal receives any proposal-specific
Goldfish treatment; an ineligible proposal remains stored as an ordinary
block and as evidence but is ignored as the distinguished proposal.  The
proposal itself never replaces the stable root.  From
\(\stableRoot_u^r\), follow
\(\mathsf{goldfishHead}(\stableRoot_u^r,\Omega)\) inside the viable subtree.  If the result
has state-height below \(\Sigma.h_{\max}-1\), continue through viable children
using the same previous-slot participating-vote plurality, with deterministic
ties, until that height bound is met.  The returned block is
\(\mathsf{walk}(\Sigma,\Omega,u;\stableRoot_u^r)\).  The available-vote step
stores the action state~\(\Sigma\) and~\(\stableRoot_u^r\).
Every later walk belonging to that completed action uses the stored state and
root and does not recompute the lock.  Later state changes apply only to later
actions.
\end{definition}

The last step affects only the height of the returned block, not its
confirmation status.  By definition, a viable child exists until the height
bound is reached.

\begin{definition}[Safe confirmation]\label{def:safe-confirm}
Fix an honest view~\(u\), its derived store~\(\Sigma\), Simplex root
\(R=\simplexRoot(\Sigma)\), and Goldfish available-confirmed head
\(\liveConfirmed_u\).  The Simplex root~\(R\) is always safe-confirmed.  If
\(\liveConfirmed_u\) does not descend from~\(R\),
the safe-confirmed head is~\(R\).  Otherwise it is the deepest \(\Gzero\)-clear
block between~\(R\) and~\(\liveConfirmed_u\), or~\(R\) if no strict descendant qualifies.
Every block between~\(R\) and the returned block is also safe-confirmed.  This
rule is used only by FG; it does not change the Goldfish available-confirmed
head.
\end{definition}

The prefix property follows because every branch conflicting with an ancestor
of a \(\Gzero\)-clear block also conflicts with that block.

\begin{assumption}[Post-\(\GST\) delivery and a counted TSQ honest proposer]\label{ass:honest-proposer}
After \(\GST\), honest blocks, available votes, SG pre-votes, FG messages, and the
blocks needed to validate them propagate within~\(\Delta\).  A round is a
\emph{counted TSQ honest-proposer round} when it is scheduled as a TSQ
synchronization round, its first proposer is honest, and:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item before proposal selection, every honest validator reconstructs the
    same proposal-independent selection state~\(\Sigma^\star\), hence the same
    viable candidate tree and Simplex root.  Every responsive honest
    support-round SG head has an effective head in that tree.  At the later
    available-vote action, every honest validator uses the same
    post-proposal action state~\(\Sigma\), and every computed TSQ lock is
    viable there and descends from \(\simplexRoot(\Sigma)\), so no fallback is
    used;
  \item each validator freezes its support-round view at least one delivery
    bound before the proposal.  A signed head or conflicting copy received by
    an honest validator is forwarded.  At selection the proposer separately
    re-gossips every valid support-round head, conflicting copy, named block,
    and ancestry it knows, including messages excluded as equivocations.
    Those messages, the proposal block, and its ancestry reach every honest
    validator at least one delivery bound before the available-vote action;
  \item the proposal block is a viable descendant of both the proposer
    candidate of Definition~\ref{def:synchronization-root} and the proposer's
    current available-confirmed head.  It therefore extends every available
    prefix already common to the responsive honest views.  Every responsive honest
    validator has the same available-layer input~\(\Omega\) before the
    available vote; and
  \item every validator uses the same action schedule through delayed
    confirmation and the SG vote, and no validator switches to unrelated
    local state during those actions.
\end{enumerate}
An implementation that spreads these duties across slots must ensure the same
conditions.  They do not assume a height advance, justification, or finality.
The proposal-descends-candidate-and-current-head and common-\(\Omega\)
conditions are explicit liveness premises: the construction below does not
prove that every honest proposer can satisfy them for every selection-state
candidate tree.
\end{assumption}

\section{Time-shifted-quorum root synchronization}\label{sec:tsq}

A TSQ synchronization round has a public freeze deadline~\(d_F\).  Its
support round is the latest scheduled SG pre-vote action whose publication
deadline is no later than \(d_F-\Delta\).  Each validator first freezes the
support-round heads it has received.  At least one
delivery bound later the proposer selects its candidate from its then-current
view.  At least one further delivery bound later each validator computes a
lock from the intersection of its frozen and current views and performs the
available vote.

A fixed public, unbounded round schedule designates TSQ synchronization
rounds.  The support-round mapping above is fresh and deterministic: it is a
function of public deadlines, not of the proposer's choice or a locally
completed-step history.  Choosing every round as a TSQ round is permitted.
The schedule is independent of a validator's local chain state.  Other rounds
use the ordinary \(\Gone\) stable root.  The healing theorem requires
designated rounds satisfying the counted-round assumptions; the schedule
alone does not make a disrupted network synchronous.

Before candidate selection, each TSQ instance pins a
proposal-independent \emph{selection state}~\(\Sigma^\star\): its block tree,
finality fields, \(h_{\max}\), Simplex root, and viable candidate tree are
fixed for the whole instance.  It is the derived state at the public selection
cutoff immediately before proposal construction, after processing every
earlier-deadline input and before admitting the new proposal or later input.
The proposer and receivers project every support head into this same state in
a counted round.  The proposer then computes its candidate and constructs an
ordinary proposal descending from it.  At the later available-vote
action, the proposal produces the action state~\(\Sigma\).  A computed TSQ
lock is used only when it is viable in~\(\Sigma\) and descends from
\(\simplexRoot(\Sigma)\); otherwise that uncounted action uses its ordinary
\(\Gone(\Sigma,u)\) root.  The counted-round assumption guarantees a common
\(\Sigma^\star\), a common~\(\Sigma\), and no such fallback.  The proposal
does not carry a protocol-selected root, and TSQ changes no consensus-state
field.

\begin{definition}[Candidate tree and effective head]\label{def:candidate-tree}
For a fork-choice state~\(\Sigma\), let
\[
  \mathcal C(\Sigma)
  =\{B\in\viableTree(\Sigma):\simplexRoot(\Sigma)\preceq B\}.
\]
This is a prefix-closed tree rooted at~\(\simplexRoot(\Sigma)\).  For a signed
head message naming~\(X\), its \emph{effective head} is the deepest
\(P\in\mathcal C(\Sigma)\) with \(P\preceq X\).  If no such~\(P\) exists, the
message supplies no TSQ support.  Thus projection may move a head
only to an actual ancestor; it never moves a vote forward or across a fork.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[TSQ candidate and lock]\label{def:synchronization-root}
Fix selection state~\(\Sigma^\star\), its publicly assigned support round~\(r\),
and a view~\(X\) containing
signed round-\(r\) SG pre-votes and the round-head equivocation evidence of
Definition~\ref{def:head-vote}.  Apply that definition's finalized-conflict
filter before testing TSQ usability or equivocation.  A head is eligible
in~\(X\) only when its
named block and ancestry were available by~\(X\)'s deadline and it has an
effective head in~\(\Sigma^\star\).  Later ancestry arrival cannot
retroactively make a frozen head eligible.  A signer is usable in~\(X\)
when~\(X\) contains exactly one eligible logical round head from it and no
other eligible message that constitutes a round equivocation under
Definition~\ref{def:head-vote}.  In particular, two distinct eligible complete
messages exclude the signer even if they name the same block root.  Raw
ineligible copies remain evidence but do not affect TSQ support.  For
\(B\in\mathcal C(\Sigma^\star)\), write
\[
  \mathsf{support}_X(B)
  =
  \sum_{\substack{i\text{ usable in }X\\
                   B\preceq\mathsf{effective\_head}_X(i)}}w(i).
\]
A signer with eligible round-equivocation evidence is excluded and supplies
no TSQ support.

For two views \(X,Y\), a signer is usable in \(X\cap Y\) only when the
\emph{same signed pre-vote} is present and eligible under both views'
deadlines, and neither constituent view contains another eligible message
that constitutes a round equivocation.  Define
\[
  \mathsf{support}_{X\cap Y}(B)
  =
  \sum_{\substack{i\text{ usable in }X\cap Y\\
                   B\preceq\mathsf{effective\_head}(i)}}w(i).
\]
All these tests use the fixed threshold~\(q\), not a local SG denominator.

At proposal time, proposer~\(p\) has current view~\(U_p\) and selects
\[
  C_p
  =
  \text{the deepest }B\in\mathcal C(\Sigma^\star)
  \text{ with }\mathsf{support}_{U_p}(B)\geq q,
\]
or \(C_p=\simplexRoot(\Sigma^\star)\) when no strict descendant qualifies.  A
counted TSQ honest proposer publishes a proposal block~\(P\) descending from
both~\(C_p\) and its current available-confirmed head.

At the later available-vote action, validator~\(u\) has its completed frozen
view~\(F_u\) and current view~\(U_u\).  It sets
\[
  L_u
  =
  \text{the deepest }B\in\mathcal C(\Sigma^\star)
  \text{ with }\mathsf{support}_{F_u\cap U_u}(B)\geq q,
\]
or \(L_u=\simplexRoot(\Sigma^\star)\) when no strict descendant qualifies.
When \(L_u\) passes the action-state viability and Simplex-root checks above,
it is the synchronization round's stable root.  A unique timely
proposal is eligible only when it descends from~\(L_u\).  Two distinct timely
blocks signed by the proposer make neither distinguished as the proposal.
Later messages do not change \(F_u\), \(L_u\), or the completed action.
\end{definition}

The numerical liveness bounds count only TSQ rounds satisfying
Assumption~\ref{ass:honest-proposer}.  If proposal processing changes the
Simplex root so that a computed lock fails the action-state check, if the
required common selection or action state is unavailable, or if the proposer
cannot publish a descendant of its candidate, that round is not counted.  It
may still run safely, but no synchronization or numerical liveness conclusion
is drawn from it.

\begin{lemma}[Quorum-supported roots form a chain]\label{lem:tsq-root-chain}
In one view with at most one usable head per signer, any two roots with
support at least~\(q\) are compatible.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The signer sets supporting conflicting roots are disjoint.  Their combined
weight would be at least \(2q>W\), which is impossible.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Same-instance TSQ locks are compatible]\label{lem:tsq-lock-compatible}
Under the TSQ relay schedule, nondefault locks computed by two honest
validators from the same selection state and eligibility filter in one TSQ
instance are compatible.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each lock has a supporting signer set of weight at least~\(q\), so the sets
intersect because \(2q>W\).  A signer in the intersection cannot have the
same head below two conflicting locks.  If it sent different heads to the two
validators before their freezes, honest relay places both copies in both
current views before the action, and the signer is excluded from both
supports.  Either case contradicts membership in the intersection.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A receiver lock transfers to an honest proposer]\label{lem:tsq-transfer}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:honest-proposer}, every nondefault lock~\(L_u\)
computed by a responsive honest validator has support at least~\(q\) in the
honest proposer's current view~\(U_p\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Take a signer counted for \(L_u\) in \(F_u\cap U_u\).  Validator~\(u\) had the
signed head, its named block, and its ancestry by the freeze and forwarded
them.  The delivery interval before proposal selection places that same
message in~\(U_p\).  If the proposer knew another support-round head from the
same signer that was eligible in~\(U_p\), it separately re-gossiped that
conflicting copy at selection; the delivery interval before the action would
then make it eligible in~\(U_u\) and make~\(u\) exclude the signer.
Thus every signer still counted by~\(u\) is usable with the same head in
\(U_p\).  The whole weight-\(q\) supporting set transfers.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[An honest TSQ candidate extends every receiver lock]\label{lem:tsq-candidate-lock}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:honest-proposer}, the candidate~\(C_p\) of a
counted TSQ honest proposer descends from every responsive honest lock~\(L_u\).
Consequently its proposal~\(P\) descends from every such lock.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The claim is immediate when \(L_u=\simplexRoot(\Sigma^\star)\).  Otherwise
Lemma~\ref{lem:tsq-transfer} makes \(L_u\) a \(q\)-supported root in~\(U_p\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:tsq-root-chain} puts it on the same chain as every other
\(q\)-supported root in that view, and the maximal-depth definition of~\(C_p\)
gives \(L_u\preceq C_p\).  The counted-round construction gives
\(C_p\preceq P\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[An honest TSQ proposer aligns the available vote]\label{lem:tsq-alignment}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:recovery-weights},
\ref{ass:goldfish}, and~\ref{ass:honest-proposer}, every responsive honest
validator in a counted TSQ honest-proposer round makes its available vote on
the proposal~\(P\)'s chain, and delayed evaluation confirms~\(P\) in every
responsive honest view.  The later SG action has one chain, one state-height
\(k\), and one current-height target~\(T\), even if the locks have different
depths or the pre-round walks were split between \(h_{\max}-1\) and
\(h_{\max}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:tsq-candidate-lock} makes the common timely proposal descend
from every responsive honest stable root.  The honest-proposal clause of
Assumption~\ref{ass:goldfish}, together with the common input~\(\Omega\),
therefore makes every responsive honest available voter vote for~\(P\), and
delayed evaluation returns the same head on~\(P\)'s chain.  This is stronger
than mere compatibility of the locks: the
proposal path has a strict local committee majority from every starting
depth.  Delayed evaluation confirms~\(P\).  From the common returned head,
the common action state, available input, and deterministic height-filter
step give the same SG walk output.

The height and current-height target are read from the slot-advanced action
state on that chain rather than from a receiver's pre-round height.  No round
outcome is consumed between the available vote and the SG/FG action.  If the
proposal's operations consume a due outcome and enter a new height, its
post-state has an empty stored target and a start slot equal to the proposal's
slot.  Every validator then derives~\(P\) as the same-slot action target, and
the following slot transition records exactly that root before a descendant
can include the target votes.  Otherwise target uniqueness on one chain gives
the same~\(T\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A quorum crossing the freeze prevents later root regression]\label{lem:tsq-frozen-prefix}
Let \(C\in\mathcal C(\Sigma^\star)\).  If signed heads of weight at least~\(q\)
descend from~\(C\), and those same heads are usable in both \(F_u\) and
\(U_u\), then the TSQ lock~\(L_u\) descends from~\(C\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The root~\(C\) itself has frozen/current support at least~\(q\).  It therefore
qualifies in the deepest-root calculation, so the selected lock cannot be a
strict ancestor.  Lemma~\ref{lem:tsq-root-chain} excludes a conflicting
qualifying root.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[TSQ gives no positive power to subquorum support]\label{lem:tsq-no-active-reorg}
Assume the Goldfish properties of Assumption~\ref{ass:goldfish}.  Suppose a
block~\(C\) is common and Goldfish-confirmed; the selection-state and
action-state Simplex roots are compatible with~\(C\); every responsive honest
support-round head is usable in each relevant frozen/current view, every responsive honest latest SG head is
present and live in each honest view under consideration, and all those heads
descend from~\(C\); every responsive honest available
voter participates and its latest vote entering the action descends
from~\(C\); and the weight
outside the responsive honest set is both less than~\(q\) and less than the
responsive honest weight.  Then the actual stable root, including an ordinary
\(\Gone\) fallback, is compatible with~\(C\), and Goldfish keeps the honest
available votes and head in the subtree of~\(C\).  A sub-\(q\) set of heads
cannot by itself support any strict-descendant TSQ candidate or lock, cannot
support a root conflicting with~\(C\), and no proposal field installs a root.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A root conflicting with~\(C\) can be supported only by the remaining
validators and therefore cannot reach~\(q\).  Since the selection-state
Simplex root is compatible with~\(C\), every TSQ lock is either an ancestor or
a descendant of~\(C\).  If the lock is rejected by the action-state check,
the ordinary \(\Gone\) walk starts at the compatible action-state Simplex
root.  At every fork from the path to~\(C\), a conflicting child has support
at most the outside weight, which is less than half the live weight, and so
cannot meet the two-thirds \(\Gone\) threshold.  The fallback therefore also
remains compatible with~\(C\).  The compatible-ancestor clause of
Assumption~\ref{ass:goldfish} now keeps the available head and votes in
\(C\)'s subtree, regardless of the proposal.  Once responsive honest
descendant heads also cross a freeze and remain usable at its action,
Lemma~\ref{lem:tsq-frozen-prefix} strengthens output-level non-reorg to the
root-level invariant \(C\preceq L_u\).
\end{proof}

Thus a one-third adversary may still veto \(\Gzero\)-clearance and may withhold
enough data to stall a fixed-\(q\) synchronization.  It cannot use that
one-third support as a positive root-selection signal.  The TSQ liveness statements below
require responsive honest weight at least~\(q\).  The weaker, below-one-half
statement is only that a subquorum adversary cannot actively reorg an
otherwise Goldfish-stable chain; it is not a TSQ liveness claim.

\section{Round schedule and state processing}\label{sec:rounds}

FG time is grouped into rounds of one or more consecutive slots; the safety
and liveness arguments do not fix the round length.  The paper does not spread
FG duties across those slots.  It models one quorum-contributing FG action per
validator after the proposal has received Goldfish available votes and been
confirmed.  The strict order is
\[
\begin{array}{c}
\text{support-round SG heads}
\xrightarrow{\ \Delta\ }\text{freeze}
\xrightarrow{\ \Delta\ }\text{candidate selection}
\\[-1mm]
\longrightarrow \text{proposal}
\xrightarrow{\ \Delta\ }\text{TSQ lock and available vote}
\xrightarrow{\ \Delta\ }\text{delayed Goldfish confirmation}
\\[-1mm]
\longrightarrow \text{new SG head pre-vote}
\xrightarrow{\ \Delta\ }\text{FG checkpoint/finality action}
\\[-1mm]
\longrightarrow \text{mark the round's outcome due}
\longrightarrow \text{next-slot inclusion and processing}.
\end{array}
\]
Before a step reads state, it processes every message whose deadline has
passed.
The support SG heads come from the public support-round action defined above.
Their first delivery interval may therefore lie in that preceding round.  The
freeze is an immutable local snapshot.  The next interval transfers every
frozen message to the proposer; the following interval transfers the honest
proposal and all messages and equivocations known to that proposer to the
voters.  Confirmation is evaluated after the available votes, and the new SG
pre-vote is delivered for one more interval before the FG action.  The next
slot supplies the minimum inclusion opportunity for the resulting FG vote.
Every arrow labelled~\(\Delta\) leaves at least one post-\(\GST\) delivery
bound before the receiving action.

When the following round is designated for TSQ, its freeze occurs at the SG
delivery boundary before FG signatures at that boundary.  Thus newly aligned
SG heads can cross the next freeze without adding another voting round.  The
later proposal and lock action still observe the two delivery intervals
required by the TSQ transfer proof.  Consequently, a freeze before an
FG signature is not the same as a TSQ action before that signature.  When the
fresh-recovery proof forbids a height-\(H+2\) signature through the action, the
profile must postpone that FG checkpoint action across those intervals.

When one round is one slot, the support heads may have been sent in the prior
slot, but the current slot still needs the four delivery intervals from
freeze through FG, plus local processing.  If it does not, the actions span
enough slots to preserve the order, and no same-slot liveness bound is
claimed.

\begin{lemma}[Delivery before the next step]\label{lem:delivery-before-action}
After \(\GST\), every honest message consumed by a later network-dependent
step is received by every honest validator before that step.  In particular,
support SG heads arrive before the freeze, every frozen head relayed by an
honest validator arrives before proposal selection, proposer-known
equivocations arrive before the lock action, available votes arrive before
delayed confirmation, new SG pre-votes arrive before the FG action, and all
honest FG messages arrive within one further~\(\Delta\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The schedule leaves at least the post-\(\GST\) delay bound~\(\Delta\) before
each network-dependent action consumes the preceding honest messages, and
processes messages that arrive on time before that action.  Apply this to the
labelled arrows and to the inclusion deadline for FG messages.  A Byzantine
message may arrive before a deadline in some views and after it in others; the
lemma applies only to honestly sent messages.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Round close]\label{def:round-close}
At the boundary after a round, the protocol marks one outcome due.  It consumes
that outcome exactly once after the following slot's operation phase: after
the block's attestations if the slot has a block, or after the empty operation
phase otherwise.  It then checks finality first, current-height target
justification second, and timeout third.  It does not evaluate any branch
earlier using only some of the round's timely votes.
\end{definition}

In every round counted below, responsive honest validators of total weight at
least~\(q\) complete the available, SG, and FG steps, and their votes are
included in the following slot before that round's due outcome is consumed.
If a round's outcome advances the height, before the next SG and FG votes every responsive honest validator uses
the same resulting state and has safe-confirmed a block carrying it.  The
first counted round is a counted TSQ honest-proposer round whose proposal is
common before the available-vote action.  Later counted rounds do not require
honest proposers once the new state and its confirmed chain are common.

This uses the state transition of Section~\ref{sec:state-machine}.  Safety does
not depend on the one-slot inclusion grace.  The liveness bounds assume that
all timely votes are included before the target branch is checked.

\begin{lemma}[Latest-message concentration]\label{lem:head-concentration}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:recovery-weights}, suppose all responsive honest
validators publish their next SG pre-vote on descendants of one chain~\(C\).
One delivery bound later, every honest latest message from the responsive set
lies on~\(C\)'s chain in every honest view.  Every conflicting branch then has
support below one third of the local live denominator, and remains below it
while those honest latest messages stay on the chain.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(a_u\leq a\) be the weight of live latest messages outside the responsive
set in view~\(u\).  The recovery assumption gives
\(W_{\rm resp}>2a\geq2a_u\).  A conflicting branch has support at most~\(a_u\),
while \(\Dvote_u=W_{\rm resp}+a_u\); hence
\(3a_u<W_{\rm resp}+a_u=\Dvote_u\).  Later honest messages only supersede
same-validator stale messages with another descendant of~\(C\).
\end{proof}

A deployment using a nonstandard rounded threshold must check
\(W_{\rm resp}>2a\) directly
with integer weights; it must not infer the inequality from a rounded adjusted
denominator.

\section{Validator voting rule}\label{sec:signer}

An honest validator~\(i\)'s durable local signing history~\(\mathcal H_i\)
records, for
each height~\(h\),
\[
  \mathcal H_i.T(h),\qquad
  \mathcal H_i.\tau(h),\qquad
  \mathcal H_i.\lambda(h).
\]
Here \(\mathcal H_i.T(h)\) is the first nonempty target signed at~\(h\), or
\(\bot\); \(\mathcal H_i.\tau(h)\) records whether the validator has signed a
timeout at~\(h\); and \(\mathcal H_i.\lambda(h)\) records whether it has
committed to finalize \(\mathcal H_i.T(h)\).
It also records its SG and FG messages for every round.  These records are
written before the validator releases a signature.  After a restart, the
validator does not sign until it has recovered them.  A lock retains the
corresponding justification certificate and ancestry;
\(\mathcal H_i.T(h)\) is written at most once, while
\(\mathcal H_i.\tau(h)\) and \(\mathcal H_i.\lambda(h)\) can change only from
false to true.  Once validators
again use the same chain and have confirmed its current-height target, a
validator repeats its saved target if it is on that chain or, if it is not
locked, votes for timeout.

An honest validator makes a target or timeout vote at height~\(k\) only from
the slot-advanced action state on its SG walk's chain after that state has
reached~\(k\).  In particular, signing at height \(k\) proves that the signer
previously reached that height.  The record is retained across restarts and
checkpoint sync.

\begin{definition}[Current-height target]\label{def:current-height-target}
Let \(W_r\) be the walk output chosen by the round's SG pre-vote.  Let
\(\sigma_r\) be the state obtained by advancing \(\sigma[W_r]\) through the
scheduled slot processing up to the FG action, on \(W_r\)'s chain.  Set
\(k=\sigma_r.h\).  Normally
\(T=\sigma_r.\currentHeightTarget=T_k\).  If that field is empty and
\(W_r.\slot\geq\sigma_r.\currentHeightStartSlot\), set \(T=W_r\): this is the
same-slot case in which \(W_r\)'s post-state entered~\(k\), but its canonical
root cannot appear inside that same post-state.  The next slot transition will
store the same root.  Otherwise set \(T=\bot\).  When \(T\neq\bot\), it is the
first block of height~\(k\) on that chain and supplies the candidate target
vote~\((k,T)\), which need not name the round's stable root or proposal.
Validators first choose their walk, then derive its action-state height and
target.  A vote signed in the target block's slot cannot be included in that
block; a descendant includes it only after the stored field is populated.
Write \(\mathsf{get\_current\_height\_target}(\sigma_r,W_r)\) for this
stored-target-or-same-slot-head rule.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Same-slot target materialization]\label{lem:same-slot-target}
Suppose the same-slot rule of Definition~\ref{def:current-height-target}
returns block~\(B\) at height~\(k\) while
\(\sigma_r.\currentHeightTarget=\bot\).  Before a descendant can process
a target vote for~\((k,B)\), slot processing records
\(\currentHeightTarget=B\).  If the chain has already advanced beyond~\(k\),
the vote is stale and changes neither participation bit.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The rule implies \(B.\slot\geq\sigma_r.\currentHeightStartSlot\).  The vote
cannot be included in~\(B\) itself.  Before processing a descendant block, the
state transition processes~\(B\)'s slot, fills its cached state root, and
records its canonical block root as the stored target.  That happens before
the descendant's attestations.  A prior height advance instead resets the
target and both participation arrays, and the vote's old height then fails the
height-validity check.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Finality vote]\label{def:finality-vote}
Let \((J,h_j)\) and \((F,h_F)\) be the latest justified and finalized pairs in
the chain state used by the FG action.  An honest validator sets
\((v.\finalizeHeight,v.\finalizeTarget)=(h_j,J)\) exactly when
\(h_j>h_F\), \(F\preceq J\), and:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \(\mathcal H_i.T(h_j)=J\);
  \item \(\mathcal H_i.\tau(h_j)=\mathsf{false}\);
  \item it knows the justification certificate for \(J\); and
  \item \(\mathcal H_i.\lambda(h_j)\) is false or already locks that same
    target.
\end{enumerate}
Before signing, it records
\(\mathcal H_i.\lambda(h_j)\gets\mathsf{true}\) and
retains the certificate.  Otherwise it sets
\[
  (v.\finalizeHeight,v.\finalizeTarget)=(\bot,\bot).
\]
A newer justification becomes the only eligible finality
commitment and resets the previous justification's partial finality tally.
The rule finalizes only the
latest justified block.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[FG voting rule]\label{def:fg-rule}
At the FG action, let \((k,T)\) be derived from the SG walk chosen earlier in
the round, and write~\(C_i\) for validator~\(i\)'s safe-confirmed head.  After independently
choosing its finalize pair, validator~\(i\) emits an empty voted checkpoint if
\(T=\bot\).  Otherwise it chooses
\((v.\height,v.\target)\)
in this order:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item If \(\mathcal H_i.\lambda(k)=\mathsf{true}\), let
    \(T_L=\mathcal H_i.T(k)\) and repeat target~\(T_L\) iff
    \(C_i\succeq T_L\); otherwise emit empty.  The vote may be
    included only on a descendant of~\(T_L\).  If \(T_L=T\), it sets the
    target-participation bit; whether or not it is canonical, it sets the
    timeout bit when included on that chain.  At a nonjustifiable height it
    cannot justify.  A locked
    validator never signs timeout at~\(k\).
  \item If \(\mathcal H_i.\tau(k)=\mathsf{true}\), repeat timeout iff
    \(\sigma[C_i].h\geq k\); otherwise emit empty.
  \item If \(\mathcal H_i.T(k)=T_0\neq\bot\) and
    \(\mathcal H_i.\lambda(k)=\mathsf{false}\), repeat
    target~\(T_0\) iff \(C_i\succeq T_0\).  Such a vote may be
    included only on a descendant of~\(T_0\).  Otherwise sign timeout iff
    \(\sigma[C_i].h\geq k\); otherwise emit empty.  Thus a validator repeats a
    saved target that is on the current chain, and switches to timeout if that
    target is not on the chain.  At a nonjustifiable height an on-chain repeat
    sets the timeout bit but cannot justify.  Signing the timeout
    permanently forfeits finality eligibility for the old target.
  \item With no history at a nonjustifiable height, sign timeout iff
    \(\sigma[C_i].h\geq k\); otherwise emit empty.
  \item With no history at an ordinary height, sign target~\(T\) iff
    \(C_i\succeq T\); otherwise sign timeout iff
    \(\sigma[C_i].h\geq k\); otherwise emit empty.
\end{enumerate}
A new target writes \(\mathcal H_i.T(k)=T\) before signing; a timeout sets
\(\mathcal H_i.\tau(k)=\mathsf{true}\).  Every FG message has
\(v.\headf=W_r\), repeating the already-signed SG head.  An empty vote has
\((v.\height,v.\target)=(\bot,\bot)\) and contributes to neither a target nor
a timeout quorum, but still carries that head and the independently computed
finalize pair.
\end{definition}

The state bit \(\timeouts[i]\) is a height-progress marker, not the validator's
durable signing record \(\mathcal H_i.\tau(k)\).  Processing a nonempty on-chain
target may set \(\timeouts[i]\), but only signing an explicit
empty-target timeout sets \(\mathcal H_i.\tau(k)\) and forfeits finality
eligibility.

Timeouts require confirmation of the height, but do not require an additional
grade-0 check.  Once a validator safely confirms the current-height target, it
contributes either a target or a timeout.  If it has not confirmed that height,
it emits an empty vote.  This does not exempt it from a stall penalty.

\begin{lemma}[Honest votes are not slashable]\label{lem:signer-safety}
The validator of Definition~\ref{def:fg-rule} never violates E1 or E2.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first-target entry can be set only once, so the validator never signs two
distinct targets.  It may sign timeout after a target that cannot be repeated on the
current confirmed chain only while no lock exists; after doing so it can never
make a finality vote at that height.  Once a finality
commitment creates a lock, the validator either repeats the exact locked target or
emits empty.  It never signs a conflicting target or timeout after the
commitment.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Using earlier votes and certificates]\label{def:earlier-votes}
While a height can affect an honest fork choice, every honest validator
re-sends:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item every saved target or timeout vote that is still valid on a branch an
    honest validator may follow, including one from a past local height that
    is still current on a competing chain;
  \item the blocks, ancestry, and signed evidence needed to validate the
    branch's latest justified/finalized state, plus any justification
    certificate retained by an active lock; and
  \item every live SG head message, named block, and ancestry needed for the
    latest-message and TSQ frozen/current checks.
\end{enumerate}
Votes whose referenced blocks are not yet known are queued and processed once
those blocks arrive.  A signature
does not affect a chain's consensus state until it is included in a block
whose transition accepts it; a certificate by itself does not replace the
branch's latest justified pair.  The validator may discard an entry only once
every honest validator has finalized the same root and no competing branch at
or below that height can affect protocol choices.  Advancing its local
height is not enough.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[A first justification has an available confirmation]\label{lem:first-jc-confirmation}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:fixed-electorate}, let
\(\mathsf{JC}(h,T)\) be the first justification certificate formed at
height~\(h>0\), with \(T\neq\genesis\).  It contains an honest signer whose signing action
Goldfish-confirmed a descendant of~\(T\).  Every later justification
certificate at the same height has the same target unless the accountable
fault bound is violated.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The certificate has weight at least~\(q>b\), so it contains an honest signer.
Before the first height-\(h\) certificate forms, no height-\(h\) justification
can be the action's Simplex root.  The action's finalized or justified floor
therefore has finality height below~\(h\).  The honest target-voting rule
requires its safe-confirmed head, and hence~\(T\), to descend from that floor.
Current-height target validity puts \(T\) at state-height~\(h\), so the descent
is strict.
An honest target vote for~\(T\) requires a safe-confirmed head at or above
\(T\).  Since only the Simplex root is safe-confirmed by convention, a strict
descendant can be safe-confirmed only when the action's Goldfish
available-confirmed head descends from it.  Thus this signer
Goldfish-confirmed a descendant of~\(T\).
Lemmas~\ref{lem:target-uniqueness} and
\ref{lem:chain-target-uniqueness} give the last claim: two distinct certified
targets can be neither conflicting nor distinct compatible current-height
targets.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Locked validators still contribute to height advance]\label{lem:lock-alignment}
Suppose that, during recovery, all responsive honest validators use one chain
at height~\(k\), derive its current-height target~\(T\), and safely confirm
that target before voting.  Assume the SG/AC composition-safety premise of
Assumption~\ref{ass:goldfish} covers the confirmation underlying every active
lock, and assume \(b<2q-W\).  If an honest validator is locked at~\(k\), then
either the chain has already advanced beyond~\(k\), or the validator's saved
target lies on the safe-confirmed chain and its earlier or repeated target
vote contributes to the timeout quorum.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A lock at~\(k\) was created only after the validator voted for~\(T_L\) and
learned \(\mathsf{JC}(k,T_L)\).  If that target quorum has already been
processed on the current chain, the height has advanced.  Otherwise the common
walk is at height~\(k\) and its safely confirmed target is~\(T\).

If \(T=\genesis\), target validity gives \(k=1\) and
\(T=T_L=\genesis\).  Otherwise, if \(T\) is the Simplex root, that non-genesis
root is justified or finalized at height~\(k\): the same block cannot be the
current-height target at~\(k\) and a certified target at another height.
Its certificate \(\mathsf{JC}(k,T)\) and the retained
\(\mathsf{JC}(k,T_L)\), together with target uniqueness and
chain-target uniqueness, give \(T=T_L\).
Otherwise, Goldfish confirmed a descendant of~\(T\).  The retained JC for
\(T_L\) has the target of the first height-\(k\) JC by
Lemma~\ref{lem:first-jc-confirmation}; one of that certificate's honest
signers Goldfish-confirmed a descendant of~\(T_L\).
Assumption~\ref{ass:goldfish} makes these confirmations compatible.
In either case, exact-target validity of the retained JC and
inheritance of the height state imply \(T_L=T\) while both chains remain
at~\(k\), and hence \(T_L\preceq C_i\).  The broader timeout predicate needs
only this ancestry conclusion: the original target vote contributes to the
timeout quorum if it has already been included on this chain; otherwise the
validator repeats~\(T_L\).  It never needs to vote for timeout.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Leak-fairness consequence]\label{rem:leak-fairness}
The target bit does not assume that competing chains have one shared target.
Each chain counts only its own current-height target toward justification.
Once a counted TSQ honest proposer
makes all responsive honest validators use the same chain, an unlocked
validator repeats a saved target on that chain.  If its saved target is not on
the chain, it may vote for timeout because it has made no finality commitment.
A locked validator cannot vote for timeout, but
Lemma~\ref{lem:lock-alignment} shows that its justified target lies on the
chain now used by honest validators.  Its target therefore sets the timeout
bit whether or not equality with the chain's current-height target is needed.
The independent timeout bit therefore preserves every honest validator's
contribution to the timeout quorum.  This state transition does not set
\(\mathcal H_i.\tau\): the validator has repeated its saved nonempty target,
not signed an explicit timeout.
\end{remark}

\section{Healing and finality liveness}\label{sec:healing}

We first show that two height advances from a nonjustifiable height establish
a common confirmed prefix, and that a fresh TSQ action then makes every later
stable root descend from it.  We then give the additional conditions under
which a new justification forms and finality resumes.  Stabilizing the
available chain does not by itself imply finality.

In this section, a \emph{shared store}~\(\Sigma\) means that every responsive
honest validator uses~\(\Sigma\) as input to the next protocol step.  A
validator may retain additional evidence that cannot affect that step.

\begin{assumption}[Message delivery and proposer fairness]\label{ass:recovery-delivery}
After \(\GST\), every message sent by an honest validator in one step arrives at
every honest validator before the next step and before the deadline at which
it is used.  Honest current-height and finality votes are included on the
common chain before the applicable round's due outcome is consumed.  There is
a future counted TSQ honest-proposer round satisfying
Assumption~\ref{ass:honest-proposer}.  After each common height transition,
another such counted TSQ honest proposer eventually proposes a descendant of the
new state.

The numerical round bounds require this timely processing in every counted
round, with the additional conditions stated after
Definition~\ref{def:round-close}.  Without continuous timely processing, the
same argument gives eventual height advance by waiting for a counted TSQ honest
proposal after each transition.  Eventual finality then requires a later ordinary
height above every height previously reached or signed at by an honest
validator, reached before any honest validator votes at that height.  Its
target votes and the following finality votes must also be included by their
respective deadlines.  Recurring counted TSQ honest proposers alone do not
guarantee such a height, because votes may be cast there before the chain
becomes common.
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[Common target before FG voting]\label{def:common-target}
For round~\(r\), let \(W_i^r\) be honest validator~\(i\)'s SG walk output,
and let \(\sigma_i^r\) be its slot-advanced action state as in
Definition~\ref{def:current-height-target}.  Set
\(k_i^r=\sigma_i^r.h\), and let
\(T_i^r=\mathsf{get\_current\_height\_target}(\sigma_i^r,W_i^r)\).  The round
has a \emph{common target \((k,T)\)} when every responsive honest validator
has \(k_i^r=k\), \(T_i^r=T\neq\bot\), and all \(W_i^r\) lie on one chain.  The target
is \emph{confirmed before FG voting} when every responsive honest validator's
safe-confirmed head lies on that chain at height at least~\(k\).  Every such
head then descends~\(T\).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[A counted TSQ honest proposer gives validators a common confirmed target]\label{lem:honest-proposer-confirmation}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:recovery-weights}, \ref{ass:goldfish},
and~\ref{ass:honest-proposer}, a post-\(\GST\)
counted TSQ honest-proposer round has a common target that is confirmed before the
round's FG action, after processing any preceding due outcome in the proposal
block.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:tsq-alignment} gives one chain, height~\(k\), and
current-height target~\(T\).  Let~\(B_r\) be the honest proposal block.  The
state used for the proposal includes every signed message received by the
applicable deadline.  Its operation phase then consumes any outcome due from
the preceding round.  If that advances the height, \(B_r\)'s post-state enters
the new height and the same-slot target rule gives \(T=B_r\).  Otherwise the
stored target satisfies \(T\preceq B_r\).  In either case every honest available voter votes for
\(B_r\) or a descendant.  Write~\(c_u\leq c_{\rm adv}\) for the adversarial
weight in one honest view.  Assumption~\ref{ass:goldfish} gives
\(c_{\rm honest}>c_{\rm adv}\), hence
\(c_{\rm honest}>(c_{\rm honest}+c_u)/2\).  Delayed evaluation therefore confirms~\(B_r\), and its
ancestor~\(T\), in every honest view without requiring equal denominators.

Every responsive honest validator then publishes its SG pre-vote on that chain.
One delivery bound later Lemma~\ref{lem:head-concentration} makes every
conflicting branch strictly sub-\(1/3\), so~\(T\) is grade-0 clear as well as
available-confirmed.  Thus every safe-confirmed head has reached the height
before the FG action.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A common confirmed target advances the height]\label{lem:aligned-height-advance}
Consider a round that satisfies Assumptions~\ref{ass:fixed-electorate}
and~\ref{ass:goldfish}.  Suppose it has a
common target~\((k,T)\), that target is confirmed before FG voting, and the
responsive honest votes together with all unresolved older votes that they
retransmit are included before the round's due outcome is consumed.  The responsive honest
validators have total weight at least~\(q\).  Then their accepted votes set
their timeout bits at~\(k\), and the round advances the height exactly
once: by justification if one target has weight~\(q\), and otherwise by
timeout.  At an ordinary height, if no honest validator previously timed out
and no honest validator voted for a target other than~\(T\), then~\(T\) is
justified.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Partition the responsive honest validators by their previous votes at
height~\(k\).  A previously processed target or timeout already set the
validator's timeout bit whenever its target was empty or lay on the including
chain.
Validators that previously timed out repeat their timeout.  An unlocked old
target that lies on the common chain is repeated and counts toward timeout,
whether or not it equals the current-height target~\(T\); one that does not
lie on the current confirmed chain votes for timeout.  For a lock,
Lemma~\ref{lem:lock-alignment} says either the height has
already advanced or the saved target lies on the chain, and its old or
repeated vote sets the timeout bit.  A validator that has not yet voted at~\(k\) targets~\(T\) at an
ordinary height and times out at a nonjustifiable height.  Confirmation
of~\(T\) makes every validator satisfy the corresponding condition in
Definition~\ref{def:fg-rule}, so none emits an empty vote.

If \(T\) was derived from a same-slot transition block whose stored target was
still empty, Lemma~\ref{lem:same-slot-target} records~\(T\) before any of these
new target votes can be processed.  Thus the exact-target predicate counts
them toward justification on the common chain; older noncanonical targets need
only satisfy the independent on-chain timeout predicate.

Every responsive honest validator's accepted vote therefore sets its timeout
bit, and their
total weight is at least~\(q\).  By assumption, the old votes and the newly
repeated targets or timeouts are processed before that due outcome.  The state
transition first checks for a target quorum and then
for a timeout quorum, and increments the height only once.  Under the final
conditions of the lemma, every previous honest target is~\(T\), every honest
validator that has not yet voted targets~\(T\), and no honest validator has
timed out.  Target support therefore reaches~\(q\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[One counted TSQ honest-proposer round advances one height]\label{cor:honest-round-advance}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:fixed-electorate}, \ref{ass:recovery-weights}, \ref{ass:goldfish},
\ref{ass:honest-proposer}, and~\ref{ass:recovery-delivery}, a post-\(\GST\)
counted TSQ honest-proposer round advances the common height when the round outcome
is consumed, unless a certificate has already advanced it.  The advance need
not be a justification: if validators previously cast both target and timeout
votes at that height, only the timeout quorum may reach~\(q\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Combine Lemmas~\ref{lem:honest-proposer-confirmation}
and~\ref{lem:aligned-height-advance}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[At most one round is needed to reach \(h_{\max}\)]\label{cor:height-catchup}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:fixed-electorate}, \ref{ass:recovery-weights}, \ref{ass:goldfish},
\ref{ass:honest-proposer}, and~\ref{ass:recovery-delivery}, suppose the shared
store after processing a proposal has
\(\Sigma.h_{\max}=H\), so the
height-filter postcondition permits an honest walk only at~\(H-1\) or~\(H\).
A counted TSQ honest-proposer round gives all responsive honest validators a
common height and its due outcome advances it.  If that height is~\(H-1\), one
voting round brings the common chain to~\(H\) when its outcome is consumed.
The next timely round, or the next counted TSQ honest proposal after the new state
is common, acts at~\(H\) unless a certificate has already advanced it.
Validators therefore need not all be voting at~\(H\) before that counted
proposal.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:tsq-alignment} derives one height from the common
post-proposal walk.  Corollary~\ref{cor:honest-round-advance} advances that
height exactly once.  The next timely round, or the next counted TSQ honest
proposal, starts from the resulting common state.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A timeout quorum contains an honest confirmation]\label{lem:timeout-honest-confirmation}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:fixed-electorate}, whenever a timeout quorum advances
height~\(h\), at least one honest validator in that quorum safe-confirmed a
block at height~\(h\) before voting.  Empty votes do not count.  Since the same
timeout vote may be included on sibling chains at the same height,
the timeout quorum alone does not identify the current-height target of the
chain that includes it.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The timeout certificate has weight at least~\(q\), while adversarial weight is
strictly below the quorum-intersection bound and in particular below~\(q\).
It therefore contains an honest vote.  Every honest target vote
requires a safe-confirmed head descending its named target on the selected
chain; every honest timeout requires the safe-confirmed head to have reached
height~\(h\) in the signing view.  An empty vote has no height and is excluded from the
timeout quorum.  A timeout therefore proves that an honest signer confirmed some
block at height~\(h\), but not which target belongs to the chain that later
includes the timeout.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Votes advancing a common height share a confirmed target]\label{lem:common-target-confirmation}
Suppose a round satisfying Assumptions~\ref{ass:fixed-electorate}
and~\ref{ass:goldfish} has common
target~\((k,T)\), that~\(T\) is confirmed before FG voting, and responsive
honest votes of weight at least~\(q\) are processed before the round's due
outcome is consumed.
Each of these votes is a target or timeout vote cast with a safe-confirmed
head descending from~\(T\).  Thus every responsive honest signer has
safe-confirmed~\(T\), and~\(T\) remains a common prefix whether the height
advances by justification or timeout.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every responsive honest safe-confirmed head lies on the common chain at height
at least~\(k\), hence above its authenticated stored or deterministically
derived current-height target~\(T\).
For a lock, Lemma~\ref{lem:lock-alignment} gives either an already
completed transition or equality with~\(T\); in the nonadvanced case
Definition~\ref{def:fg-rule} makes it repeat that same~\(T\).  A validator that
previously timed out, or whose saved target is incompatible, signs a new
timeout only after the safe confirmation in the premise.  A validator with a
compatible saved target repeats it, and a validator with no previous vote
chooses the common target or timeout according to the height class.  Thus each
current message sets the
timeout bit while its signer safe-confirms~\(T\).  Their recorded weight is at
least~\(q\), so they themselves form a timeout quorum even if older target or
timeout votes are also present.  Whether the target or timeout branch advances the
height does not change the common confirmed prefix established by these votes.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Later Simplex roots are compatible with a safe-confirmed block]\label{lem:simplex-root-compatible}
Let~\(C\) be safe-confirmed during an interval in which
Assumption~\ref{ass:goldfish} holds.  Under \(b<2q-W\), every Simplex root
selected at that or a later action in the same interval is compatible
with~\(C\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For every non-genesis justified block used as a Simplex root,
Lemma~\ref{lem:first-jc-confirmation} supplies an honest Goldfish confirmation
of a descendant of that target.  A later certificate at the same height has
the same target under the accountable fault bound.  Every non-genesis
finalized block was previously justified and has the same property.

If~\(C\) lies strictly above its action's Simplex-root floor, the current
available-confirmed head confirms~\(C\).  If~\(C\) is the floor, then it is
genesis or a justified or finalized root with such a prior confirmation.
The external composition-safety clause of Assumption~\ref{ass:goldfish} makes these confirmations
pairwise compatible, so their ancestors are compatible with~\(C\).  This
applies to every justification selected during the interval.  Since a Simplex
root is either~\(F\) or~\(J\), later updates to either value preserve the
result.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Fresh recovery action]\label{def:fresh-recovery-action}
Let~\(\Sigma_2\) be the first shared store with
\(\Sigma_2.h_{\max}=H+2\) after the two recovery advances.  A later TSQ action
is \emph{fresh at \(H+2\)} when:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the height-\(H+2\) transition state, the common confirmed prefix~\(C\),
    an accepted \(C\)-descendant of state-height at least~\(H+1\), and all
    required ancestry are delivered before its selection cutoff;
  \item every responsive honest selection and action view has
    \(h_{\max}=H+2\); and
  \item before that action is completed, no responsive honest validator has
    signed a target or timeout at height~\(H+2\) or above.
\end{enumerate}
Merely receiving, storing, or replaying the height-\(H+2\) transition state
does not violate freshness.  The definition places an explicit action barrier
after the transition state becomes common and before any responsive honest FG
signature uses that height.  Since
\(w(V\setminus V_{\rm resp})\leq W-q<q\), freshness also excludes a
height-advance or justification quorum at height~\(H+2\) or above before the
action.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Frozen SG votes keep later stable roots in the confirmed subtree]\label{lem:descendant-stable-root}
Consider one TSQ action.  In every responsive honest view~\(u\), let
\(\Sigma_u^\star\) and~\(\Sigma_u\) be its selection and action states, with
the same \(h_0=\Sigma_u.h_{\max}\).  Before these actions complete, suppose no
responsive honest validator has signed a target or timeout at height~\(h_0\)
or above.  Let~\(C\) lie on their common safe-confirmed chain.  In each
selection state, suppose either
\[
  C\preceq\simplexRoot(\Sigma_u^\star)
  \quad\text{or}\quad
  C\in\mathcal C(\Sigma_u^\star).
\]
In each action state, suppose either
\[
  C\preceq\simplexRoot(\Sigma_u)
  \quad\text{or}\quad
  \simplexRoot(\Sigma_u)\preceq C
\]
and an accepted descendant of~\(C\) has state-height at least~\(h_0-1\).
At this instance's freeze, suppose every responsive honest support-round SG
head descends from~\(C\).  Their total weight is
\(W_{\rm resp}\geq q\).  In every view whose selection-state Simplex root is
an ancestor of~\(C\), each such head is usable in the frozen view and remains
usable in the corresponding current view.  Every later TSQ instance uses the
public fresh support-round mapping of Section~\ref{sec:tsq}.  Every stable root
used by a responsive honest validator in this or a later recovery round equals
or descends from~\(C\), provided
Assumptions~\ref{ass:fixed-electorate}, \ref{ass:recovery-weights},
and~\ref{ass:goldfish} continue to hold.  Every resulting honest walk,
available-confirmed head, safe-confirmed head, and SG pre-vote also equals or
descends from~\(C\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We prove jointly, in action order, that every Simplex root is compatible
with~\(C\), that~\(C\) remains viable whenever that root is an ancestor, and
that the stable roots, walks, confirmations, and fresh responsive honest heads
remain in its subtree.  The two disjunctions and the accepted descendant
establish the base case without requiring~\(C\) to belong to a candidate tree
whose root already descends from~\(C\).

Consider an action at or after the stated freeze.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:simplex-root-compatible}, the Simplex roots of both its
selection state, when it is a TSQ action, and its action state are compatible
with~\(C\).  If either root descends from~\(C\), every candidate-tree
descendant of that root also descends from~\(C\).  Otherwise the root is an
ancestor of~\(C\), and viability puts~\(C\) in that state's candidate tree.

In a TSQ action, the publicly assigned fresh SG support round supplies
responsive honest heads descending from~\(C\).  Assumption~\ref{ass:recovery-weights}
makes their total weight at least~\(q\); in an ancestor-root view, the stated
eligibility and relay premises make that weight usable in the frozen/current
pair.  If the selection-state Simplex root is an ancestor of~\(C\),
Lemma~\ref{lem:tsq-frozen-prefix} makes the lock descend from~\(C\); if that
root already descends from~\(C\), so does every possible lock.  If the lock
passes the action-state checks, it is therefore a descendant stable root.

It remains to cover an ordinary action or a TSQ lock that falls back to
\(\Gone\).  Every responsive honest latest head descends from~\(C\).  Each
child on the path to~\(C\) therefore has support at least
\(W_{\rm resp}\), while the local denominator is at most
\(W_{\rm resp}+a\).  The inequality \(W_{\rm resp}>2a\) gives
\[
  3W_{\rm resp}>2(W_{\rm resp}+a),
\]
so, when the action-state Simplex root is an ancestor of~\(C\), the
\(\Gone\) walk reaches~\(C\) and can continue only through its descendants.
When that root already descends from~\(C\), the claim is immediate.

Goldfish starts at a descendant stable root, so
Assumption~\ref{ass:goldfish} makes the walk and the next responsive honest SG
heads descend from~\(C\).  The resulting available-confirmed head descends
from~\(C\).  Let~\(a_u\leq a\) be the actual outside live weight in view~\(u\).
Every conflicting branch has SG support at most~\(a_u\), while
\(\Dvote_u=W_{\rm resp}+a_u\).  The inequality
\(W_{\rm resp}>2a\geq2a_u\) gives
\(3a_u<\Dvote_u\), so~\(C\) is \(\Gzero\)-clear.  The safe-confirmed head
therefore also equals or descends from~\(C\), even when the action's Simplex
root is a strict ancestor.

Because each TSQ action uses the latest
schedule-eligible SG round, their timely relay supplies fresh descendant heads
to the next relevant frozen and current views; no later instance can replay a
pre-\(C\) support round.  It remains to preserve viability if a later
selection or action state raises \(h_{\max}\) to~\(x\).
A block first reaching state-height~\(x\)
contains or descends from a quorum that advanced height~\(x-1\).  Weight
outside \(V_{\rm resp}\) is at most \(W-W_{\rm resp}\leq W-q<q\), so that
  quorum contains a responsive honest signer.  Since \(x>h_0\), the quorum's
  height \(x-1\geq h_0\).  The premise excludes a pre-action responsive
  signature at that height, so this responsive signer acted after the base
actions.  Whether the transition used target or timeout, the signing rule
requires that signer to have safe-confirmed an accepted block at height at
least~\(x-1\); for timeout this is also
Lemma~\ref{lem:timeout-honest-confirmation}.  The induction hypothesis makes
that block a descendant of~\(C\).  The vote need not be bound to the branch
that included it: viability needs the signer's confirmed \(C\)-descendant,
not inclusion on the advancing branch.  Data delivery makes that block and
its ancestry available before the next selection or action state uses the
raised height, so~\(C\) remains viable.  This completes the joint induction.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Two advances and a fresh TSQ action stabilize the available chain]\label{thm:available-healing}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:fixed-electorate}, \ref{ass:recovery-weights}, \ref{ass:goldfish},
\ref{ass:honest-proposer}, and~\ref{ass:recovery-delivery}, let every valid
branch reaching~\(H\), including one imported after recovery
begins, have \(\nj=\mathsf{true}\) in its height-\(H\) state, and suppose
either:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item a round has a common target at~\(H\), that target is confirmed before
    FG voting, and the responsive honest votes are processed before that
    round's due outcome; or
  \item a valid timeout certificate has already advanced~\(H\), and all
    responsive honest validators use its resulting height-\(H+1\) state.
\end{enumerate}
Suppose these assumptions continue through every later step considered here.
A counted TSQ honest proposer at~\(H\) gives case~(a) unless it observes that
case~(b) has already occurred.  Then the common height eventually advances
\[
  H\longrightarrow H+1\longrightarrow H+2.
\]
Let~\(\Sigma_2\) be the resulting first shared store with
\(\Sigma_2.h_{\max}=H+2\).
After the SG votes establishing~\(C\) are delivered, there is a common
safe-confirmed prefix~\(C\).  Normally \(C=T_{H+1}\).  If a certificate has
already advanced~\(H+1\), let~\(C\) be the common target in the first later
round whose action state is shared and in which the target is confirmed before
FG voting.  Let the responsive honest SG heads descending from~\(C\) cross a
TSQ freeze and remain usable at its action, and require that action to be fresh
at~\(H+2\) in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:fresh-recovery-action}.  In the
latter case, the profile must postpone that round's height-\(H+2\) FG
checkpoint action until after this TSQ action; placing only the freeze before
the FG action is insufficient.  Then every stable root and available head
from the corresponding TSQ action onward descends from~\(C\).  Between the common
confirmation and that action, ordinary Goldfish already prevents an arbitrary
proposal from reorging~\(C\), but the stronger descendant-root invariant is
not claimed yet.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In case~(b), the first transition is already complete.  Since justification is
disabled at the nonjustifiable height~\(H\), it occurred by timeout.  In
case~(a), Lemma~\ref{lem:aligned-height-advance} gives a timeout quorum and the
round advances to~\(H+1\) by timeout, unless a certificate has already made
the same transition.  In either case, the next step starts from a common state
at height~\(H+1\).

On the block path, the block~\(B\) that consumes the first due outcome has a
height-\(H+1\) post-state and is itself~\(T_{H+1}\).  If that next round meets
the timing conditions stated after Definition~\ref{def:round-close} and the
one-slot condition in Section~\ref{sec:rounds}, Goldfish confirms~\(B\) before
that round's SG and FG votes, so those votes can already target~\(B\) in the
same slot.  On the empty path, or without continuous
timely processing, wait for the first later counted TSQ honest proposal after the
transition state is common.  That proposal is the first later block and hence
the target; it descends from the common state by
Assumption~\ref{ass:recovery-delivery}.  Unless a certificate
has already advanced the height, its common height is~\(H+1\), and
Lemma~\ref{lem:honest-proposer-confirmation} confirms its common target before FG
voting.  In either case, all responsive honest validators have one chain,
height, and action target, and the delivered SG pre-votes make the target
grade-0 clear before the FG action.

If no intervening certificate has yet advanced~\(H+1\),
Lemmas~\ref{lem:aligned-height-advance}
and~\ref{lem:common-target-confirmation} advance it to~\(H+2\) and give
\(C=T_{H+1}\).  If a certificate has already made that transition, use the
next round meeting the conditions after Definition~\ref{def:round-close}, or
wait for the next counted TSQ honest proposer.  Its common target is confirmed before FG
voting by the round's timing or by Lemma~\ref{lem:honest-proposer-confirmation}; take
that target as~\(C\).  The two height advances are already complete in this
case, and the SG votes establish the same property for~\(C\).

By Lemma~\ref{lem:nonjustifiable-fg}, while
\(h_{\max}=H+1\), no height-\(H\) justification exists and the Simplex root
is~\(F\), so FG
cannot interfere with the available walk.  Once \(h_{\max}=H+2\), any
eligible height-\(H+1\) justification root is compatible with honest safe confirmations by
Lemma~\ref{lem:simplex-root-compatible}.  In both branches, the theorem's
construction gives a common target~\(C\) confirmed before its source round's
FG action, in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:common-target}; the delayed
branch postpones that FG action but not the preceding confirmation or SG
pre-vote.  The Goldfish persistence assumption and those SG pre-votes keep
votes on descendants of~\(C\).
The height-filtered common walk that establishes~\(C\) is an accepted
descendant of~\(C\) at state-height at least the action's \(h_{\max}-1\).
Before the chosen TSQ freeze, Lemma~\ref{lem:tsq-no-active-reorg} and the
Goldfish persistence clause keep the available head in \(C\)'s subtree, although a
TSQ default or ordinary root may still be a compatible ancestor.  Timely
delivery then places responsive honest heads of weight at least~\(q\) in
\(C\)'s subtree in both the frozen and current views of the chosen action.
Freshness is required through the action, not merely through its earlier
freeze.  It fixes every view's \(h_{\max}\) at~\(H+2\), while delivery makes
the common \(C\)-descendant and ancestry accepted before selection.
Lemma~\ref{lem:simplex-root-compatible} makes each selection and action
Simplex root compatible with~\(C\).  If a root descends from~\(C\), every
stable root below it already descends from~\(C\); if it is an ancestor, the
accepted height-\(H+1\) descendant makes~\(C\) viable and places it in that
selection state's candidate tree.
Lemma~\ref{lem:descendant-stable-root} shows that every stable root from that
action onward descends from~\(C\); neither an ancestor nor a conflicting root
can reset the available-layer walk.  Hence the confirmation
established for~\(C\) persists for every later round in which the assumptions
hold.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Number of counted TSQ honest proposers]\label{rem:honest-proposers}
One counted TSQ honest-proposer voting round is sufficient for one height advance
when its due outcome is consumed, not necessarily for a justification
(Corollary~\ref{cor:honest-round-advance}).  Therefore two
counted TSQ honest-proposer rounds, the second beginning after the first transition
state is common, suffice for \(H\to H+1\to H+2\) if timely processing does not
continue from one round to the next.  They need not be consecutive wall-clock
rounds, but this gives no bound on the intervening rounds or on when such a
round can be constructed.
Those two voting rounds establish the height advances and common confirmed
prefix.  The stronger descendant-root invariant starts only at the separate
fresh TSQ action of Definition~\ref{def:fresh-recovery-action}; a profile may
need an additional scheduled phase, and must hold height-\(H+2\) FG signatures
until that action completes.

The round counts below count voting rounds.  In a one-slot-round profile, the
last counted round's result becomes state during the following slot's
settlement phase; this is not an additional voting round.

After the available chain is stable, a counted TSQ honest proposer at an ordinary
height with no previous honest votes produces a JC\@.  The following
finality-piggyback round does \emph{not} require an honest
first-slot proposer: it requires only that the common JC state be known before
the FG action and that the finality votes be included before the finality check
when that round's due outcome is consumed.  Starting from a walk at~\(d-1\), or first entering a
nonjustifiable height, adds the rounds counted in
Theorem~\ref{thm:finality-liveness}.  Thus two consecutive counted TSQ honest
proposers followed by one arbitrary proposer suffice only when the second
counted TSQ honest-proposer round is already at an ordinary height with no
previous honest votes.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[No pre-existing justification at \(H+2\) or above]\label{lem:no-preexisting-high-j}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:fixed-electorate},
\ref{ass:recovery-weights}, and~\ref{ass:goldfish}, let~\(C\) be the persistent safe-confirmed prefix
from Theorem~\ref{thm:available-healing}, beginning at an action fresh at
\(H+2\).  No justification available before that action can redirect the walk
from~\(C\)'s chain:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item Definition~\ref{def:simplex-root} cannot select a \(J\) of height at
    most~\(H-1\);
  \item a height-\(H\) justification is impossible;
  \item every height-\(H+1\) justification formed before or after the store became shared, and
    selected as a Simplex root during the interval, is compatible with~\(C\); and
  \item no justification at height~\(H+2\) or higher can exist before the
    fresh action.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
When \(\Sigma.h_{\max}=H+2\), Definition~\ref{def:simplex-root} can select a
justification only at height~\(H+1\), proving~(i).  Since~\(H\) is
nonjustifiable, a height-\(H\) justification is impossible, proving~(ii).  A
height-\(H+1\) justification quorum contains an honest target vote.
If the justification is selected as a Simplex root here, Assumption~\ref{ass:goldfish}
makes the confirmation that preceded that vote
compatible with~\(C\).
Target uniqueness excludes a conflicting certified target.  Any remaining
compatible height-\(H+1\) target lies on the same chain.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:chain-target-uniqueness}, it is the same \(T_{H+1}\); hence every
height-\(H+1\) justification names the same compatible target, proving~(iii).

Every quorum contains a responsive honest signer because the total weight
outside \(V_{\rm resp}\) is less than~\(q\).  Freshness says that no responsive
honest validator signed a target or timeout at any \(x\geq H+2\) before the
action.  No valid quorum justification at such a height can therefore predate
it.  This proves~(iv).
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Height bound after recovery]\label{def:height-bound-recovery}
After the available chain has stabilized, consider a counted TSQ honest-proposer
round whose proposal includes all blocks and still-valid honest votes received
before the proposal deadline.  Let~\(\Sigma\) be the shared store after processing
that proposal, its signed messages, and any due outcome consumed after its
operation phase,
but before the available-vote action.  Write
\[
 d=\Sigma.h_{\max}.
\]
Assume also that \(d\geq x\) for every height~\(x\) that an honest validator
reached or signed at before this store.  No height transition occurs between
this store
and the round's available and SG walks.  The height filter therefore puts the
common decision height in \(\{d-1,d\}\), and no honest validator has a target,
timeout, finality vote, or lock above~\(d\) from before this store.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[An ordinary height with no prior votes is justified]\label{lem:unused-ordinary-justify}
Let~\(h^\ast>d\) be a common ordinary height reached before any honest
validator has cast a target or timeout vote there.  Let~\(U=T_{h^\ast}\) be
its common current-height target.  If~\(U\) is confirmed before FG voting and
responsive honest votes of weight at least~\(q\) are included before the
round's due outcome is consumed, then~\(U\) is justified and the chain advances
to~\(h^\ast+1\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
No honest validator has an earlier height-\(h^\ast\) vote.  Every responsive
honest validator therefore votes for the same target~\(U\).  Their included weight
reaches~\(q\), so the state transition sets
\(\mathsf{JC}(h^\ast,U)\) before considering the simultaneously enabled timeout.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The next FG round finalizes the latest justification]\label{lem:later-finalization}
Suppose responsive honest validators of total weight at least~\(q\) signed the
target in \(\mathsf{JC}(h^\ast,U)\), and none of them timed out at~\(h^\ast\).  Before
their next FG action, suppose they have safe-confirmed a block whose state has
\((U,h^\ast)\) as the latest justified pair.  Suppose \((U,h^\ast)\) remains the
latest justified pair through collection of that action's finalize pairs; in
particular, no intervening state transition or branch change sets a newer
pair.  Every one of those validators then carries the finality commitment
\((h^\ast,U)\).  If those messages are included before the round's due outcome
is consumed, \(U\) finalizes there.  The round's first-slot proposer need not
be honest.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each quorum signer previously targeted~\(U\), never timed out there, and now
knows the justification.  Since it is still the latest justified pair at the
action, the signer satisfies Definition~\ref{def:finality-vote} and records its
lock on~\(U\), regardless of whether its new voted checkpoint is
target, timeout, or empty.  These commitments add signer weight at least~\(q\)
to~\(P\).  Since finality is checked first when the due outcome is consumed, it sets
\((F,h_F)=(U,h^\ast)\) before a simultaneous target quorum can set a newer
justification and reset~\(P\).  This is why the lemma requires \((U,h^\ast)\) to
remain the latest justification until all finalize pairs are collected.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finality after recovery]\label{thm:finality-liveness}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:fixed-electorate}, \ref{ass:recovery-weights}, \ref{ass:goldfish},
\ref{ass:honest-proposer}, and~\ref{ass:recovery-delivery}, assume the
available chain has stabilized through the fresh recovery action of
Theorem~\ref{thm:available-healing}.  Afterward,
let~\(d\) be as in Definition~\ref{def:height-bound-recovery}.  Consider five
consecutive rounds \(r_1,\ldots,r_5\).  In each round, responsive honest
validators of total weight at least~\(q\) complete the available, SG, and FG
steps, and their votes are included before that round's due outcome is
consumed.  Each of \(r_1,\ldots,r_5\) has a common target in the sense of
Definition~\ref{def:common-target}, confirmed before its FG action.  Whenever a round
advances the height, before the next round's SG and FG votes every responsive
honest validator has the same resulting state and has safe-confirmed a block
carrying that state.  In particular, if the advance forms a justification, it
is the latest justification in that state.  The first round is a counted
TSQ honest-proposer round and has a common proposal before the available-vote
action.  Later rounds need not have honest proposers.

Counting \(r_1\) as the first round, finalization occurs no later than when
\(r_5\)'s due outcome is consumed.  It occurs by consumption of \(r_4\)'s outcome if
height~\(d+1\) is ordinary, and by consumption of \(r_3\)'s outcome only when
the first common walk has height \(k=d\) and height~\(d+1\) is ordinary.

Without five consecutive rounds satisfying these conditions, finality still
resumes if a counted TSQ honest proposer later reaches a common ordinary
height~\(h^\ast>d\) before any honest validator votes there, its target~\(U\)
is confirmed before FG voting, and the target votes are included before that
round's due outcome.
Before the next FG
action, every responsive honest validator must safe-confirm a block carrying
the resulting justification.  The justification must remain latest until that
action's finalize pairs are collected, and the included finality votes must
have total weight at least~\(q\).  This
eventual result has no fixed round bound.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:no-preexisting-high-j} excludes every pre-existing justification that could
move the walk to a conflicting chain, and Lemma~\ref{lem:descendant-stable-root}
keeps later walks on the stabilized chain.  In the shared store, \(d\) is the
value of~\(h_{\max}\) and bounds all heights previously reached or
signed at by an honest validator.  The height filter and the counted TSQ honest proposal
therefore give a common first decision height \(k\in\{d-1,d\}\).
Lemmas~\ref{lem:honest-proposer-confirmation}
and~\ref{lem:aligned-height-advance} advance~\(k\).  If \(k=d-1\), the next
round's separately assumed common confirmed target and common transition state
satisfy Lemma~\ref{lem:aligned-height-advance} at~\(d\), so the common chain
enters height~\(d+1\) after at most two rounds.

No honest vote at height~\(d+1\) predates the shared store.  The assumptions on
the counted rounds give validators a common confirmed target before the first
FG vote at that height.
If the height is ordinary, Lemma~\ref{lem:unused-ordinary-justify} justifies its target.
If it is nonjustifiable, the timeout rule advances it.  Consecutive heights
cannot both be multiples of~\(K\geq2\), so the next height is
ordinary and Lemma~\ref{lem:unused-ordinary-justify} applies there.  The new
justification is carried by a common safe-confirmed block before the next FG
action.  Every
justification quorum contains honest weight, and~\(d\)
bounds all heights previously reached or signed at by honest validators.
Therefore no branch state with a higher justification can predate the shared
store.  The theorem assumes that the chosen justification remains latest while
the finalize pairs are collected, and the due-outcome rule checks finality
before it can install a newer justification, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:later-finalization} finalizes the latest justified target.

If \(k=d\), entry into~\(d+1\), its ordinary justification, and its finality
use rounds 1, 2, and 3; an intervening nonjustifiable~\(d+1\) adds one round.
If \(k=d-1\), the extra round gives the four- and five-round cases.  For a
later ordinary height~\(h^\ast>d\), the theorem's stated conditions satisfy
Lemmas~\ref{lem:unused-ordinary-justify}
and~\ref{lem:later-finalization}, which give eventual finality without a numerical
bound.
\end{proof}

The phrase ``later rounds need not have honest proposers'' is conditional on
the theorem's separate common-target and common-state premises in every one of
those rounds.  One counted TSQ honest proposer does not by itself establish
those premises for four later Byzantine-proposer rounds.  Without them, use
later counted TSQ honest proposers and the eventual, unbounded conclusion
instead.

\begin{theorem}[Healing]\label{thm:eventual-healing}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:fixed-electorate},
\ref{ass:recovery-weights}, \ref{ass:goldfish},
\ref{ass:honest-proposer}, and~\ref{ass:recovery-delivery}, suppose recovery reaches
a height~\(H\) that is nonjustifiable on every valid branch, including a
branch revealed later.  Suppose a shared store has
\(\Sigma.h_{\max}=H\), and a future counted TSQ honest-proposer round uses that
store.  Its common height is in \(\{H-1,H\}\).  If it is~\(H-1\),
suppose either the next round satisfies the timing conditions after
Definition~\ref{def:round-close}, or a later counted TSQ honest proposer uses the
resulting shared state.  Corollary~\ref{cor:height-catchup} then brings the
shared chain to~\(H\).  If the common height is already~\(H\), no preliminary round is
needed.  In either case Theorem~\ref{thm:available-healing} applies.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the common height advances through \(H\to H+1\to H+2\);
  \item let~\(\Sigma_2\) be the resulting first shared store with
    \(\Sigma_2.h_{\max}=H+2\).  If the SG votes for~\(C\) cross a TSQ freeze,
    remain usable at an action fresh at~\(H+2\), and, in the exceptional
    later-\(C\) branch, the source round's height-\(H+2\) FG checkpoint action
    is postponed until after that TSQ action, then from the
    corresponding TSQ action the available layer uses only stable roots and
    heads that equal or descend from~\(C\) during any later action in which the recovery
    assumptions hold.  Before that freeze, Goldfish still preserves the
    common confirmed prefix, but TSQ roots may be compatible ancestors; and
  \item under the conditions of item~2, if the conditions of
    Theorem~\ref{thm:finality-liveness} also hold, then the target at a later
    ordinary height with no previous honest votes is justified and finalized.
\end{enumerate}
The accountable-safety theorem does not use the synchrony or participation
assumptions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Corollary~\ref{cor:height-catchup} gives the first case of
Theorem~\ref{thm:available-healing} immediately or after one preliminary round,
unless a certificate has already given its second case.  That theorem first
proves the two advances.  Under item~2's fresh-action and timing conditions it
also proves stability of the available chain, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:no-preexisting-high-j} excludes any remaining hidden
justification that could affect fork choice.  Under item~3's additional
conditions, Theorem~\ref{thm:finality-liveness} gives a new justification and
finalizes it within its stated round bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Recurring healing]\label{cor:recurring-healing}
If every later finite disruption is followed by another uninterrupted window
satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:eventual-healing}, including
item~3's conditional premises, at a new height that is nonjustifiable on every
valid branch, then another block is finalized after each disruption.
Finitely many disruptions followed by one stable period are the special case
in which finality resumes.  Continued ordinary finality additionally uses the
usual delivery, participation, and inclusion assumptions, which are not proved
here.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:eventual-healing} after each finite disruption.  Each
newly finalized target has a strictly greater height than the preceding one,
so every application strictly advances~\(h_F\).
\end{proof}

\section{Incentives and empty votes}\label{sec:incentives}

The safety and healing theorems do not depend on rewards or the inactivity
leak.  The only point relevant to rewards is that an empty vote
sets no timeout bit and cannot exempt its signer from a stall penalty.
Otherwise all validators could vote empty, avoid the penalty, and prevent
height advance forever.

This paper does not prove a quantitative lower bound for the inactivity leak.
A deployment may pay
FG target and finality participation once per round or once per height.  The
design must specify eligible validators, rounding, fork-transition accounting,
and which validator balances and epoch are used to calculate rewards.  Paying
target/finality flags once per height avoids repeatedly rewarding the same
stalled checkpoint, but the practical gain is limited because the inactivity
leak quickly removes positive rewards during a stall.  The liveness proof is
independent of that choice.

\section{Implementation requirements}\label{sec:refinement}

SG and FG votes used by the proof occur after Goldfish confirmation.  A
concrete protocol may spread these duties across a round, but its schedule
must preserve the same order for every vote used in the proof.  The paper
fixes one validator set and weight function.  An executable profile with
validator churn must separately prove the required overlap between successive quorums
from its rate limits and weak-subjectivity rules; merely assigning one common
electorate to each height is not enough.  Checkpoint sync must authenticate
\texttt{current\_height\_target} and
\texttt{current\_height\_start\_slot} together with the current height and
finalized state; at a transition-block checkpoint it must also authenticate
the head used by the same-slot derivation.  Figure~\ref{alg:state-machine} enters a
new height after the inclusion-opportunity slot's block operations, or after
that slot's empty operation phase.  On the block path, the transition block is
the new target; its root is stored by the following slot transition, while
same-slot voters derive it from the authenticated head.  On the empty path,
the first later block becomes the target.  The one-slot grace lets final-slot
attestations satisfy their inclusion delay; it does not make closing a
block-supplied choice.  The outcome is derived from the slot schedule and
requires no \(\mathsf{closes\_round}(B)\) flag.  A valid vote that refers to an unknown block must be queued and replayed
when the block arrives.

TSQ uses only the fixed value~\(q\); ordinary \(\Gone\) and \(\Gzero\)
calculations continue to use their local live denominators.  Each TSQ instance
uses the public deadline-based support round defined in
Section~\ref{sec:tsq} and pins one
proposal-independent selection state~\(\Sigma^\star\), including its Simplex
root and prefix-closed viable candidate tree.  It later records the
post-proposal action state~\(\Sigma\).  Head messages project only to actual
ancestors in the selection-state tree.  A head can enter the frozen view only
when its named block and ancestry are already available.  A computed lock is
used only if it remains viable in the action state and descends from the
action-state Simplex root; otherwise the action uses ordinary \(\Gone\).

The implementation must retain the exact frozen view.  It leaves one delivery
bound after the freeze before candidate selection and another after candidate
selection before the lock action.  Honest nodes relay every valid
support-round message, named block, ancestry, and conflicting copy.  The
proposer selects the deepest current-view fixed-\(q\) candidate and publishes
a descendant.  Each receiver independently selects the deepest fixed-\(q\)
frozen/current lock, excluding signers with two eligible heads.  The lock, not a proposer field,
is the TSQ round's stable root.  The ordinary proposal is merely eligible
when it descends from that lock and is then evaluated by Goldfish.  The
completed freeze, action state, lock, and current input remain fixed for all
later operations belonging to that action.

The public synchronization-round schedule must recur independently of local
fork state.  A deployment that does not fit the freeze, proposal, action,
confirmation, SG, and FG intervals in one slot must spread them across slots.
For root-level persistence after recovery, responsive honest SG heads of
weight at least~\(q\) must cross a freeze and remain usable through an action
completed before responsive honest validators sign at the fresh height.
Putting only the freeze before that FG action is not sufficient; the delayed
case must postpone the FG checkpoint signature until the TSQ action completes.
Before that happens, the paper's below-one-half non-reorg statement relies on
ordinary Goldfish from a compatible ancestor; fixed-\(q\) TSQ alone supplies
no liveness with only an honest majority.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Fresh Simplex uses one fixed weighted validator set, E1 and E2, and one
target-participation bit and one timeout bit per validator.  In a counted TSQ
honest-proposer round, each validator freezes the support-round SG heads.  The
proposer later chooses the deepest viable fixed-\(q\) candidate and publishes
a descendant.  Each validator later chooses the deepest fixed-\(q\)
frozen/current lock as its stable root.  Time-shifted delivery proves that an
honest proposal extends every such lock, even when the locks have different
depths.  Goldfish therefore confirms the common proposal chain; SG and FG
then vote from that confirmed chain.  The proposal never becomes a stable
root by itself, signers with two eligible heads are excluded, and \(1/3\)
support remains only a \(\Gzero\) veto.

The recovery argument begins at a height~\(H\) that is nonjustifiable on every
valid branch, including a branch revealed later.  Advancing through
\(H\to H+1\to H+2\) establishes a common confirmed prefix.  If the confirming
SG heads cross a TSQ freeze and remain usable at an action completed before
any responsive honest height-\(H+2\) target or timeout signature, then every
later stable root descends from that prefix.  Receiving the transition state
does not violate this action barrier.  The delayed-\(C\) case postpones its
height-\(H+2\) FG checkpoint action until afterward.  The same freshness
excludes a pre-existing justification at height~\(H+2\) or above.
Finality additionally requires a later ordinary height with no previous honest
votes, timely inclusion of its target votes, and a following round in which
that justification remains latest until the finality votes are included.

The result assumes responsive honest validators of total weight at least~\(q\),
fixed weights during the
recovery interval, Byzantine weight below \(2q-W\), data and ancestry
availability, the stated external SG/AC composition-safety and Goldfish
persistence properties, a counted TSQ
honest proposer to make the chain and target common, and timely delivery,
freezing, and inclusion for the rounds being counted.  With only recurring
counted TSQ honest proposers the height still advances, but there is no fixed
round bound and finality needs a later ordinary height above all previous
honest votes before any honest validator votes there.
